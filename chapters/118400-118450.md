## 9 January 118,400 — The Martian satellite crisis

The Confederacy’s longest-lived operational satellite, **Ares Concordia**, abruptly stops transmitting.

The failure is initially treated as ordinary equipment loss. The spacecraft is already several years beyond its intended design life.

Three days later, ground-based optical stations observe that the satellite is not merely tumbling. Its orbit is changing faster than expected.

The Integrated Martian Orbital Environment Model predicts no such behaviour.

For the first time since the discovery of Ares Unitas a century earlier, Mars confronts evidence that its orbital science remains incomplete.

---

## January–April 118,400 — The missing effect

Investigators reconstruct the satellite’s final months.

They discover that the failure resulted from a combination of:

- electrostatic charging by dust and plasma;
- slow degradation of attitude-control surfaces;
- a weak interaction between the spacecraft’s conductive frame and Mars’s irregular magnetic environment;
- repeated local IRF fluctuations affecting the timing of control impulses;
- previously underestimated atmospheric drag at high altitude during global dust events.

The critical gap is not any one effect.

It is the way small effects align over decades.

Martian models had treated them as statistically independent. In reality, they reinforced one another.

Ares Concordia’s attitude system repeatedly corrected in the wrong phase, making later corrections increasingly severe until the satellite exhausted its control reserve.

---

## June 118,400 — The coupled-degradation model

Martian researchers develop the **Coupled Orbital Ageing Model**.

Unlike earlier models, it predicts how a spacecraft changes physically and operationally over time.

It tracks:

- material fatigue;
- surface charging;
- dust accumulation;
- control-response delay;
- IRF intensity;
- atmospheric variability;
- propellant decline;
- sensor drift.

Rome dedicates additional Wound computing capacity to validate the model.

The model immediately improves expected satellite lifespan estimates and reveals that several planned Martian spacecraft would have failed years earlier than believed.

Ares Concordia is deliberately deorbited on **17 November 118,400**.

Its final telemetry becomes a foundational engineering dataset.

---

# 118,401 — The Census of the Shield Age

On **23 March 118,401**, Lunar archivists announce the recovery of a partially preserved census compiled during the Shield Programme shortly before the Second Breaking.

The original dataset is incomplete, but surviving regional summaries are mutually consistent.

The estimated human population in **101,250** was approximately:

| Location | Population in 101,250 |
| --- | --- |
| Terra | ~11.2 billion |
| Luna | ~2.4 billion |
| Mars | ~3.1 billion |
| Asteroid Belt | ~620 million |
| Orbital habitats | ~570 million |
| Outer-system colonies | ~210 million |
| **Known total** | **~18.1 billion** |

The figure shocks every Solar society.

Historians had generally assumed that peak Industrial Age humanity numbered at least thirty or forty billion.

The surviving human population in the present Solar System is already greater than the total known population immediately before the Second Breaking.

---

## April–December 118,401 — Reinterpreting the Industrial Age

The census forces historians to revise several assumptions.

Industrial civilisation had been technologically immense but demographically more restrained than later Dark Age societies imagined.

The data suggests that:

- many orbital and offworld habitats were smaller than later legends claimed;
- advanced medicine did not lead to unlimited population growth;
- birth rates had already declined substantially in affluent Industrial societies;
- Terra remained the overwhelming demographic centre;
- Luna’s population was enormous but not remotely equal to Terra’s;
- Mars’s pre-Closing-Sky population was approximately twenty times its present one.

Roman demographers make an uncomfortable comparison.

The pre-Second-Breaking fertility decline was less severe than Rome’s panic-driven collapse surrounding the Second Resonance Wave.

The finding becomes further evidence that Roman authorities failed to manage public fear effectively.

---

## 118,402 — Mars adopts the Three-Billion Horizon

Martian media begins depicting a restored future population of approximately **3.1 billion**.

The number is initially historical symbolism rather than official policy.

It rapidly becomes part of the Martian Dream.

Murals, stories and public simulations show:

- extensive valley settlements;
- cities beneath transparent atmospheric structures;
- green surface belts;
- Martian families spread across the planet;
- Phobos and Deimos in inhabited skies.

The Confederacy formally calls 3.1 billion a **historical horizon**, not a demographic target.

Nevertheless, “returning to the billions” becomes another long-term expression of Martian ambition.

Critics warn that numerical restoration is meaningless without ecological capacity.

The government agrees officially, but the number retains enormous cultural power.

---

# 118,402–118,406 — The New Martian Industrial Initiative

The Confederacy concludes that its population boom and terraformation ambitions require a much larger industrial base.

It approves the **Surface Industrial Expansion Programme**.

Its goals are:

- produce habitat materials locally;
- expand food and water infrastructure;
- reduce dependence on ancient salvaged machinery;
- manufacture rocket components;
- create gases that thicken and warm the atmosphere;
- reactivate viable Industrial Age infrastructure;
- provide employment for the growing young population.

The programme deliberately concentrates new industry on the surface.

Facilities are easier to expand there, and their heat and gas emissions can contribute—slightly—to planetary warming.

---

## 118,403 — Martian fallback doctrine changes

Most new industrial plants are designed with:

- Class65 operating systems where locally viable;
- Class75 fallback systems whenever technically possible;
- mechanical shutdown;
- local isolation;
- distributed spare parts.

Unlike Roman industrial policy, Mars does not require Class100 fallback for most factories.

The Confederacy argues that industrial plants are not survival systems.

If they fail, habitats must continue operating independently.

Class100 capacity remains mandatory for a limited core producing:

- basic air-system components;
- water fittings;
- emergency food-processing equipment;
- simple medicine;
- pressure seals.

This creates a deliberate divide:

- Martian survival infrastructure remains extremely resilient.
- Martian expansion infrastructure accepts greater risk.

---

## 118,404–118,412 — Surface factories rise

The first industrial corridors are built near:

- old mining districts;
- restored rail routes;
- geothermal sites;
- historical Industrial foundations.

New facilities produce:

- glass;
- ceramics;
- steel;
- pressure vessels;
- insulation;
- fertiliser;
- synthetic greenhouse gases;
- rocket propellant;
- modular settlement frames.

The atmospheric effect is initially negligible.

Even massive emissions barely alter a planetary atmosphere.

However, local effects become measurable near industrial regions:

- slightly warmer ground;
- more persistent artificial haze;
- increased dust nucleation;
- changes in frost behaviour.

Confederal scientists estimate that industrial emissions alone would require many centuries to produce a clearly measurable global climatic change and millennia to create transformative effects.

Their immediate value is economic.

---

## 118,405 — Industrial professions become dominant

Martian universities experience an enrolment shift.

Popular fields include:

- industrial chemistry;
- surface construction;
- atmospheric processing;
- pressure engineering;
- materials science;
- low-energy automation;
- geothermal systems;
- rocket manufacturing.

Ecology remains central to education, but a new identity develops:

> Terraformers plan the future; industry builds it.

Some ecological factions fear pollution and reckless growth.

The Confederacy requires every industrial zone to maintain:

- atmospheric monitoring;
- waste recovery;
- surface-contamination limits;
- emergency shutdown;
- long-term remediation funds.

Enforcement varies between regions.

---

# 118,403 — Novaterra’s first agricultural alarm

On Novaterra, farmers in three states report a new disease affecting major grain and tuber crops.

The disease appears to combine fungal and bacterial characteristics.

It spreads through:

- seed stock;
- irrigation water;
- contaminated tools;
- insect carriers;
- airborne spores.

The first outbreaks seem manageable.

The affected governments restrict trade and destroy infected fields.

Within a year, genetically different crop lines begin displaying related symptoms.

The pathogen is not a single static organism.

It is a rapidly adapting disease complex.

---

## 118,404 — The diversity failure

Novaterran scientists discover that many apparently separate crop varieties share common ancestral genetic material.

Thousands of years of selective breeding had created visible diversity without sufficient underlying resistance diversity.

The planet’s limited original founder stock now becomes catastrophic.

The disease adapts to:

- major grains;
- root crops;
- several legumes;
- cultivated algae.

No one treatment works for long.

Novaterra possesses advanced biological knowledge, but full IRF conditions prevent:

- rapid genomic manufacturing;
- large-scale synthetic fertiliser substitution;
- high-output controlled-environment agriculture;
- fast global transport;
- automated quarantine.

Political fragmentation further obstructs response.

---

## 118,405 — The Concord returns

The Novaterran Concord, weakened for decades, suddenly becomes indispensable.

It establishes:

- a global disease registry;
- emergency seed exchange;
- quarantine corridors;
- crop-burning rules;
- protected breeding reserves;
- common rationing recommendations.

Several states temporarily restore powers they had withdrawn.

For a brief period, the catastrophe appears capable of rebuilding planetary cooperation.

The Concord’s experts recommend immediate destruction of nearly one-quarter of all cultivated food stock to interrupt transmission.

Governments refuse.

The short-term famine would be politically unbearable.

The disease continues spreading.

---

# 118,406 — The Roman Mars carrier programme slows again

Rome completes the first full-scale ground model of the Mars carrier’s navigation and thermal systems.

The test reveals that:

- the entry capsule remains too heavy;
- long-duration seals degrade;
- optical systems cannot remain perfectly aligned for the required period;
- the transition between Class50 and Class75 control produces timing errors;
- several Martian landing zones have unacceptable seasonal dust uncertainty.

The late-118,300s landing target is formally abandoned.

No replacement date is announced.

Internal planning moves the most credible first attempt into the **118,500s**, possibly later.

Roman public disappointment is limited because expectations had already adjusted.

Martian opinion remains supportive but less celebratory.

---

## 118,407 — Alternative achievement: the Interplanetary Navigation Grid

The Mars programme redirects part of its budget toward a chain of navigation references between Terra and Mars.

These include:

- Class100 beacons;
- faster Class75 relays;
- solar-weather sensors;
- optical reference stations;
- passive archive nodes.

The grid improves:

- deep-space navigation;
- asteroid tracking;
- Solar research;
- Martian communication;
- mission simulations.

It also gives future spacecraft multiple independent position references.

The project is slow but immediately useful.

---

# 118,407 — The Titan Cavern Survey begins

The University of Algarath establishes the **Titan Accessible Cavern Survey**.

Its purposes are to:

- map known accessible cavern systems;
- identify stable expansion zones;
- locate water and thermal reserves;
- document ecological capacity;
- estimate safe transport routes;
- identify dangerous geological interfaces;
- calculate Titan’s long-term sustainable population capacity.

Algarath cannot conduct the survey alone.

Travel between distant cavern systems may require years.

Some regions are physically inaccessible from Algarath.

The university therefore creates a distributed survey network.

Local teams collect information using standard Titanian methods and Common Titanian terminology.

---

## 118,408–118,420 — Mapping Titan slowly

Survey teams use:

- mechanical distance measurement;
- acoustic sounding;
- thermal-gradient mapping;
- pressure records;
- gravimetric instruments;
- physical exploration;
- old settlement archives.

Progress is limited by:

- full IRF conditions;
- slow transport;
- cavern collapses;
- political mistrust;
- incompatible historical maps;
- inaccessible voids;
- refusal by some hereditary technical houses to release private geological records.

The project quickly becomes larger than originally expected.

Several so-called separate cavern systems may actually connect through unstable or flooded regions.

Other presumed connections prove nonexistent.

---

# 118,409 — The refinery crew record is recovered

Luna’s recovery mission reaches the diverted central core of the ancient Industrial refinery.

The object contains several sealed archive cells.

Most are damaged.

One mechanical emergency recorder survives.

It identifies the refinery as **Helios Forge Twelve**.

Its crew numbered 18,430.

They had not originally captured the asteroid for ordinary mining.

They were responding to the Second Breaking.

---

## The final mission of Helios Forge Twelve

When the IRF returned, Helios Forge Twelve was operating in a mobile processing orbit between Terra and Luna.

Its crew detected a large asteroid whose trajectory had been altered by:

- failed Industrial propulsion equipment;
- gravitational interaction with refinery infrastructure;
- emergency movement of nearby orbital assets.

The asteroid was not naturally on course to strike Luna.

The failure of an automated Industrial Age mining tug placed it on that trajectory.

The tug’s engines became unstable under the returning IRF and continued firing intermittently.

The refinery crew had three options:

1. abandon the refinery and evacuate;
2. attempt to destroy the asteroid;
3. capture it and break its trajectory gradually.

Evacuation would have left too little time to divert the asteroid.

Destruction risked creating a high-velocity fragment cloud.

They chose capture.

---

## What happened to the crew

Helios Forge Twelve attached itself to the asteroid using mining anchors and redirected the refinery’s remaining mass drivers.

As the IRF intensified:

- autonomous control failed;
- cooling systems weakened;
- pressure regulation degraded;
- mass drivers became erratic;
- communication with Luna collapsed.

The crew converted the refinery to manual operation.

For eleven days, they fired processed asteroid material away from Luna to create a slow opposing thrust.

When the primary refinery structure began breaking apart, they separated the asteroid into controlled fragments.

The fragmentation was intentional.

Each section was given a slightly different trajectory so that most would miss Luna.

The operation succeeded.

The refinery itself lost structural integrity.

Escape craft could no longer launch safely.

Some crew survived for months in sealed compartments.

The final archive indicates that the last organised group deliberately maintained beacon and trajectory records so future observers could reconstruct the fragment stream.

They expected Luna to recover the data within years.

Instead, the record remained lost for more than seventeen millennia.

No crew member is believed to have survived.

---

## 118,410 — The Forge Memorial

Luna declares the Helios Forge crew protectors of the world.

A memorial is established in the recovered refinery core.

The object itself is moved gradually into a stable museum orbit.

Its surviving machinery becomes valuable to:

- asteroid-defence engineers;
- archaeological astronautics;
- the Mars carrier programme.

The crew’s manual mass-driver control methods inspire a new generation of low-energy deflection systems.

---

# 118,410 — Novaterra turns inward

Food production falls approximately 12% worldwide.

States impose:

- grain export bans;
- border inspections;
- seed confiscation;
- migration limits;
- military control of food stores.

The Concord warns that protectionism will worsen shortages by trapping surplus in some regions and starving others.

Governments refuse to trust one another.

Black markets expand.

Food convoys require armed protection.

Several smaller states begin raiding neighbouring storage centres.

---

## 118,411 — The First Grain Wars

Two Novaterran states go to war over a disputed river granary system.

The conflict spreads when allies intervene.

Because armies operate under full IRF conditions, warfare resembles the most organised conflicts of the Long Age:

- rail mobilisation;
- infantry;
- cavalry or animal transport in some regions;
- low-power radio;
- mechanical artillery;
- siege warfare;
- river control.

The wars are slower than Industrial Age conflicts but devastating to agriculture.

Fields are burned.

Irrigation systems are destroyed.

Labour is diverted into armies.

Food decline accelerates.

---

# 118,412 — Terra’s final accession negotiations

Only Orassia and one small coastal republic remain outside Rome.

The coastal republic has already accepted most Roman institutions.

Its referendum produces 81% support for accession.

It joins on **3 September 118,412**.

Orassia remains independent.

Its citizens value:

- technological autonomy;
- rapid conditional innovation;
- separate constitutional identity;
- prestige as Terra’s last sovereign non-Roman state.

The political distinction is now immense symbolically and small materially.

---

# 118,413 — Haven expands AI governance

The Haven Commonwealth assigns AI systems greater responsibility for:

- logistics;
- mining;
- settlement construction;
- ecological modelling;
- atmospheric processing;
- medical distribution;
- infrastructure inspection.

Human institutions retain final political authority.

However, many ordinary decisions are now generated and implemented automatically.

Haven establishes a rule that no AI may optimise only one output.

Every major system must track:

- present production;
- future capacity;
- ecological cost;
- maintenance debt;
- human safety;
- political constraints.

The rule develops from the failed mining swarm’s tendency to defer maintenance.

---

## 118,414 — AI-designed settlement districts

Haven opens its first city district designed almost entirely through artificial intelligence.

The system optimises:

- sunlight;
- transport;
- heat recovery;
- air circulation;
- maintenance access;
- population density;
- ecological isolation.

Construction robots assemble the district rapidly.

Residents report excellent functionality and a strange absence of cultural character.

Later projects require human artists, historians and residents to participate in design.

Haven learns that efficient space is not automatically meaningful space.

---

# 118,414 — The Novaterran seed panic

The pathogen reaches several protected seed reserves.

Some infections result from contamination.

Others are deliberate.

States accuse rivals of biological sabotage.

Evidence is inconclusive.

A few military governments begin destroying foreign crop lines, fearing hidden infection.

This further reduces genetic diversity.

The Concord tries to move uncontaminated material into internationally guarded vaults.

Armed units seize several vaults before transfer.

Trust collapses.

---

## 118,415 — Concord emergency authority

The Concord declares a planetary food emergency and attempts to impose:

- mandatory crop destruction;
- food redistribution;
- prohibition on attacks against agricultural infrastructure;
- protection of breeding laboratories;
- common quarantine.

Several states comply.

Major military governments refuse.

The Concord has no independent armed force.

Its authority exists only where governments accept it.

Its influence peaks precisely as its practical power reaches its limit.

---

# 118,416 — The Martian industrial accident at Eos

A newly reactivated Industrial Age chemical complex begins producing atmospheric fluorine compounds intended for controlled greenhouse warming.

Ancient pressure vessels pass inspection.

An undocumented buried pipeline does not.

The line ruptures and releases toxic gas into a maintenance district.

More than 8,000 people die.

The accident causes a planetary debate over reactivating old infrastructure.

The Confederacy orders:

- complete subsurface mapping;
- no reuse of undocumented lines;
- modern isolation barriers;
- mandatory Class100 emergency breathing systems near hazardous plants.

Industrial expansion slows temporarily.

It does not stop.

---

## 118,417 — The Martian Industrial Charter

The Confederacy adopts a planetary industrial law.

Every major facility must demonstrate:

- economic usefulness;
- settlement-support value;
- atmospheric effect;
- waste recovery;
- failure isolation;
- long-term upgrade path;
- emergency shutdown independent of normal control systems.

The law explicitly accepts that most industry will not remain operational at full IRF.

It requires that its failure not endanger nearby survival systems.

Mars defines industrialisation as an instrument of the Martian Dream, not an end in itself.

---

# 118,418 — The Orassian question

Orassia experiences growing emigration.

Its young adults can move freely into Rome, where they gain access to:

- larger markets;
- civic income;
- universities;
- Wound computation;
- Lunar opportunities.

Orassian leaders fear demographic hollowing.

They propose a special association preserving sovereignty while granting near-complete Roman market integration.

Rome agrees temporarily.

World-union supporters demand accession instead.

The Orassian government announces a final sovereignty referendum for 118,423.

---

# 118,419 — Novaterra’s first continental famine

Food production in one continental region falls by nearly 40%.

Urban populations begin leaving cities.

Governments establish military rationing.

Refugees move toward:

- river valleys;
- surviving grain regions;
- forests;
- less-affected states.

Border forces fire on refugee columns.

Disease spreads through camps.

Deaths rise from:

- starvation;
- exposure;
- ordinary infection;
- violence;
- untreated chronic disease.

The food pathogen itself does not directly kill humans.

It destroys the systems that keep them alive.

---

## 118,420 — The Concord’s last successful operation

The Concord coordinates evacuation of a threatened agricultural basin and transfers a rare disease-resistant crop strain to twelve states.

For several months, the strain appears promising.

Then local variants of the pathogen adapt to it.

The symbolic blow is severe.

The Concord had presented the strain as proof that common action could prevail.

Instead, its failure convinces many governments that no planetary solution exists.

---

# 118,421 — Titan’s first survey synthesis

The University of Algarath publishes an interim map covering approximately one-third of known inhabited Titanian cavern space.

The results show:

- greater geological connectivity than expected in some regions;
- less safe expansion room near major metacaverns;
- large unexplored thermal zones;
- several possible water-rich systems;
- serious collapse risk beneath densely inhabited districts.

Orenth-Marellon delays one expansion project based on the findings.

Algarath’s reputation increases further.

Some cavern governments still refuse participation, fearing that common maps could have military or economic value.

---

# 118,422 — Mars pauses the Phobos programme

The Confederacy formally acknowledges that no successful Phobos mission is possible within current budget and reliability constraints.

The mission remains a constitutional long-term objective.

Immediate funding is redirected to:

- orbital environment science;
- communications;
- high-altitude return vehicles;
- industrial propulsion research;
- Mars-lander support.

The decision is unpopular among space enthusiasts but broadly accepted.

Industrialisation and terraformation remain higher priorities.

---

# 18 May 118,423 — Orassia votes

The referendum asks whether Orassia should:

- remain sovereign under association;
- enter Rome as provinces within the World of Terra.

The result is close:

- 54.8% support accession
- 45.2% support independence

Turnout reaches 92%.

The Orassian government accepts the result.

Rome approves accession after a five-month constitutional transition.

---

# 1 January 118,424 — Terra is united

Orassia formally enters Rome.

For the first time since the Second Breaking, every sovereign territory on Terra belongs to one constitutional state.

The **World of Roman Terra** is renamed officially the **World of Terra**.

Obsolete institutions are dissolved or transformed.

These include:

- international Terran diplomatic offices;
- associated-state councils;
- foreign-aid authorities restricted to Terra;
- border and customs administrations between Terran states;
- Roman ministries for relations with non-Roman Terran governments.

Their functions transfer to:

- Terran regional administration;
- interworld relations;
- provincial equalisation;
- planetary environmental governance;
- Roman constitutional institutions.

---

## The duration of division

At the moment of unification:

- 17,045 years have passed since the Second Breaking in 101,379.
- 2,682 years have passed since the restored Roman Republic was founded in 115,742.
- Approximately 119,177 years have passed since the traditional founding of the ancient city of Rome in 753 BC.

Terra’s political division lasted longer than the entire Industrial Age and most remembered states.

Its reunification occurs without a final war.

It is achieved through:

- accession;
- demographic movement;
- shared infrastructure;
- language;
- economic dependence;
- constitutional adaptation;
- the exhaustion of viable alternatives.

---

# 118,424 — The Festival of Four Ages

The city of Rome hosts a year-long celebration.

The principal ceremony takes place on **21 April**, the traditional anniversary of the ancient city’s foundation.

Representatives attend from:

- every Terran province;
- Luna;
- Mars;
- Europa;
- Titan;
- Concordia Orbitalis;
- the restored Lunar habitats.

The celebration honours the four great ages.

### The First Age

Represented by stone, civic law and the memory of ancient Rome.

### The Long Age

Represented by craftsmanship, stewardship and durable low-energy civilisation.

### The Industrial Age

Represented by recovered electronics, spacecraft fragments and the first expansion beyond Terra.

### The Dark Age

Represented by Class100 machinery, the reunited Republic and the survival of separated human worlds.

---

# 118,424–118,428 — The Monument of Human Continuity

A permanent monument is constructed in Rome.

It consists of four interlocking structures:

- a stone civic arch for the First Age;
- an ornate mechanical and hydraulic gallery for the Long Age;
- a metal and glass tower representing the Industrial Age;
- a deep reinforced foundation representing the Dark Age.

At the centre stands an open circular space symbolising the unknown age after the IRF disappears.

The monument is designed to survive:

- earthquake;
- flood;
- fire;
- abandonment;
- total loss of electricity;
- many millennia without maintenance.

Its inscriptions use Latin and mathematical diagrams.

---

## The sealed archive beneath the monument

A chamber beneath the structure contains:

- the complete Roman constitution;
- the accession instruments of every Terran state;
- a concise history of the Republic;
- maps of Terra and Luna;
- the dates of the city, Republic and reunification;
- records of the four ages;
- basic Latin references;
- summaries of Roman citizenship and law.

Storage media include:

- engraved stone;
- corrosion-resistant metal plates;
- ceramic tablets;
- microphotographic film;
- optical media;
- magnetic archives.

The chamber is sealed against water, pressure and biological decay.

It is not meant for ordinary public access.

It exists so that even if Rome falls, someone in a distant future may learn that Terra was united peacefully and know the name of the civilisation that achieved it.

---

# 118,425 — The cost of unity

Unification does not immediately erase regional difference.

Orassia’s conditional-technology sector now falls under Roman regulation.

Compromises allow:

- experimental zones;
- faster approvals;
- conditional systems with layered fallback;
- continued local scientific institutions.

Some Orassian firms leave Terra or relocate to the Wound.

Others become major Roman innovators.

Former sovereignty supporters continue peaceful political activity.

Rome treats opposition to accession as legitimate opinion rather than disloyalty.

---

# 118,425 — Novaterra’s Concord dissolves

On **6 October 118,425**, the Novaterran Concord holds its final assembly.

Delegates from fewer than half the recognised states attend.

The Concord admits it cannot:

- enforce quarantine;
- protect seed reserves;
- stop wars;
- redistribute food;
- maintain communications;
- guarantee safe travel.

Its final declaration calls for local preservation of:

- agricultural knowledge;
- ecological archives;
- seed diversity;
- disease records.

The institution formally dissolves.

Several regional bodies continue using parts of its archive and law.

The failure removes the last planetary coordination mechanism.

---

## 118,426–118,433 — Novaterra fragments

The collapse accelerates.

Events include:

- military coups;
- dynastic emergency governments;
- food seizures;
- urban uprisings;
- refugee wars;
- secession of agricultural provinces;
- collapse of currencies;
- destruction of rail lines;
- raids on seed banks.

Many states cease functioning outside their capitals.

Others survive only as military-controlled food regions.

Some governments intentionally abandon distant populations to preserve core territories.

Authoritarianism becomes common because rulers argue that rationing and movement control require absolute power.

In many places, authoritarian rule fails anyway.

---

# 118,427 — The War of the Southern Granaries

Five Novaterran states fight over one of the last large relatively healthy grain regions.

The war lasts four years.

The granaries change hands repeatedly.

Fields are overharvested.

Irrigation canals are sabotaged.

Refugees consume emergency reserves.

By the time one faction gains control, the region can feed fewer than half the people who fought over it.

The war becomes the clearest example of scarcity converting valuable land into destroyed land.

---

# 118,428 — Nomad corridors emerge

Large populations abandon fixed settlements.

Nomadic groups move between:

- surviving seasonal farms;
- forests;
- river fisheries;
- abandoned towns;
- state borders that no longer function.

Some groups are organised cooperatives.

Others become armed raiders.

Many combine both roles depending on circumstances.

Trade shifts toward:

- seed;
- animals;
- tools;
- medicine;
- information about safe routes.

Former urban professionals adapt to subsistence life.

Literacy and institutional knowledge decline unevenly.

---

# 118,429 — Martian industrial output doubles

Mars’s surface industrial programme reaches a major threshold.

Compared with 118,400, production of:

- pressure structures;
- glass;
- fertiliser;
- metal components;
- greenhouse panels;
- rail equipment

has roughly doubled.

Living standards improve.

Settlements gain:

- larger homes;
- more reliable heat;
- better schools;
- more varied food;
- shorter maintenance shifts.

Industrial emissions remain far too small to transform the atmosphere globally.

They begin producing consistent measurable warming in selected industrial basins.

Martian public belief in terraformation strengthens.

---

## 118,430 — Mars encounters industrial inequality

Regions with factories prosper faster.

Settlements without:

- minerals;
- transport;
- geothermal access;
- old infrastructure

fall behind.

The Confederacy creates a national industrial equalisation fund.

A share of industrial output finances:

- remote settlements;
- new railways;
- education;
- water projects;
- settlement restoration.

Martians debate whether industrialisation should be centrally planned or locally competitive.

The government adopts a mixed model.

---

# 118,431 — The first Mars Plaque Capsule prototype

Rome completes a full-scale prototype of the future Martian entry capsule.

It is tested in a high-altitude Terran environment and later in a Lunar vacuum facility.

The capsule successfully transitions between:

- Class50 guidance;
- Class75 fallback;
- passive optical control.

It fails its simulated Martian landing.

The parachute system deploys too late after an artificial dust-density change.

The failure delays the mission further but validates most of the carrier architecture.

Mars provides revised atmospheric data.

---

# 118,432 — Titan survey disaster

A survey expedition in a distant cavern region enters a thermal corridor thought to be stable.

A hidden ice layer collapses after being warmed by equipment and human presence.

Seventy-six explorers die.

The University of Algarath suspends direct exploration in poorly characterised regions.

More of the survey shifts toward:

- local teams;
- remote mechanical instruments;
- archival reconstruction;
- acoustic sensing.

The project slows further.

The deaths lead to Titan-wide exploration safety standards.

---

# 118,433 — The Solar population comparison

Roman historians publish a comprehensive comparison.

Known Solar humanity now numbers approximately:

- Terra: ~20 billion;
- Luna: ~1.8 billion;
- Mars: ~180 million;
- Titan: ~240 million;
- Europa: ~6 million;
- orbital settlements and stations: several million.

The total exceeds the known **18.1 billion** of 101,250 even without counting Novaterra or Haven.

The finding changes Solar self-perception.

The Dark Age is no longer viewed only as an era of demographic collapse.

Humanity has numerically surpassed the Industrial Age while operating under severe physical limits.

Yet Novaterra’s catastrophe demonstrates that population alone is not proof of civilisational strength.

---

# 118,434 — Novaterra’s disease reaches the ecological base

The pathogen begins affecting wild descendants of cultivated plants.

This is the turning point.

Until then, governments hoped to replace agriculture with collection from semi-wild ecosystems.

Now grasslands, managed forests and wetland food systems also decline.

Because Novaterra’s biosphere originated from limited Terran stock, agricultural and wild systems share vulnerabilities.

The food crisis becomes an ecosystem crisis.

Pollinator populations crash in several regions.

Soil fertility declines as associated microorganisms disappear.

Recovery becomes far more difficult than simply planting new crops.

---

# 118,435 — Population collapse accelerates

Novaterra’s population falls below one billion.

Most deaths result from:

- starvation;
- epidemic disease among weakened populations;
- war;
- exposure;
- collapse of sanitation;
- loss of medicine.

Some older ecological states preserve stable cores through:

- severe quarantine;
- population evacuation;
- burning infected regions;
- strict rationing;
- controlled seed vaults.

They survive at the cost of abandoning outsiders.

Moral legitimacy becomes less important than continued existence.

---

# 118,436 — The Roman Response to Novaterra

Sol remains unaware of Novaterra’s current catastrophe because no practical communication exists.

The tragedy unfolds entirely beyond Solar knowledge.

On Terra, attention remains focused on unification and the Mars programme.

The contrast is stark but invisible to both civilisations.

---

# 118,436 — Haven’s AI ecological planners

Haven extends AI into ecological design.

Artificial intelligences now coordinate:

- microbial succession;
- soil treatment;
- water chemistry;
- species introduction;
- genetic diversity;
- atmospheric modification.

The systems identify that earlier Haven projects attempted to optimise for rapid plant coverage rather than stable ecological depth.

New projects prioritise:

- diverse microbial foundations;
- slow soil formation;
- redundant food webs;
- multiple competing species;
- local failure containment.

Greening slows in visible area but improves in durability.

---

# 118,437 — Terra’s first unified planetary election

For the first time, every adult Terran Roman citizen participates in the same World of Terra election.

The election concerns:

- regional equalisation;
- Terra Restituta;
- Class50 regulation;
- support for Mars missions;
- management of former Orassian innovation zones.

Turnout is exceptionally high.

The victorious coalition combines:

- moderate world unionists;
- social equalisation parties;
- continuity conservatives;
- regulated innovation advocates.

No one-party triumph occurs.

Terra’s political unity remains democratic and plural.

---

# 118,438 — The Mars programme’s archive windfall

A sealed archive recovered from the Helios Forge refinery includes designs for:

- long-duration mass-driver mechanisms;
- vacuum-stable bearings;
- autonomous material repair;
- deep-space thermal control.

The designs were intended for asteroid processing, not planetary missions.

Roman engineers adapt them for:

- carrier orientation;
- late-stage trajectory correction;
- relay deployment;
- long-term component preservation.

The refinery crew’s last mission contributes directly to the effort to reconnect Terra and Mars.

---

# 118,439 — Novaterra’s state count loses meaning

Only twelve recognised governments still control most of their claimed territory.

Dozens of successor regimes exist.

Some rule:

- one city;
- one valley;
- one grain basin;
- a railway corridor;
- a moving army.

Large regions have no government.

Maps become outdated within months.

The distinction between state, militia, refugee organisation and raider confederation becomes unclear.

---

# 118,440 — The surviving Novaterran food zones

Several regions remain functional.

Their common characteristics include:

- geographical isolation;
- unusual crop genetics;
- strict quarantine;
- strong local institutions;
- lower population density;
- preserved ecological complexity.

One mountain federation maintains a stable food system using crops neglected by the pre-collapse global economy.

Another survives through mixed fishing, forestry and small-scale agriculture.

These regions become potential centres of eventual recovery.

They are too weak to stabilise the planet.

---

# 118,441 — Titan’s survey reaches the outer systems

The Accessible Cavern Survey begins receiving standardised data from remote settlements that had previously refused participation.

The Novaterran catastrophe, though unknown on Titan, is not the cause.

Titanian motivation comes from its own concern over population growth.

Preliminary findings indicate that many major caverns are approaching locally sustainable limits.

The survey’s political importance increases.

Governments want to know whether expansion should focus on:

- new caverns;
- denser existing settlements;
- deeper integration;
- eventual surface technology.

---

# 118,442 — Mars limits industrial emissions locally

Several industrial basins experience:

- toxic frost;
- chemical accumulation;
- damage to microbial experiments;
- respiratory-system contamination in surface workers.

The Confederacy caps some emissions and requires atmospheric processing before release.

This creates a contradiction.

Mars wants greenhouse gases.

It does not want uncontrolled local pollution.

Industrial policy shifts toward carefully selected gases with low biological toxicity and long atmospheric persistence.

The terraforming effect remains slow.

---

# 118,443 — Luna receives the Helios Forge core

The recovered refinery core reaches a stable Lunar museum orbit after years of gradual manoeuvring.

A small permanent preservation station is attached.

The core contains:

- crew memorials;
- surviving mass-driver components;
- emergency archives;
- asteroid-fragment maps.

The station also becomes part of the Lunar Defence Belt.

The object that once threatened Luna now helps protect it.

---

# 118,444 — The first unified Terran planetary budget

The World of Terra adopts its first budget without any category for international Terran affairs.

Former diplomatic spending is redirected toward:

- provincial equalisation;
- integrated rail;
- environmental management;
- public health;
- unified asteroid defence;
- cultural preservation in recently acceded regions.

The symbolic change is important.

No part of Terra is now foreign to another part under Roman law.

---

# 118,445 — Novaterra’s population falls below half a billion

The collapse has reduced Novaterra to less than one-fifth of its 118,400 population.

Many surviving people live outside stable states.

Estimated distribution:

- roughly 180 million in functioning states or protected zones;
- around 140 million in local city-states, militias or successor governments;
- more than 100 million in nomadic, refugee or ungoverned regions.

Mortality remains extremely high.

Birth rates are low because of malnutrition, insecurity and family separation.

The disease continues evolving.

No universal cure exists.

---

# 118,446 — The Roman Mars photographic approach test

A Roman imaging spacecraft travels farther from Terra than any modern Roman craft designed for later reuse.

It does not approach Mars closely, but it tests:

- long-term autonomous navigation;
- deep-space image transmission;
- relay handoff;
- thermal stability;
- field-gradient transition.

The mission succeeds.

It sends photographs of Mars as a bright disk against the Solar background.

The images are received on Mars months later.

This becomes the century’s most visible success for the Mars Approach Programme.

---

# 118,447 — Martian public mood changes

Martians remain supportive of physical contact.

The project is no longer expected within ordinary lifetimes.

It becomes another multi-generational programme beside terraformation.

Public support now depends on continued practical benefits:

- communication;
- weather science;
- orbital models;
- industrial technology;
- education.

The Confederacy makes future Mars-approach spending conditional on meeting annual housing, food and terraformation targets.

The project survives by accepting that it is not Mars’s first priority.

---

# 118,448 — Haven’s automated construction surge

AI-directed mining and construction allow Haven to build:

- orbital yards;
- sealed ecological cities;
- atmosphere-processing towers;
- genetic archives;
- automated farms;
- new probe facilities.

Population growth remains controlled.

The Commonwealth deliberately avoids the demographic pressures that harmed the original ship.

Automation performs most dangerous physical labour.

Human employment shifts toward:

- governance;
- research;
- ecological design;
- art;
- medicine;
- AI oversight.

---

# 118,449 — Novaterra’s regional dark age

Several surviving governments stop maintaining national records.

Long-distance communication collapses.

Local calendars, currencies and legal systems diverge.

Some literate communities preserve former national archives.

Others destroy them as symbols of failed governments.

Raider groups seize mechanical rail routes and charge passage.

Nomadic societies establish seasonal conventions to avoid exhausting grazing and wild-food regions.

New forms of social organisation emerge from catastrophe.

Novaterra survives, but no longer as a connected planetary civilisation.

---

# 118,450 — End of the half-century

- Change since 118,400: +3.5%
- Sovereign states: 1
- IRF: ~46.6%

- Living standards: very high, with substantial regional equality
- Major development: complete political reunification
- Main project: integration of former Orassia, Mars Approach Programme, Terra Restituta

- Major development: Accessible Cavern Survey

## Novaterra

With no IRF:

# Major project status in 118,450

## Terra’s unification

Complete.

The World of Terra now encompasses every Terran province.

## Monument of Human Continuity

Completed and sealed with its archive beneath Rome.

## Mars Approach Programme

Delayed, but producing communications, imaging, navigation and archival benefits.

## Martian industrialisation

Expanding rapidly, with modest atmospheric effects and major economic effects.

## Helios Forge Twelve

Recovered.

Its crew’s sacrifice and the cause of the debris stream are finally understood.

## Titan Accessible Cavern Survey

Still in its early phase and expected to require many more decades.

## Haven AI development

Rapidly expanding into mining, construction and ecological management.

## Novaterra

The old planetary order has collapsed.

Survival continues through isolated states, local communities, nomadic groups and armed successor regimes.

Recovery has not begun in any meaningful planetary sense.

---

The half-century closes with two opposite images.

In Rome, a monument is sealed beneath a united world so that future civilisations will remember that humanity once overcame division.

On Novaterra, people burn abandoned archives for warmth while old borders disappear beneath hunger, migration and war.

Both worlds possess enormous inherited knowledge.

Only one still possesses the institutions capable of using it collectively.

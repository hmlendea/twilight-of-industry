# 110000 — A New Equilibrium

Approximately **7,500 years after full Solar System saturation** and more than **8,600 years after the Second Breaking**.

Compared with the last checkpoint, humanity has not merely continued declining.

It has settled into a new equilibrium.

The great age of spacecraft, artificial intelligence and planetary engineering is no longer a recent loss. It has become deep history.

The surviving human societies are now separated by distances they cannot cross and, in several cases, no longer even know with certainty whether the others still exist.

---

## 1. Humanity as a whole

The human species survives.

But there is no longer one human civilisation.

There are several isolated branches, each shaped by its environment:

- Terra remains the largest and most secure population.
- Mars survives in scattered enclosed settlements.
- Titan sustains a stable underground civilisation.
- Europa probably still supports small aquatic communities.
- At least several extrasolar human populations survive on naturally habitable planets.
- Most artificial habitats, moons and asteroid settlements are extinct.

The total human population is impossible to calculate because interplanetary communication has been absent for millennia.

The best estimate made by Terran scholars is:

| Human branch | Estimated population |
| --- | --- |
| Terra | 1.4–1.9 billion |
| Mars | 1–4 million |
| Titan | 8–20 million |
| Europa | Unknown; perhaps 100,000–1 million |
| Extrasolar worlds | Completely unknown |
| Total known or inferred | Roughly 1.5–2 billion |

Humanity's total population is therefore not dramatically larger than it was during the ancient pre-industrial plateau.

The difference is that it is now divided across multiple worlds.

---

## 2. Terra

Terra is once again the demographic and cultural centre of humanity.

Not because it conquered the other worlds.

Because it is the only surviving human world whose environment supports civilisation without continuous artificial intervention.

## Population

Terra's population has stabilised at approximately **1.6 billion**.

It fluctuates over centuries according to:

- harvest quality
- disease
- climate cycles
- regional wars
- ecosystem productivity
- migration
- institutional stability

Population density is much lower than during the industrial era.

Large regions remain sparsely inhabited.

---

## 3. Terra's environment

The planet has recovered extensively from the second impact, but it has not returned to its pre-impact condition.

Eight millennia of ecological succession have produced a new planetary biosphere.

## Forests

Forests cover much of the land once occupied by cities and intensive agriculture.

Some are descendants of surviving natural ecosystems.

Others originated from refuge seed banks and deliberate replanting.

Many are entirely new combinations of species.

Trees from formerly separate continents now grow together.

Ancient urban foundations lie beneath:

- temperate woodland
- wetlands
- grasslands
- coastal forest
- desert scrub

## Oceans

The oceans have recovered biologically, though many ancient species remain extinct.

New marine ecosystems dominate.

Several recreated species survive only because humans repeatedly reintroduced them over thousands of years.

Coral-like reef systems exist again, but many are built by organisms different from those that dominated before the impact.

## Climate

The impact winter is long over.

The climate is broadly stable, though altered coastlines and atmospheric chemistry have changed regional weather patterns.

The Pacific impact basin remains one of Terra's defining geographical features.

Its margins contain:

- volcanic island chains
- mineral-rich waters
- unstable crust
- restricted zones where unusual IRF effects remain strongest

## Ruins

Industrial ruins still exist, but most surface structures have collapsed.

The most durable remnants include:

- deep foundations
- tunnels
- underground archives
- orbital-launch complexes
- buried reactor chambers
- ceramic and stone monuments
- impact shelters

Many ruins are now archaeological sites rather than recognisable cities.

---

## 4. Terran technology

Terran technology has reached a mature low-energy plateau.

It is more sophisticated than the civilisation before the First Breaking in some respects because it inherits millennia of accumulated knowledge.

Yet it remains physically constrained by the IRF.

Common technologies include:

- highly efficient water wheels
- wind turbines
- local electric generators
- mechanical pumps
- low-pressure steam systems
- precision clocks
- optical telegraphs
- radio networks
- electromechanical computers
- mechanical printing
- advanced lenses
- durable chemical batteries
- low-energy medical instruments
- sailing ships with sophisticated navigation
- animal-powered agricultural machinery

Uncommon but possible technologies include:

- limited photography
- simple vacuum devices
- specialised local electric vehicles
- mechanically guided machines
- low-powered broadcasting
- complex relay computers

Impossible at scale remain:

- semiconductor fabrication
- industrial aviation
- high-speed rail
- nuclear power
- fusion
- mass-produced pharmaceuticals
- global electrical grids
- high-pressure industry
- spaceflight

---

## 5. Terran transport

Most long-distance movement occurs through:

- sailing vessels
- canals
- river craft
- animal-drawn vehicles
- low-speed electric trams
- mechanical railways in wealthy regions
- walking

Railways exist, but they are not industrial-age railways.

They use:

- low-pressure engines
- cable systems
- gravity-assisted lines
- local electric motors
- animal traction

Journeys between continents take weeks or months.

This reinforces regional cultural independence.

---

## 6. Terran political organisation

There is no global state.

Terra contains thousands of political entities.

The most common forms are:

- river-basin federations
- city republics
- hereditary kingdoms
- monastic archive states
- agricultural communes
- merchant leagues
- constitutional principalities
- refuge-descended technocracies
- nomadic confederations

A few large federations contain tens of millions of people.

Most political units are much smaller.

Global conquest is nearly impossible because transport and communication are too limited.

Wars occur, but they are generally regional.

No state can project power over the entire planet.

---

## 7. The Archive Tradition

The most important Terran institution is not a government.

It is the **Archive Tradition**.

Thousands of libraries descend from the emergency archives created before the Second Breaking.

Their mission is to preserve:

- scientific knowledge
- planetary history
- engineering designs
- languages
- medical understanding
- ecological records
- accounts of the spacefaring era

The largest archives maintain multiple versions of every critical work:

- printed books
- etched metal
- ceramic plates
- optical microtext
- memorised oral recitation

Archive communities exchange copies whenever possible.

They know that the greatest threat is not ignorance, but gradual corruption of knowledge.

---

## 8. How Terrans view the industrial era

To people in year 110,000, the industrial and spacefaring age is approximately as distant as the earliest cities are to us.

It lasted only a tiny fraction of recorded history.

Many Terrans struggle emotionally to comprehend it.

They know that their ancestors once:

- travelled between planets
- built conscious machines
- altered genomes
- lived for more than a century
- communicated instantly across Terra
- inhabited artificial worlds

But these facts feel almost mythological.

The difference is that physical evidence and technical records still exist.

The space age is not considered a legend.

It is considered an unreachable historical reality.

---

## 9. Religion and philosophy on Terra

The Closing Sky dominates Terran philosophy.

Several major traditions interpret it differently.

## The Steward Tradition

It teaches that human survival depends on living within natural limits.

Its followers see the industrial age as powerful but dangerously fragile.

## The Release Tradition

It teaches that the IRF is an enemy or prison.

Its followers preserve engineering knowledge in preparation for a future liberation.

## The Cycle Tradition

It teaches that civilisation moves through recurring phases:

- restraint
- release
- expansion
- collapse
- renewal

## The World Tradition

It teaches that only naturally living worlds are suitable homes for humanity.

Artificial habitats are viewed as temporary vessels, never permanent civilisations.

None of these traditions completely dominates Terra.

---

## 10. Medicine on Terra

Medicine remains scientifically informed but materially limited.

Terran physicians understand:

- infection
- anatomy
- genetics
- nutrition
- epidemiology
- mental health
- heredity
- sanitation

They can perform:

- surgery
- bone repair
- childbirth assistance
- vaccination against some diseases
- antiseptic treatment
- dental procedures
- mechanical prosthetics
- optical correction

They cannot reliably produce:

- complex antibiotics at mass scale
- artificial organs
- genetic therapies
- advanced imaging
- modern anaesthesia everywhere
- precision pharmaceuticals

Average life expectancy varies greatly.

In stable regions, it may reach **65–80 years**.

In poor or isolated regions, it may fall below fifty.

The exceptionally long lives of the industrial era are remembered with particular bitterness.

---

## 11. Terran society

Most people live in small towns, villages and regional cities.

Daily life is labour intensive.

Agriculture employs a large part of the population.

However, this is not an intellectually primitive civilisation.

Literacy is widespread in many regions.

Mathematics, philosophy and history remain highly developed.

A village may lack powered machinery yet possess a school teaching:

- orbital mechanics
- evolutionary biology
- ancient computer science
- comparative planetary history

People know how fusion reactors worked.

They simply cannot build one.

---

## 12. Mars in year 110,000

Mars has survived, but only narrowly.

Its population is far smaller than at the last checkpoint.

The surviving Martian societies are descendants of a handful of geothermal and enclosed-valley settlements.

## Population

Best Terran historical estimates suggest **1–4 million** Martians remain.

Martians themselves have no planetary census.

Many settlements have lost contact with one another.

## Distribution

Survivors live in:

- lava tubes
- geothermal caverns
- buried valley complexes
- pressurised canyon settlements
- ancient water-processing chambers
- sealed agricultural basins

There are no large surface cities.

The surface ruins of the industrial Martian age are mostly abandoned.

---

## 13. The Martian environment

Mars remains hostile.

The unfinished terraforming campaign left limited permanent effects.

## Atmosphere

The atmosphere is somewhat denser than before human settlement but still unbreathable.

Much of the artificially released material has:

- frozen
- escaped into space
- reacted with surface minerals

## Water

Water ice is abundant.

Some geothermal zones maintain liquid reservoirs.

A few enclosed valleys contain artificial lakes.

## Life

Engineered microbes survive in protected soils and beneath ice.

Lichens persist in limited regions.

No open planetary ecosystem exists.

Mars is biologically altered but not naturally habitable.

---

## 14. Martian society

Martian civilisation is defined by maintenance.

Every adult is expected to understand:

- air systems
- seals
- pressure regulation
- water purification
- crop management
- emergency repair

Martian law treats waste and sabotage as existential crimes.

Common offences that carry severe sanctions include:

- contaminating water
- damaging habitat seals
- wasting stored oxygen
- concealing crop disease
- interfering with communal pumps

Martian communities are often politically conservative.

Experimentation is viewed with suspicion because failed innovation can kill an entire settlement.

---

## 15. Martian technology

Martians retain more complex environmental machinery than Terrans, but it operates at low energy and is repaired manually.

Their technology includes:

- mechanical air pumps
- geothermal heat exchangers
- pressure-balanced water systems
- low-voltage electrical networks
- simple radio
- manually controlled greenhouse systems
- mechanical oxygen regulators
- hand-built pressure suits

Many vital components are thousands of years old in design, though repeatedly reconstructed.

Settlements guard their technical manuals as sacred civic property.

---

## 16. Martian biological change

After thousands of years in low gravity and enclosed habitats, Martians differ physically from Terrans.

Common traits include:

- taller, lighter bodies
- lower bone density
- expanded chests
- altered circulation
- greater sensitivity to high gravity
- increased tolerance of low oxygen
- reduced tolerance of Terran pathogens

Some changes originated from pre-collapse genetic engineering.

Others arose through natural selection.

A Martian transported magically to Terra would face serious medical difficulties.

Likewise, many Terran diseases could be catastrophic on Mars.

The two populations remain human but are gradually diverging.

---

## 17. Has Mars survived permanently?

Not necessarily.

Mars has survived to year 110,000, but its long-term future remains uncertain.

Every settlement depends on sealed environments.

Even low-energy machinery eventually fails.

The population has declined gradually for several millennia.

Some Martian scholars believe extinction is inevitable within another ten or twenty thousand years.

Others argue that the remaining settlements have reached genuine equilibrium.

The answer depends on whether their biological ecosystems can replace more mechanical functions before irreplaceable infrastructure fails.

Mars is alive, but never secure.

---

## 18. Titan in year 110,000

Titan is the most successful offworld human society.

It has outlived nearly every other colony.

## Population

Titan supports approximately **8–20 million people**.

The uncertainty exists because its settlements are widely dispersed and communicate slowly.

## Habitat

Most Titanians live in underground thermal regions beneath thick layers of ice and rock.

Their settlements surround:

- geothermal reservoirs
- warm saline lakes
- chemically active caverns
- ancient industrial heat-storage complexes

Titan's dense atmosphere protects against radiation and reduces pressure risks.

This is the main reason Titan survived while the Moon did not.

---

## 19. Titan's environment

The surface remains extremely cold.

Methane and ethane still flow through rivers and lakes.

Industrial ruins are preserved remarkably well by the cold.

Ancient vehicles, towers and machinery remain frozen where they were abandoned.

Below ground, however, human activity has created stable warm ecosystems.

These include:

- fungal forests
- algal pools
- moss chambers
- insect colonies
- dwarf tree species
- artificial wetlands
- small animal populations

Some cavern ecosystems have operated continuously for more than seven thousand years.

They are no longer merely farms.

They are self-regulating biological worlds.

---

## 20. Titanian society

Titanian settlements are larger and more stable than Martian ones.

Their political structures often resemble:

- cavern republics
- water councils
- thermal cooperatives
- hereditary engineering houses
- ecological priesthoods

The central resource is heat.

Access to geothermal flow determines political and economic power.

Territory is measured less by surface area than by:

- thermal capacity
- water availability
- atmospheric processing potential
- biological productivity

---

## 21. Titanian technology

Titanian technology is low-energy but highly adapted to the environment.

It includes:

- wind-driven surface machines
- geothermal pumps
- chemical heating
- mechanical atmosphere separators
- low-voltage lighting
- methane-powered local devices operating below IRF thresholds
- pressure-resistant mechanical transports
- insulated thermal storage

Titanian engineers are perhaps the most accomplished low-energy environmental specialists in human history.

They cannot build spacecraft.

But they can sustain warm biospheres beneath one of the coldest inhabited environments known.

---

## 22. Titanian biological adaptation

Titanians have changed physically and culturally.

Common adaptations include:

- greater cold tolerance
- slower metabolism
- compact body forms
- strong resistance to low-light psychological stress
- altered circadian rhythms
- greater tolerance of enclosed environments

Some traits descend from intentional genetic modification before the industrial collapse.

Others developed naturally.

Most Titanians have never seen Sol directly.

The Sun is a dim point above an atmosphere they rarely enter.

---

## 23. Titan's long-term outlook

Titan appears stable.

Unlike Mars, its surviving environments no longer depend heavily on ancient industrial infrastructure.

Natural geothermal and chemical energy supports them.

Its ecosystems are closed but mature.

Titan is likely to remain inhabited for tens of thousands of years unless:

- geothermal sources shift
- ecological disease spreads
- major cavern systems collapse
- population exceeds thermal capacity

Titan is now humanity's most secure offworld civilisation.

---

## 24. Europa in year 110,000

Europa remains uncertain.

No verified communication has reached Terra or Titan for thousands of years.

Indirect evidence suggests that humans may still survive beneath the ice.

## Probable population

Perhaps between **100,000 and one million**.

This estimate is highly speculative.

## Environment

Europa's subsurface ocean provides:

- liquid water
- natural tidal heat
- hydrothermal chemistry
- protection from radiation

These are sufficient for natural biological ecosystems.

Human survival there is therefore plausible without major industry.

---

## 25. Probable Europan civilisation

If Europans survive, they likely no longer live in conventional dry cities.

Their settlements may be:

- floating biological structures
- partially flooded caverns
- hydrothermal communities
- enclosed air chambers within the ice
- aquatic agricultural zones

Their food webs probably depend on:

- chemosynthetic microbes
- algae
- fungi
- engineered aquatic organisms
- vent ecosystems

Mechanical technology would be difficult to preserve in salt water.

Europan culture may therefore have become more biological than mechanical.

---

## 26. Europan biological divergence

Europans may be the most physically changed human population.

Possible adaptations include:

- increased tolerance of low gravity
- greater resistance to pressure changes
- altered lungs and blood chemistry
- improved swimming ability
- reduced skeletal mass
- stronger resistance to cold
- modified vision in low light

Some may spend much of their lives in water.

After thousands of years of isolation, Europans remain genetically human but may appear distinctly adapted to an aquatic environment.

Whether they survive is not confirmed.

But Europa remains one of the strongest candidates for long-term non-Terran survival.

---

## 27. The extinct societies

By year 110,000, several once-great human societies are certainly gone.

## The Moon

No human population survives.

Its caverns are frozen, depressurised or chemically dead.

## The asteroid belt

All known settlements are extinct.

Rotating habitats have stopped or broken apart.

## Ganymede

Human civilisation is extinct.

## Callisto

No confirmed survivors remain.

Any final settlements likely disappeared thousands of years earlier.

## Most orbital habitats

They have become debris, tombs or ruined shells.

## Uranian and Neptunian colonies

No active signals have been detected for millennia.

They are presumed extinct.

## Kuiper settlements

Extinct after completing their final launches.

The Solar System is filled with human ruins.

Most are unreachable.

---

## 28. Interstellar humanity

The greatest uncertainty lies beyond Sol.

Before full IRF saturation, humanity launched numerous colony missions.

Some reached naturally habitable worlds.

Others remained inside artificial vessels or settled marginal planets.

By year 110,000, the expanding IRF has travelled far beyond the Solar System.

Any colony dependent on industrial life support has probably collapsed.

Colonies on naturally habitable worlds may survive.

---

## 29. Possible extrasolar worlds

Terran records identify several missions that had strong chances of long-term survival.

These included planets with:

- breathable or nearly breathable atmospheres
- liquid surface water
- stable climates
- native or transplanted biospheres
- gravity compatible with human health

At least some likely succeeded.

Their populations may now number millions or billions.

But Terra cannot know.

Interstellar radio transmission is too weak and equipment too limited.

The nearest extrasolar humans may be separated from Terra by only a few light-years, yet the distance is now effectively absolute.

---

## 30. The expanding IRF beyond Sol

By year 110,000, the IRF has expanded through a region containing multiple nearby star systems.

Its rate of expansion has slowed but not stopped.

Physicists before the loss of advanced instrumentation predicted several possibilities:

1. The field may stabilise within a few dozen light-years.
2. It may continue indefinitely.
3. It may reconnect with the ancient galactic Prison Region.
4. It may trigger similar resonances around other stars.

No surviving society can measure it accurately now.

For practical purposes, industrial civilisation is no longer possible anywhere near Sol.

---

## 31. Communication between surviving worlds

There is no regular communication between Terra, Mars, Titan and Europa.

Occasional radio signals may cross space, but several problems make meaningful exchange almost impossible:

- transmitters are weak
- antennas are difficult to maintain
- signals take minutes or hours
- planetary alignment matters
- receiving equipment is rare
- societies use different standards
- languages have diverged
- many transmissions are indistinguishable from noise

Terran astronomers occasionally report artificial radio pulses from Mars or Titan.

Their interpretation is disputed.

A signal may be:

- a deliberate message
- an automated ancient beacon
- electrical interference
- a natural event

The surviving worlds know one another mostly through inherited history.

---

## 32. Languages

After thousands of years, languages have diverged dramatically.

Terran linguistic families number in the thousands.

Martian and Titanian languages descend from the languages of their founding populations but have developed specialised vocabulary for:

- pressure
- air
- heat
- ecology
- maintenance
- communal survival

Ancient industrial languages survive mainly in archives and liturgy.

Scholars study them to read technical documents.

The language once used for interplanetary science is no longer spoken natively anywhere.

---

## 33. Historical accuracy

The Second Breaking is well documented.

However, many details have become uncertain.

Different societies preserve different chronologies.

Terran historians debate:

- the exact population before the impact
- which defence plan came closest to success
- how many people escaped the Moon
- whether Europa still survives
- whether the asteroid was natural
- whether the IRF expansion could have been stopped

Myths have accumulated around real events.

Nemesis is sometimes portrayed as:

- a divine punishment
- an alien weapon
- a natural cosmic seed
- a recurring agent of universal balance
- the physical embodiment of technological pride

Scholarly archives reject many of these claims, but popular traditions remain powerful.

---

## 34. The asteroid crater

The principal impact region remains one of Terra's most important sites.

The original crater has been transformed by:

- erosion
- volcanism
- sedimentation
- ocean circulation
- tectonic movement

Its exact centre is still geologically active.

IRF measurements remain strongest there.

Several cultures prohibit settlement nearby.

Others maintain observatories and monasteries along its margins.

Pilgrims travel there by sea.

The region is called by many names, but the most widespread remains:

**The Wound of Return.**

---

## 35. Attempts to defeat the IRF

Even in year 110,000, some scholars continue searching for a way to remove the field.

Their work is almost entirely theoretical.

Proposals include:

- altering Terra's magnetic resonance
- redirecting large asteroids
- changing planetary orbital relationships
- excavating impact-derived crystalline material
- inducing another spacetime rupture
- moving humanity beyond the field

None is practical.

The civilisation lacks the industrial capacity necessary to test them.

This creates the central paradox of post-industrial science:

> Humanity may understand the prison, yet remain unable to build the tools required to open it.

---

## 36. Mechanical computing

Computing has recovered partially through electromechanical systems.

The largest Terran reasoning engines occupy entire buildings.

They use:

- gears
- relays
- rotating drums
- punched media
- mechanical memory
- low-voltage switching

They calculate:

- astronomical tables
- census statistics
- climate patterns
- engineering designs
- historical chronologies

They are slow but reliable.

Some contain simplified fragments of ancient artificial-intelligence methods.

They cannot host true industrial-era artificial minds.

Yet their operators sometimes describe them as descendants of the Dormant Minds.

---

## 37. The fate of artificial intelligence

No confirmed fully conscious industrial-era AI remains active.

A few ancient mechanical advisory systems may preserve partial personalities or behavioural models.

Most so-called machine oracles are not conscious.

They are expert systems encoded mechanically.

Nonetheless, several religions regard them as surviving fragments of the old minds.

Whether any genuine AI remains hidden in a dormant archive is unknown.

Even if one exists, no society possesses the hardware necessary to awaken it fully.

---

## 38. Education

Education varies widely.

In stable Terran and Titanian societies, children learn:

- reading
- arithmetic
- ecology
- local engineering
- history
- medicine
- astronomy
- survival skills

The industrial era is taught as a warning and an achievement.

On Mars and Titan, technical education is universal because environmental survival depends upon it.

A Martian child may learn pressure repair before advanced literature.

A Titanian child may learn thermal accounting before formal politics.

On Terra, education is broader but less standardised.

---

## 39. Art and culture

The lost space age remains a dominant artistic subject.

Popular Terran stories describe:

- the last launch from the Moon
- silent orbital cities
- Martian engineers beneath failing domes
- Titan's first thermal caverns
- Europan swimmers beneath the ice
- generation ships fleeing the Closing Sky

Ancient recordings survive in a few archives.

Where functioning playback machines exist, people can hear voices from the industrial era.

These recordings are among the most emotionally powerful artefacts in existence.

They allow people to hear ancestors who lived on other planets and assumed humanity would continue expanding forever.

---

## 40. Comparison with the last checkpoint

At the last checkpoint, the main question was:

> Which societies would survive the arrival of full-strength IRF conditions?

By year 110,000, the answer is clearer.

## Confirmed survivors

- Terra
- Mars
- Titan

## Probable survivor

- Europa

## Possible survivors

- naturally habitable extrasolar colonies

## Confirmed or near-certain extinctions

- the Moon
- asteroid-belt societies
- most orbital habitats
- Ganymede
- Callisto
- most minor moons
- Uranian colonies
- Neptunian colonies
- Kuiper settlements
- artificial interstellar habitats overtaken by the field

---

## 41. The greatest change since the last checkpoint

The greatest change is not technological.

It is psychological.

At the last checkpoint, societies still understood themselves as remnants of a shared Solar civilisation.

By year 110,000, that identity has faded.

Terrans, Martians and Titanians increasingly think of themselves as separate peoples.

They share ancestry and history, but not daily institutions.

No government, market, language or communication network unites them.

“Humanity” has become a biological and historical category rather than a functioning civilisation.

---

## 42. The strange success of Terra

The deepest irony of year 110,000 is that Terra, the world struck twice and the source of the restored IRF, is also the world that survives most easily.

The colonies had been technologically superior.

They had:

- cleaner cities
- longer lives
- greater scientific capability
- more advanced machines
- better planning

But they lived inside artificial environments.

Terra's inhabitants survived because their air, water, heat and soil existed without machines.

The old industrial assumption had been that enough technology could make any environment habitable.

The Closing Sky demonstrated the limitation of that idea.

Technology could create temporary worlds.

Only natural biospheres could endure the loss of technology.

---

## 43. Year 110,000 in one image

A Terran scholar stands beside the overgrown foundation of an ancient launch complex.

Above them is a clear night sky.

The Moon is visible, silent and dead.

Mars is a red point whose inhabitants may still be alive.

Titan is invisible to the naked eye, but somewhere beneath its frozen surface millions of humans live around warm underground lakes.

Europa circles Jupiter beneath its shell of ice, perhaps carrying descendants who no longer see themselves as surface-dwellers.

Beyond Sol, other human worlds may exist, separated forever.

The scholar knows exactly what the ruins once were.

They know the physics of rockets.

They know where the colonies were founded.

They know that humans once crossed the void.

But no living person on Terra has travelled higher than the atmosphere.

Humanity has not forgotten the stars.

It has simply lost the ability to reach them.

By year 110,000, the species has achieved a strange form of survival: no longer expanding, no longer unified, and no longer industrial—but still conscious of its full history, still preserving knowledge, and still enduring on every world where life managed to become more than a machine-maintained exception.

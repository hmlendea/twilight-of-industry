# 117500-117600 — The Century of Continuity

## 1. The revised historical ages

During this century, Roman historians standardise the four great ages of human history.

### The Prehistoric Age

**300,000 BC to 4000 BC**

This range covers the span from the emergence of Homo Sapiens to the appearance of the first urban civilisations.

### The First Age

**4000 BC to 1700 AD**

This range covers the era of the earliest cities, kingdoms, republics, empires and monumental cultures, ending at the Pre-Industrial Ceiling.

### The Long Age

**1700 AD to 100,029**

Humanity possesses advanced science, mathematics and craftsmanship but remains constrained by the original full-strength IRF.

### The Industrial Age

**100,029 to 101,379**

The short, extraordinary period of:
- industrialisation
- artificial intelligence
- spaceflight
- interplanetary settlement
- fusion
- planetary engineering

### The Dark Age

**101,379 to 124,550**

Despite growing prosperity and the weakening field, humanity still lives in the Dark Age.

The name does not imply universal ignorance or misery.

It refers to the continuing physical restriction imposed by the IRF and the separation of humanity’s worlds.

---

## 2. The IRF from 117,500 to 117,600

The normal decline continues.

Terra’s average intensity falls from approximately:

- 56.1% in 117,500
- 55.6% in 117,550
- 55.1% in 117,600

The next resonance burst remains scheduled for year **118,110**.

Approximate conditions in 117,600:

| Location | Approximate IRF intensity |
| --- | --- |
| Wound of Return | 44–47% |
| Terra average | 55.1% |
| Near-Terran orbit | 57–60% |
| Luna | 61–65% |
| Mars | 78–84% |
| Inner asteroid belt | 88–94% |
| Jupiter and Europa | 96–100% |
| Saturn and Titan | ~100% |
| Novaterra | 100% |
| Ahead of the expanding frontier | 0% |

The weakening remains most technologically significant around Terra and Luna.

Mars experiences measurable improvement, but not enough for major industrialisation.

Europa and Titan remain essentially unchanged.

---

## 3. What becomes possible on Terra

At approximately 55% intensity, Terra gains incremental rather than revolutionary capabilities.

Newly practical developments include:
- more compact low-density electronic components
- better long-distance aviation
- more reliable orbital launches
- larger but still decentralised electrical networks
- improved medical imaging
- denser electromechanical computation
- more efficient chemical synthesis
- reusable portions of some launch vehicles
- better energy storage at moderate density
- more sophisticated automation

Still unreliable or impossible at scale:
- modern semiconductor civilisation
- nuclear fission power
- fusion
- high-density artificial intelligence
- cheap interplanetary transport
- compact supercomputers
- fully automated high-energy industry

Ancient industrial designs remain invaluable, but many must be radically enlarged, slowed or distributed to remain stable.

---

## 4. Human physiology across the surviving worlds

By this century, humanity no longer has a single typical physical form.

The differences are not generally large enough to make interbreeding impossible. All surviving populations remain human.

But thousands of years of isolation, founder effects, environmental pressure and inherited Industrial Age genetic modification have produced visibly distinct populations.

---

## 5. Terrans

Modern Terrans remain the closest to the ordinary human form of the Industrial Age.

### Average appearance

Terrans vary enormously across regions, but compared with Industrial Age humans they are generally:

- somewhat leaner
- physically more active
- less affected by sedentary metabolic disease
- more diverse in locally inherited adaptations
- slightly shorter on average in poorer rural regions
- slightly taller in prosperous provinces with strong nutrition

Industrial Age genetic modifications persist in some lineages, especially resistance to:

- certain cancers
- inherited metabolic disorders
- radiation damage
- some infectious diseases

However, these traits are unevenly distributed.

### Health

Roman public health gives many citizens excellent lives, but modern Terra lacks some Industrial Age therapies.

Compared with Industrial Age Terrans, contemporary Terrans possess:

- less advanced organ replacement
- less effective genetic medicine
- fewer artificial organs
- weaker emergency trauma care
- lower dependence on continuous pharmaceuticals
- more robust preventive health systems

Average Roman life expectancy in prosperous regions reaches roughly **100–110 years**.

The healthiest individuals sometimes live longer.

This remains below the exceptional lifespans available during the height of the Industrial Age.

### Physiological resilience

Terrans are the least environmentally specialised human population.

They tolerate:

- natural gravity
- open atmosphere
- diverse foods
- wide microbial exposure
- natural weather

This makes them physically adaptable but poorly suited to Mars, Luna, Europa or Titan without preparation.

---

## 5. Lunars

The resettled lunar population is still relatively young, but enough time has passed for families to have lived there for several generations.

Most Lunars descend from Terran settlers rather than ancient lunar survivors.

### Appearance

Compared with Terrans, Lunar-born people tend to be:

- taller
- more lightly built
- lower in muscle mass
- longer-limbed
- more prone to bone loss
- more sensitive to full Terran gravity

Strict exercise, centrifuge use and medical monitoring reduce these effects.

Children spend scheduled time in rotating gravity facilities.

### Biological adaptation

There has not yet been enough time for major natural evolution.

However, inherited Industrial Age traits and selective reproductive screening favour:

- stronger bone maintenance
- improved calcium metabolism
- better circulation in low gravity
- reduced inner-ear sensitivity
- improved radiation repair

Lunars remain visibly close to Terrans.

A Lunar visiting Terra can function normally, but may require months of conditioning and medical support.

### Comparison with ancient Industrial Age Lunars

Industrial Age lunar populations relied more heavily on advanced medicine and artificial gravity technology.

Modern Lunars possess less medical capability but much stronger cultural discipline around:

- exercise
- nutrition
- pressure safety
- radiation exposure
- reproductive health

They are physically less protected by technology and more socially adapted to compensate.

---

## 6. Martians

Martians are the most clearly differentiated large non-Terran population.

Their ancestors lived for thousands of years in low gravity, low-pressure environments and sealed settlements.

### Appearance

Typical Martians are:

- tall and lightly built
- narrow-waisted
- long-limbed
- relatively low in body mass
- large-chested
- pale or lightly pigmented in settlements with little natural light
- highly sensitive to heavy gravity

Some possess subtly altered facial structures linked to respiratory and sinus adaptation.

### Physiology

Martians tend to have:

- expanded lung capacity
- efficient oxygen transport
- lower baseline blood pressure
- reduced bone density
- altered cardiovascular regulation
- slower muscle growth
- strong tolerance for low oxygen
- weaker tolerance for Terran pathogens

Some of these characteristics descend from deliberate Industrial Age gene modification.

Others emerged through long selection.

A Martian suddenly placed on Terra could suffer:

- joint strain
- heart stress
- difficulty walking
- respiratory overload
- severe fatigue

### Comparison with Industrial Age Terrans

Industrial Age Terrans could visit Mars using advanced medical support and pressure systems.

Modern Martians are physically much better suited to Mars than those early colonists, but considerably less suited to Terra.

They are moving toward an environment-specific human form.

---

## 7. Europans

Europans are the most physically unusual known human population.

Their communities have lived for millennia in low gravity, cold water and pressurised subsurface environments.

### Appearance

Available descriptions suggest that Europans are often:

- tall but lightly muscled
- extremely flexible
- long-fingered
- broad-chested
- relatively low in skeletal mass
- pale or translucent-skinned by Terran standards
- large-eyed or highly sensitive to dim light

Some lineages display partial webbing of fingers or toes, though this is not universal.

### Physiology

Likely Europan adaptations include:

- strong diving reflexes
- slower oxygen consumption
- enhanced cold tolerance
- resistance to pressure changes
- altered blood chemistry
- high tolerance for elevated carbon dioxide
- improved balance in low gravity
- efficient swimming musculature

Many Europans can spend long periods in water but still require air.

They are not fully aquatic.

### Comparison with Industrial Age Terrans

An Industrial Age Terran required extensive equipment to live beneath Europa’s ice.

A modern Europan is biologically far better adapted, but still depends on artificial air spaces, cultivated ecosystems and pressure structures.

A Europan brought to Terra would find:

- gravity exhausting
- sunlight painful
- open horizons psychologically disorienting
- uncontrolled microorganisms dangerous

---

## 8. Titanians

Titanians have adapted to enclosed underground environments, weak light and extreme external cold.

### Appearance

They are generally:

- shorter and more compact than Martians
- sturdily built
- relatively high in body fat
- broad-bodied
- pale-skinned in most populations
- slow-moving by Terran cultural standards

These are broad tendencies, not universal traits.

### Physiology

Titanians often possess:

- slower metabolism
- strong cold tolerance
- efficient heat retention
- resistance to low-light circadian disorders
- reduced caloric requirements
- strong tolerance for enclosed environments
- somewhat reduced peak physical speed

Many adaptations began as Industrial Age modifications intended for long-duration outer-system habitation.

### Comparison with Terrans

A Titanian could live on Terra more easily than a Martian or Europan, but might struggle with:

- heat
- strong sunlight
- rapid day-night changes
- open natural environments
- unfamiliar pathogens

Titanians are less physically extreme than Europans but culturally more isolated.

---

## 9. Novaterrans

Novaterrans live on an Earthlike world with similar gravity, pressure, temperature and day length.

Physiologically, they remain close to Terrans.

### Differences

After millennia of isolation, common Novaterran traits include:

- adaptation to a roughly twenty-five-hour day
- altered seasonal hormone cycles
- founder-effect differences in immunity
- slightly different average body proportions in some regions
- strong resistance to the limited microbial ecosystem they created

Because Novaterra originally contained no life, its inhabitants historically encountered fewer uncontrolled pathogens.

This has left them vulnerable to biological diversity that Terrans consider ordinary.

A Terran visiting Novaterra could introduce devastating diseases.

A Novaterran visiting Terra would face even greater risk.

### Compared with Industrial Age Terrans

Physically they resemble Industrial Age humans more closely than Martians, Europans or Titanians do.

Their main divergence is immunological and cultural, not anatomical.

---

## 10. The fleeing ship population

The inhabitants of the endless colony ship remain biologically close to Industrial Age humans because their environment is carefully controlled.

However, they possess:

- adaptations to rotational gravity
- reduced tolerance for natural weather
- strong resistance to enclosed-environment diseases
- highly managed genetic diversity
- modified circadian rhythms
- low tolerance for uncontrolled microbial ecosystems

Their bodies are not dramatically changed.

Their psychology and sensory expectations are.

Open sky, natural horizons and unregulated seasons would be profoundly unfamiliar.

---

## 11. The Human Continuity Exchange

In **117,506**, Rome launches the **Human Continuity Exchange**, a joint Terra–Luna, Martian, Europan and Titanian Class100 programme.

Its founding principle is:

> Safety is a human inheritance, not a commercial possession.

All participating societies agree that survival technology cannot be privately monopolised.

The programme shares:

- life-support methods
- water systems
- low-energy computation
- agricultural designs
- air purification
- emergency medicine
- archive preservation
- structural safety
- low-power communication
- Class100 manufacturing techniques

No licence fees are charged for core survival designs.

Commercial improvements may still be sold, but the underlying emergency designs remain public.

---

## 12. Automatic Class100 compatibility

The programme initially classifies the following as **Class100-Compatible**:
- all verified Long Age designs
- all Dark Age designs created before the IRF began weakening
- all proven Titanian low-energy systems
- surviving Martian and Europan systems known to operate under local near-full IRF conditions

This classification means only that the design can function under full-strength IRF.

It does not guarantee that it is:
- efficient
- durable
- safe
- scalable
- resource-efficient
- easy to repair

Each design must undergo formal review before receiving true **Class100 certification**.

---

## 13. The review process

Review teams examine millions of historical designs.

A surprising proportion of Long Age devices receive full Class100 status.

Long Age engineers had refined many systems over thousands of years.

Common successful designs include:
- water regulators
- ventilation systems
- passive cooling
- hydraulic controls
- mechanical warning devices
- low-pressure heat engines
- gravity storage
- modular mills
- durable bearings
- relay logic
- low-power radio equipment

Many Titanian designs also qualify, particularly in:
- insulation
- thermal storage
- fungal agriculture
- low-light ecosystems
- chemical heat management

Martian systems excel in:
- air recycling
- water conservation
- pressure compartmentalisation
- ecological isolation

Europan contributions are strongest in:
- aquatic filtration
- corrosion resistance
- thermal-gradient power
- biological nutrient cycling

---

## 14. How the exchange improves Luna

Luna receives immediate benefits.

Martian pressure-system techniques improve:
- airlock durability
- compartment isolation
- emergency water recovery

Titanian methods improve:
- thermal storage
- low-light agriculture
- insulation

Europan biological filtration improves:
- water recycling
- microbial control
- nutrient recovery

Class100 machinery cannot make Luna self-sufficient.

It can make failure slower and recovery more likely.

Emergency survival times increase from months to years in some districts.

---

## 15. How the exchange improves Mars

Mars receives Roman and Long Age designs for:
- low-friction pumps
- hydraulic computation
- passive heat management
- durable mechanical controls
- distributed manufacturing
- low-resource medicine

Several devices are physically simple enough to reproduce with Martian industry.

Living standards improve modestly.

Settlement maintenance requires fewer labour hours.

Water loss decreases.

Mechanical failures become less frequent.

Mars remains poor in energy but gains time and reliability.

---

## 16. How the exchange improves Europa

Communication delays make progress slow.

Still, Europans receive designs for:
- low-power acoustic networks
- ceramic mechanical controls
- long-lived archive systems
- pressure monitoring
- emergency air storage
- low-light medical equipment

Rome receives Europan methods for:
- biological filtration
- submerged structural repair
- aquatic food chains
- corrosion-resistant ceramics

The exchange is measured in years rather than days.

It nonetheless produces measurable improvements in Europan survival.

---

## 17. How the exchange improves Titan

Titan contributes more technology than it initially receives.

Its society has operated at full IRF intensity for millennia.

Roman designs eventually improve:
- medical instruments
- electromechanical communication
- archive indexing
- legal and technical standardisation

Titanian cavern federations remain cautious about adopting foreign systems.

They accept only designs that can be locally reproduced.

---

## 18. Collecting offworld histories

Rome begins assembling a full history of humanity after the Second Breaking.

The Great Libraries already contain detailed Terran and lunar records.

They now seek:

- Martian settlement histories
- Europan oral and acoustic records
- Titanian cavern chronicles
- interplanetary communication fragments
- demographic records
- environmental accounts
- personal testimony

The work is slower than the Class100 exchange because immediate survival technology receives transmission priority.

---

## 19. Martian history

Mars sends the most complete offworld historical archive.

Its records describe:

- the arrival of refugees from Luna
- the failure of terraforming systems
- famine
- wars over geothermal zones
- the Martian Covenant
- settlement extinctions
- ecological adaptation
- the recent agricultural crisis

The history is often painful.

It also demonstrates that Mars survived because cooperation eventually replaced competition.

Roman schools begin teaching Martian history as part of the Dark Age curriculum.

---

## 20. Europan history

Europan history is difficult to reconstruct.

Much was preserved through:

- oral recitation
- acoustic pattern archives
- engraved ceramics
- lineage memory
- ecological records

The Europans do not always organise time according to the same calendar as Terra.

Their histories emphasise:

- movement between vents
- ocean currents
- settlement merging
- ecological lineages
- periods of ice disruption

Rome must avoid forcing Europan history into Terran political categories.

Only fragments are incorporated into the Great Libraries during this century.

---

## 21. Titanian history

Titanian records are extensive but slow to transmit.

They describe:

- the retreat from surface cities
- creation of thermal caverns
- famine and population contraction
- formation of cavern republics
- ecological stabilisation
- disputes over heat
- isolation from Terra

Titanian historians are initially reluctant to send complete archives.

They fear cultural appropriation and Roman centralisation.

Trust improves gradually.

---

## 22. The Roman Safety Institutions

Rome creates a powerful regulatory structure known collectively as the **Continuity Authorities**.

Its principal bodies include:

### The Authority for Medicines and Biological Goods

Approves:

- drugs
- vaccines
- mass-produced food
- biological treatments
- restored species intended for release

### The Authority for Devices and Machinery

Approves:

- consumer devices
- industrial equipment
- vehicles
- electrical systems
- Class100 machinery

### The Authority for Structures and Habitats

Approves:

- buildings
- bridges
- hospitals
- public works
- orbital structures
- lunar habitats

### The Office of Resource Continuity

Assesses:

- material scarcity
- repairability
- recycling
- replacement-part requirements
- disaster survivability

These institutions can prohibit any product from mass production.

---

## 23. Regulatory tiers

Products are classified by use.

## Civilian tier

Ordinary tools, appliances and consumer goods.

## Commercial-industrial tier

Factories, transport and large machinery.

## Public tier

Schools, water systems, transport networks and government facilities.

## Health tier

Hospitals, medicines, food and biological systems.

## Military tier

Weapons, military vehicles and defence systems.

## Space tier

Orbital and lunar equipment.

## Continuity tier

Systems intended to function during civilisational collapse or full IRF return.

Each tier has different requirements.

Space and Continuity standards are the strictest.

---

## 24. Durability requirements

Rome imposes exceptionally long design lives.

Depending on tier, equipment may be required to remain serviceable for:

- twenty years
- fifty years
- one hundred years
- several centuries
- indefinitely with component replacement

Manufacturers must provide:

- repair manuals
- part specifications
- fallback modes
- material lists
- known failure patterns
- Class rating
- recycling instructions
- archive copies of the design

Planned obsolescence becomes illegal for critical products.

---

## 25. Low-resource engineering

Roman planners realise that low energy is not enough.

A machine may operate at 100% IRF but still be useless after a catastrophe if it requires:

- rare metals
- complex lubricants
- precision replacement parts
- imported chemicals
- specialist tools

The new doctrine therefore evaluates **resource depth**.

A resilient device should ideally be repairable through several descending material levels.

For example:

1. original high-quality parts;
2. standard industrial substitutes;
3. locally machined replacements;
4. simplified emergency components;
5. manual fallback.

This becomes known as **material descent**.

---

## 26. Criticism of Roman regulation

Manufacturers complain that approval takes too long.

A new medicine may require decades of testing.

A building design may face years of review.

Small inventors struggle with compliance costs.

Critics argue that Rome is:

- slowing innovation
- protecting large established firms
- making housing more expensive
- reducing product variety
- treating every object as if it must survive an asteroid impact

The term **Civic Paranoia** becomes common.

Some independent states deliberately advertise themselves as freer environments for experimentation.

---

## 27. Rome refuses to retreat

The government makes limited procedural reforms but does not weaken the core standards.

Its position is that a century of delay is insignificant compared with the cost of another collapse.

The Senate repeatedly states:

> A dangerous device is not innovative merely because it is new.

Rome creates public laboratories to help small inventors meet certification requirements.

This reduces but does not eliminate the burden.

Innovation slows in some fields.

Reliability improves dramatically.

---

## 28. Effects on architecture

Urban building codes are transformed.

New structures must provide:

- passive ventilation
- gravity-fed water reserves
- fire compartmentalisation
- emergency shelter
- manual access systems
- standard measurements
- durable materials
- local repairability
- energy fallback
- long-term maintenance plans

Large residential buildings must be capable of surviving extended grid failure.

Hospitals require multiple independent systems.

Schools double as disaster shelters.

Buildings become more expensive.

They also become extraordinarily durable.

---

## 29. The aesthetic of permanence

The safety codes reinforce Ars Continua.

Roman buildings are designed to age over centuries.

Exposed mechanisms are labelled in Permanent Latin.

Structural components can be inspected.

Service tunnels are large enough for human repair.

Decorative elements often communicate function.

A water reservoir may display carved diagrams of the city’s supply system.

A hospital wall may contain engraved emergency instructions that remain readable without electricity.

Rome begins to look less disposable than any Industrial Age civilisation.

---

## 30. Lunar provincialisation

Luna Restituta continues expanding.

By year **117,520**, many Lunar-born residents are adults whose parents and grandparents were also born there.

The population demands:

- civilian neighbourhoods
- elected local councils
- family housing
- ordinary schools
- cultural institutions
- legal equality with Terran provinces

Rome begins preparing some lunar districts for eventual provincial status.

---

## 31. Daily life on Luna

Lunar life becomes less like an expedition and more like a society.

Residents possess:

- schools
- theatres
- public baths
- markets
- libraries
- gardens
- civic halls
- sports facilities
- local media

Artificial daylight cycles are carefully regulated.

Children spend time in rotating gravity facilities.

Most adults still perform periodic safety duty.

Emergency drills remain frequent.

Every resident knows:

- pressure procedures
- fire response
- suit use
- shelter routes
- air-system basics

Lunar citizenship remains demanding.

---

## 32. Restricted movement continues

Rome does not permit unrestricted migration to Luna.

Applicants must meet:

- health standards
- psychological screening
- technical education
- population-capacity limits
- emergency training requirements

Lunar-born citizens possess full Roman rights but cannot freely bring unlimited relatives from Terra.

This creates constitutional controversy.

The government argues that freedom of movement cannot override habitat survival.

The Constitutional Court accepts temporary restrictions but orders periodic review.

---

## 33. Luna in year 117,600

Lunar population reaches approximately **420,000–550,000**.

Most live in restored ancient caverns or new modular districts.

Several settlements are economically specialised in:

- archaeology
- archive preservation
- astronomy
- relay operation
- biological storage
- spacecraft maintenance

Luna remains dependent on Terra for:

- complex medicine
- precision components
- population replacement
- many food supplements

However, local emergency self-sufficiency improves to several years in the best settlements.

The first fully fledged lunar province is expected in the following century, but none is formally created by 117,600.

---

## 34. Terra’s political map in year 117,500

At the beginning of the century, Terra contains **28 internationally recognised sovereign states**.

These include:

- the Roman Republic
- six Atlantic League members
- nine Serevan Compact members
- twelve unaffiliated or associated independent states

Rome controls roughly:

- 80% of Terra’s population
- 68% of inhabited land
- 78% of total economic output

The remaining states survive through combinations of trade, geography, historical legitimacy and Roman restraint.

---

## 35. Political consolidation by year 117,600

During the century:

- four poor states join Rome
- three long-independent former League states are accepted into Rome
- two small unaffiliated states merge with neighbouring independent states
- two League members secede
- one Serevan member withdraws and becomes Roman-associated

By year **117,600**, Terra contains **21 sovereign states**:

| Group | Sovereign states | Population share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roman Republic | 1 | 85–87% |
| Atlantic League | 4 | 3–4% |
| Serevan Compact | 8 | 7–8% |
| Other independent states | 8 | 3–5% |

Rome governs a vast majority of humanity on Terra.

The remaining states are politically significant but demographically small.

---

## 36. Size of the Roman Republic

By the end of the century, Rome contains approximately **7.2–7.8 billion people** out of a total Terran population of roughly **8.5–9.0 billion**.

Its territory includes:

- most of the great continental agricultural regions
- the majority of major cities
- nearly all lunar launch infrastructure
- most restored industrial zones
- extensive former Mandate lands
- several former League states

The Republic is effectively continental and planetary in scale.

It still refuses to describe independent states as illegitimate.

---

## 37. Former League states join Rome

Rome finally accepts several states that had left the Atlantic League many decades earlier.

The Republic argues that enough time has passed that accession cannot reasonably be interpreted as wartime poaching.

The remaining League objects rhetorically but does not impose sanctions.

It lacks both the power and desire for confrontation.

Accession referenda produce strong majorities in favour.

The new Roman provinces retain one secondary official language but adopt Permanent Latin for national affairs.

---

## 38. The Atlantic League and the Serevan talks

The reduced Atlantic League formally opens negotiations with the Serevan Compact in **117,548**.

The League wants:

- a wider market
- political protection
- reduced dependence on Rome
- access to inland resources

The Serevan Compact wants:

- League ports
- financial expertise
- maritime transport
- diplomatic weight

Talks continue for years.

---

## 39. Why full merger fails

The League and Compact cannot agree on:

- common tariffs
- military command
- financial regulation
- voting power
- Roman relations
- treatment of secession movements

League states fear domination by the more numerous Serevan members.

Serevan states fear control by League banks and ports.

In **117,563**, they settle for an **Associated Partnership**, not full membership.

The arrangement includes:

- preferential trade
- joint diplomatic consultations
- limited defence planning
- transport cooperation

The League remains legally separate.

The Serevan Compact remains the principal organised non-Roman bloc.

---

## 40. The orbital station project

In **117,527**, Rome proposes a permanent crewed station in Terra orbit.

Unlike wartime platforms, it is intended for:

- science
- debris clearing
- communications
- medical research
- lunar mission support
- international cooperation

Rome invites every sovereign state to co-found the project.

Participants may construct their own modules if they follow Roman space standards.

---

## 41. International participation

The Atlantic League accepts.

Several Serevan states accept individually, despite Compact hesitation.

Mars is offered symbolic founding status and contributes environmental-control designs.

Luna supplies construction crews and orbital experience.

The station is named **Concordia Orbitalis**.

Permanent Latin is the operational language.

Participating states may use their own languages internally within national modules, but all emergency and technical systems must use Roman standards.

---

## 42. Debris clearing accelerates

The station cannot be safely built without clearing orbital debris.

Rome dramatically expands the Orbital Cartography Service.

New systems include:

- radar networks
- optical telescope chains
- debris-tagging beacons
- slow capture tugs
- drag sails
- ablative interception nets
- controlled deorbit vehicles

Debris removal remains slow.

Some fragments are too small to capture and too numerous to ignore.

Launch corridors are carefully scheduled.

The programme reduces—but never eliminates—risk.

---

## 43. Concordia Orbitalis

Construction begins in **117,539**.

The first crew arrives in **117,546**.

The station uses rotating habitation sections to provide partial gravity.

By 117,600 it contains:

- Roman scientific modules
- Atlantic commercial laboratories
- Serevan observation modules
- a Lunar logistics section
- a joint asteroid-monitoring centre
- a Martian communications chamber

The permanent population varies between **1,200 and 1,800**.

Rotating crews increase the total number of people who have lived there to tens of thousands.

It becomes the first enduring multinational institution physically above Terra.

---

## 44. Old orbital habitats

A few remnants of ancient Industrial Age habitats remain in high or unusually stable orbits.

Most are:

- depressurised
- structurally damaged
- fragmented
- contaminated by debris

Some contain sealed internal sections.

Rome sends recovery missions.

---

## 45. The Habitat Archive Missions

Between **117,552 and 117,588**, crews reach five major habitat remnants.

They recover:

- population records
- engineering manuals
- AI fragments
- personal archives
- recordings of the post-impact orbital collapse
- medical databases
- habitat ecological records

One habitat contains a sealed chamber with preserved bodies and an intact mechanical emergency archive.

Another breaks apart during inspection, killing six astronauts.

The missions complete an important part of the Dark Age historical record.

---

## 46. The rediscovery of the pyramids

In **117,518**, geological surveys in a heavily sedimented North African region identify enormous artificial stone formations beneath layers of sand, flood deposits and later ruins.

Excavation reveals the remnants of the ancient pyramids of Egypt.

They have been:

- eroded
- partially collapsed
- quarried repeatedly
- buried by geological and human processes
- forgotten for tens of thousands of years

Only fractions of their original forms survive.

Even so, their scale is unmistakable.

---

## 47. The dating shock

Initial dating places their construction deep in the First Age, more than one hundred thousand years before the present.

The discovery astonishes the public.

The builders possessed:
- no industrial machinery
- no advanced electrical systems
- no modern logistics
- limited metals
- comparatively primitive science

Yet they organised labour and materials on a monumental scale.

Long Age scholars had known fragments of early monumental traditions, but the pyramids had faded into incomplete references.

Their physical rediscovery transforms understanding of the First Age.

---

## 48. Finding the records

Archivists search Great Library collections, lunar databases and recovered Industrial Age records.

They eventually identify:
- old photographs
- archaeological surveys
- architectural measurements
- translations of inscriptions
- theories of construction
- historical descriptions

The pyramids had been extensively studied during multiple earlier ages.

The relevant records survived, but had become disconnected from the physical site and buried within enormous archives.

This demonstrates a crucial problem:

Knowledge can survive and still become functionally lost if no one knows where to look.

---

## 49. The restoration begins

Rome immediately protects the site.

Restoration policy is conservative.

The Republic does not simply rebuild perfect modern replicas.

It seeks to preserve:

- surviving original stone
- later alterations
- evidence of erosion
- excavation layers
- historical context

A limited reconstruction demonstrates the original geometry without hiding the surviving ruins.

The project is expected to last centuries.

Nearby settlements expand into a major archaeological and cultural centre.

---

## 50. Effect on the wider restoration programme

The pyramid discovery produces a wave of public enthusiasm.

Funding increases for:

- buried-city excavation
- monument surveys
- archive indexing
- First Age research
- Long Age archaeology
- restoration education

Citizens begin searching local landscapes for forgotten structures.

Thousands of sites are identified.

Most are not as ancient or dramatic as the pyramids.

Together they reveal the extraordinary depth of human settlement on Terra.

---

## 51. The century’s major new archaeological settlements

Several buried urban centres are partially restored and resettled.

They include cities from:
- the First Age
- the Long Age
- the Industrial Age
- early Dark Age refuge networks
- the Unification War

New communities live within restored historical structures while using modern infrastructure beneath or beside them.

These cities become symbols of continuity across the four ages.

---

## 52. Mars during the century

Mars’s population grows to approximately **430,000–600,000** by 117,600.

Its IRF level falls toward **78–84%**.

This permits modest improvements in:

- electrical reliability
- radio strength
- mechanical transport
- low-pressure industrial chemistry

It does not permit high-energy industry.

### Daily life

Martians still live in:

- geothermal caverns
- enclosed valleys
- buried agricultural districts

Mandatory maintenance remains part of citizenship.

Class100 exchange reduces labour requirements and improves food diversity.

### Society

The Martian Covenant becomes a genuine confederal government in limited fields:

- communication
- archives
- ecological safety
- external relations
- planetary defence

Individual settlements retain sovereignty over most daily law.

### Outlook

Mars is no longer demographically endangered.

Its population remains tiny, but it is slowly expanding.

---

## 53. Europa during the century

Europa’s population reaches an estimated **230,000–430,000**.

IRF intensity remains around **96–100%**.

No major new energy technology becomes possible.

Class100 exchange improves:

- emergency air systems
- medical equipment
- archives
- acoustic communications
- food-web monitoring

Europans remain physiologically and culturally distinct.

Their Oceanic Assembly gains regular communication with several vent regions.

It remains a cooperative body rather than a state.

---

## 54. Titan during the century

Titan’s population grows to approximately **24–32 million**.

IRF remains effectively **100%**.

Titan contributes heavily to Class100 technology.

The first Roman relay finally reaches a useful Saturnian communication orbit late in the century.

Communication remains extremely slow, but reliable text exchange becomes possible.

A short message may take weeks to transmit completely.

Titanian federations begin sending:

- engineering manuals
- historical records
- ecological methods
- linguistic archives

Permanent Latin becomes increasingly common among Titanian external-relations scholars.

---

## 55. Novaterra during the century

Novaterra’s population rises to approximately **120–170 million**.

Its biosphere continues spreading but remains patchy.

### Physiology

Novaterrans remain physically Terran-like, with strong founder effects and distinctive immunity.

### Technology

The system remains under full-strength IRF.

Novaterra continues using:

- water and wind power
- low-voltage electricity
- mechanical railways
- hydraulic and relay computation
- advanced ecological science

### Politics

The nine major states remain independent.

The Novaterran Concord becomes stronger in ecological administration but does not become a world government.

### Cultural direction

Unlike Rome, Novaterra is less cautious about future industrialisation.

Many Novaterrans consider the full-strength IRF an unjust physical barrier and intend to industrialise rapidly if it ever weakens.

Their present low-energy systems are respected as necessities, not necessarily permanent ideals.

---

## 56. The fleeing ship

The colony ship remains beyond the IRF frontier.

Its population is increasingly adapted to controlled rotation and enclosed life.

The ship’s industrial systems continue functioning.

During this century, its scientists confirm that the IRF shell behind them is not uniform.

They identify:

- thinning regions
- pulse-like structures
- irregular intensity gradients

They still do not know that the field is weakening at its Solar origin.

A serious scientific faction now argues that endless flight may no longer be necessary.

The government refuses to reverse course without stronger evidence.

---

## 57. Terra’s population and living standards

By year **117,600**, Terra contains approximately **8.5–9.0 billion people**.

Rome contains approximately **7.2–7.8 billion**.

Population growth has slowed significantly.

In prosperous Roman provinces, birth rates are near or below replacement.

Poorer associated and independent states grow more rapidly.

### Roman living standards

Most Roman citizens have access to:

- universal healthcare
- the civic income floor
- reliable food and water
- electricity
- public transport
- advanced education
- durable housing
- extensive communication
- legal protection
- emergency resilience systems

Regional inequality remains, but absolute deprivation is rare.

---

## 58. Comparison with Industrial Age Terra

Modern Roman society is less technologically concentrated than Industrial Age civilisation.

It lacks:

- semiconductor abundance
- advanced AI
- fusion
- effortless global transport
- interplanetary commerce
- extreme medical intervention

It surpasses much of the Industrial Age in:

- infrastructure durability
- universal legal protection
- disaster preparation
- regional equality
- archival redundancy
- low-energy resilience
- product reliability
- long-term planning

Industrial Age people lived with more powerful machines.

Modern Romans increasingly live with machines expected to remain useful for generations.

---

## 59. Was Rome too cautious?

By the end of the century, the argument remains unresolved.

Critics can point to:

- slower innovation
- expensive housing
- long drug approvals
- reduced consumer choice
- bureaucratic power
- regulatory capture
- talented inventors leaving for independent states

Supporters can point to:

- low rates of structural collapse
- reliable medicines
- minimal catastrophic product failures
- extraordinary disaster resilience
- long-lived infrastructure
- successful Lunar safety
- improved offworld survival

Rome has chosen to accept slower progress in exchange for lower civilisational risk.

Independent Terran states become testing grounds for faster, less regulated innovation.

Some succeed spectacularly.

Others experience major failures that reinforce Roman caution.

## 60. The state of the Great Projects in 117,600

### Class100 exchange

Active across Terra, Luna, Mars, Europa and Titan.

### Class100 certification

Millions of designs reviewed; Long Age systems perform exceptionally well.

### Great Libraries

Expanded with offworld histories, survival designs and recovered orbital archives.

### Lunar settlement

Approaching half a million inhabitants and preparing for eventual provincial status.

### Concordia Orbitalis

Permanently crewed and internationally supported.

### Solar relay system

Operational to Mars, improving toward Jupiter, newly functional toward Saturn.

### Latin preservation

Highly effective formally, though local spoken varieties persist.

### Restoration programme

Revitalised by the pyramids and buried-city discoveries.

### Biosphere restoration

Continuing under strict safety review.

### AI archaeology

Growing rapidly, though no complete Industrial Age AI has yet returned.

---

## 61. The final character of the century

The century’s greatest achievement is not one machine or one city.

It is the creation of a shared concept of survival across multiple human worlds.

Terrans, Lunars, Martians, Europans and Titanians inhabit radically different environments and possess increasingly different bodies.

They do not share one government.

They do not share one daily life.

They are beginning to share:

- technical standards
- emergency designs
- historical archives
- Class100 knowledge
- Permanent Latin for interworld communication
- the belief that safety cannot be privately owned

The Industrial Age had connected the worlds through powerful transportation.

The Dark Age begins reconnecting them through slow messages, shared memory and machines designed to keep working when almost everything else fails.

And while Rome grows more powerful, its most important export is no longer law, citizenship or industry.

It is redundancy.

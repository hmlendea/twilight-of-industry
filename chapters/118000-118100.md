  At the beginning of **118,000**, the Roman Republic is no longer merely a territorial state spread across Terra and Luna.

  It is becoming a polity composed of worlds.

  That transition is forced by practical reality. Luna now contains over one hundred million people, its own economy, its own environmental limits and a growing political identity. Mars has unified independently under the Martian Confederacy. Titanian societies are creating global institutions without accepting global government. Even the Harbour System begins dividing into separate futures.

  The century is dominated by five connected developments:

  1. The Martian Dream becomes the organising purpose of an entire planet.
  2. A second Lunar food crisis forces Luna to govern itself collectively.
  3. Rome restructures itself from a province-centred state into a world-centred republic.
  4. Urban overcrowding drives mass migration, especially toward Luna.
  5. The Harbour civilisation begins preparing to divide into settlers, returners and continuing travellers.

  The next scheduled IRF resonance burst is due in **118,110**, only ten years after the end of this period.

## 118,001 — The Martian Demographic Declaration

The Martian Confederacy publishes its first long-term demographic strategy.

It does not impose compulsory reproduction. Instead, it frames population growth as a civic contribution to the transformation of Mars.

The declaration argues that Mars needs:

- more ecologists;
- more engineers;
- more farmers;
- more settlement founders;
- more teachers;
- more citizens capable of maintaining a planetary project across generations.

Larger families become culturally associated with confidence in Mars’s future.

A common Martian saying emerges:

> “More hands for the open world.”

The Confederacy provides:

- expanded family housing;
- food guarantees;
- childcare cooperatives;
- parental maintenance exemptions;
- reproductive medicine;
- education subsidies;
- settlement grants.

The birth rate begins rising within a decade.

---

## 118,003 — The Martian Education Reform

Mars rewrites its education system around the Martian Dream.

Every child continues learning ordinary subjects, but the curriculum increasingly emphasises:

- ecology;
- hydrology;
- atmospheric physics;
- soil chemistry;
- microbiology;
- low-energy engineering;
- settlement maintenance;
- planetary history;
- terraformation ethics.

Students undertake practical environmental work.

A child may help:

- monitor microbial soil;
- maintain algae systems;
- map water loss;
- test pressure-tolerant plants;
- analyse atmospheric data.

Terraforming is not taught as distant fantasy.

It is taught as the central intergenerational responsibility of Martian citizenship.

---

## 118,005 — The First Open Ground Schools

Several Martian settlements create schools attached to controlled outdoor ecological test areas.

Students cannot walk outside without protection, but they can observe:

- exposed microbial mats;
- thin-cover plant enclosures;
- ice-melt channels;
- surface weather instruments;
- pressure-tolerant mosses.

The schools become symbols of the new Martian identity.

Older Martians often describe the surface as a place of danger.

Younger Martians increasingly describe it as unfinished territory.

---

## 118,007 — Rome expands Wound experimentation

The Roman Republic authorises a larger experimental zone around the Wound of Return, where IRF intensity is approximately fifteen percentage points lower than the Terran average.

Researchers test:

- denser electronic switching;
- compact turbine systems;
- improved rocket stages;
- higher-energy medical imaging;
- advanced synthetic materials;
- faster computing architectures.

The Continuity Authorities require that every experiment remain geographically confined and mechanically isolatable.

The Wound develops into Terra’s technological frontier.

It is also increasingly unequal.

Local industries can use machinery unavailable elsewhere, attracting:

- engineers;
- investors;
- migrants;
- research institutions.

Housing prices rise dramatically.

The government begins regulating access to prevent the region from becoming a privileged technological enclave.

---

## 118,009 — The Second Lunar Monoculture Crisis

A new fungal pathogen appears in Luna’s principal high-protein algae system.

Unlike the earlier grain failure, this pathogen spreads through several provinces simultaneously because their algae lines descend from a small number of highly productive genetic strains.

Within months:

- protein output falls by nearly 30%;
- emergency reserves are opened;
- civilian transport is reduced to prioritise food shipments;
- Terra begins massive supply launches;
- Lunar prices rise sharply.

The crisis is not immediately catastrophic, but Luna’s population is now so large that Terran supply alone cannot cover a complete failure.

---

## 118,010 — Luna acts without Terra

The five Lunar provincial governments convene in emergency session.

Without waiting for the Roman central government, they adopt identical regulations:

- no critical food system may rely on fewer than twelve major genetic lines;
- no province may source more than one-third of a staple from the same biological lineage;
- every province must maintain isolated seed, fungal and microbial reserves;
- food systems must remain separable by province;
- emergency agricultural capacity must be tested annually;
- Lunar provinces must share biological information automatically.

The rules are adopted simultaneously and enforced through the Lunar Provincial Council.

This is the first effective world-level Lunar policy made independently of Terra.

Rome later approves it retroactively.

The sequence matters politically.

Luna acted first.

---

## 118,012 — The Lunar Autonomy Movement

The food crisis ends after Mars, Titan and Terra provide replacement strains and quarantine methods.

Lunar activists argue that the successful response proves Luna requires permanent world-level government.

Their demands include:

- a Lunar executive council;
- a Lunar legislature above the provinces;
- authority over food, migration, habitat safety and transport;
- direct control of local taxation;
- representation in Roman interworld policy.

They do not demand independence.

Their slogan is:

> “One Republic, more than one world.”

---

## 118,014 — Rome divides over Lunar autonomy

The autonomy question splits Roman politics.

### Moderate World Unionists

They support a world-based structure.

They argue that true planetary unity must allow each world to govern its own environmental realities.

### Centralist World Unionists

They oppose Lunar autonomy.

They believe stronger central authority is necessary precisely because food systems failed across multiple provinces.

### Roman National Hardliners

They claim Lunar world government will weaken Roman identity and lead eventually to secession.

### Constitutional Pluralists

They argue that Rome cannot govern Luna as if it were merely another group of provinces on Terra.

The World Union movement fractures.

Its extreme nationalist wing loses influence because it cannot reconcile total planetary unity with Lunar self-government.

Moderate world-union support grows substantially.

---

## 118,016 — Independent Terran states protest

The remaining independent Terran states react angrily when Rome begins discussing a formal “Terran world administration.”

Their governments argue that Rome has no right to create an institution called the government of Terra while independent states still exist.

One diplomatic statement declares:

> “Rome governs most Terrans. It does not govern Terra.”

The protest is legally valid.

Rome responds by distinguishing between:

- the Roman Terran administration;
- planetary institutions shared with independent states.

Critics call this wordplay.

The dispute becomes one of the central constitutional problems of the reform.

---

## 118,018 — The Martian Territorial Declaration

The Martian Confederacy formally reasserts sovereignty over:

- the entire Martian surface;
- the Martian atmosphere;
- the subsurface;
- Phobos;
- Deimos;
- all natural orbital space immediately associated with Mars.

The declaration does not challenge Roman activity, because Rome has no permanent Martian installations.

Its purpose is symbolic and preventative.

Mars does not want future Terran expansion to treat empty Martian land as unclaimed.

The Confederacy praises Rome’s willingness to address Lunar self-determination, but adds:

> “Each world must shape its own future according to its own people and conditions.”

Relations remain warm.

Mars is clearly establishing itself as an equal political civilisation, not a distant Roman dependency.

---

## 118,020 — The Titanian Congress convenes

Representatives from the major Titanian cavern states meet through a combination of physical delegations and slow relay communication.

The Congress debates whether Titan should follow Mars and create one planetary government.

Full federation fails.

The reasons include:

- fear of ecological overintegration;
- linguistic and cultural differences;
- mistrust of distant caverns;
- opposition from hereditary technical houses;
- concern that a central government could control heat distribution;
- wariness of becoming politically legible to Rome.

However, nearly every delegation accepts that some planetary coordination is necessary.

---

## 118,022 — The Titanian Accord of Thermal Peace

The Congress signs the **Accord of Thermal Peace**.

It establishes several Titan-wide institutions.

### The Titan External Office

Coordinates communication with other worlds.

### The Planetary Ecological Council

Sets minimum quarantine and biosphere standards.

### The Thermal Trade Commission

Creates basic rules for inter-cavern trade, heat credits and water exchange.

### The Titan Disaster Compact

Requires settlements to provide aid during collapse, famine or cavern failure.

### The Common Archive Assembly

Collects and duplicates historical, scientific and legal knowledge.

### The Scientific Exchange Forum

Coordinates Class100 research.

Titan does not become one state.

It becomes a network of independent states subject to limited common law in specific fields.

---

## 118,024 — Titanian reaction to Roman reform

The prospect of a Roman world-based constitution causes mixed reactions.

Some Titanian leaders approve.

They see it as evidence that Rome can distinguish political unity from direct central control.

Others become more suspicious.

They argue that Rome is learning how to govern multiple worlds and may eventually attempt to incorporate Titan.

The Orenth-Marellon Commonwealth adopts a formal neutrality doctrine:

- no Roman military presence;
- no exclusive Roman technical dependence;
- full participation in Class100 exchange;
- permanent preservation of Titanian political independence.

Anti-Roman political organisations grow in several caverns despite the absence of any Roman threat.

---

## 118,026 — The Roman World Administration Commission

Rome creates a constitutional commission to design a new administrative level above provinces but below the central Republic.

The proposed structure is:

1. Roman Republic central government
2. World governments
3. Provincial governments
4. Municipal governments

The central government would control:

- foreign policy;
- interworld law;
- defence;
- Roman citizenship;
- currency;
- Great Libraries;
- Class100 continuity;
- major space infrastructure;
- constitutional rights.

World governments would control:

- food systems;
- health administration;
- education;
- environment;
- transport;
- housing;
- local economic planning;
- ordinary civil administration.

Provinces would become subordinate to world governments rather than directly to the central Republic.

---

## 118,028 — The Emergency Principle

The draft constitution allows direct central control of provinces only:

- during declared emergencies;
- when a world government has collapsed;
- during initial administration of a new colony;
- when constitutional rights are being systematically violated.

New colonies would remain under temporary central administration until they could form or join a world government.

This clause is designed for future settlement of:

- additional moons;
- orbital habitats;
- Mars-linked territories if ever Roman;
- entirely new worlds.

It is also controversial because the definition of “emergency” could permit central overreach.

---

## 118,030 — The Equalisation Deadlines

Urban overcrowding on Terra becomes politically impossible to ignore.

The largest Roman cities suffer from:

- housing scarcity;
- transport congestion;
- long medical queues;
- water stress;
- high food prices;
- loss of public green space.

The Senate establishes binding equalisation targets.

By **118,075**, every Roman province must reach minimum standards in:

- healthcare access;
- rail connectivity;
- electricity;
- water security;
- education;
- digital and radio communication;
- emergency food capacity;
- housing availability.

By **118,100**, no province may fall below 80% of the Roman median in the major living-standard indicators without entering a mandatory recovery programme.

The deadlines greatly accelerate investment in underpopulated regions.

---

## 118,032 — The Lunar Settlement Expansion Act

Rome authorises its largest Lunar housing and food-production expansion to date.

The programme includes:

- new residential caverns;
- agricultural chambers;
- rotating-gravity nurseries;
- public transport tunnels;
- schools and hospitals;
- Class100 emergency systems;
- civilian launch capacity.

Terran residents of overcrowded cities receive incentives to move to Luna:

- subsidised passage;
- guaranteed housing;
- employment;
- family grants;
- extended civic-income supplements.

The policy is voluntary.

Critics argue that economic pressure makes it only nominally voluntary.

---

## 118,034 — Mandate comparisons

Opposition movements compare Lunar migration incentives to forced demographic policies of the old Eastern Mandate.

The comparison is emotionally powerful but incomplete.

Rome does not:

- forcibly relocate people;
- seize children;
- assign permanent occupations;
- prohibit return.

Still, poorer citizens are more likely to accept the incentives.

Critics argue that Rome is moving its urban poor into a dangerous offworld environment while wealthier people remain on Terra.

The government responds by making the programme open to all income groups and publishing risk data.

The controversy persists.

---

## 118,036 — Restoration of Mare Fecunditatis

Roman Lunar archaeologists identify a large ancient habitat network beneath Mare Fecunditatis.

Its main structures are damaged but unusually extensive.

Rather than restore it solely as an archaeological zone, the government designates it for a new civilian province.

The site contains:

- old transport tunnels;
- water reservoirs;
- pressure chambers;
- industrial foundations;
- buried agricultural space.

Reconstruction begins immediately.

The province is designed around Class100-compatible systems and modern Lunar food-diversity rules.

---

## 118,038 — The Martian Population Charter

Mars adopts a confederal family policy.

It includes:

- universal childcare;
- reproductive-health guarantees;
- larger family housing;
- educational credits per child;
- settlement expansion grants;
- long parental leave;
- priority access to new settlements for large families.

The state does not define childbearing as compulsory.

Public culture increasingly treats raising children as participation in the Martian Dream.

The total fertility rate rises strongly.

The population boom begins visibly.

---

## 118,040 — Martian university reorganisation

The Confederacy reorganises its universities into planetary institutes.

Major fields receive exceptional funding:

- atmospheric retention;
- low-pressure botany;
- cryospheric engineering;
- soil biogenesis;
- planetary hydrology;
- low-energy chemistry;
- radiation-resistant ecology;
- surface construction;
- Class100 computation.

Students in unrelated disciplines are still required to complete a basic Martian ecology sequence.

Even literature and history programmes increasingly engage with the planetary dream.

Critics worry that Mars is subordinating all culture to one national objective.

Supporters argue that the dream gives shared meaning without erasing local identity.

---

## 118,043 — The First Martian Exterior Census

The Confederacy conducts a planetary survey of all biological activity outside sealed settlements.

It finds:

- widespread engineered microbes;
- several stable lichen zones;
- seasonal melt ecosystems;
- isolated moss survival under protective films;
- spontaneous microbial adaptation near geothermal vents.

The biosphere is still negligible by Terran standards.

It is larger and more resilient than expected.

The findings cause enormous public optimism.

A popular slogan appears:

> “Mars has already begun.”

---

## 118,045 — The Wound Electronics Breakthrough

Roman researchers near the Wound create a compact switching architecture capable of operating reliably below approximately 45% local IRF intensity.

The device is still unstable elsewhere on Terra, but within the Wound it allows:

- much faster computation;
- more accurate medical imaging;
- better spacecraft guidance;
- advanced simulation;
- improved archival indexing.

Rome restricts production geographically.

Other provinces accuse the Wound region of becoming a state-supported technological aristocracy.

The government begins funding remote access to Wound computation rather than distributing the hardware itself.

This creates the first large-scale Roman “computational service” economy.

---

## 118,047 — The Wound accident

A high-energy turbine test exceeds containment limits during an unexpected local IRF fluctuation.

The turbine disintegrates.

Fragments destroy a neighbouring laboratory.

More than 600 researchers and workers die.

The accident demonstrates that lower IRF intensity does not eliminate instability.

Roman regulators suspend several experiments.

The World Union movement’s industrial wing accuses the Continuity Authorities of timidity.

Public sympathy favours the regulators.

---

## 118,049 — Titan’s Orenth-Marellon population surge

The merged Commonwealth passes **7 million residents**.

Its integrated infrastructure supports:

- lower heat loss;
- more specialised medicine;
- larger schools;
- denser trade;
- new manufacturing districts.

The planned settlement corridor between the original caverns is now continuously inhabited.

It is not one open cavern, but a chain of sealed districts.

The Commonwealth begins describing itself as a **metacavern city**.

Other Titanian states imitate the model cautiously.

## 118,052 — The Mare Fecunditatis migration begins

The first major civilian migration fleet reaches the restored Mare Fecunditatis habitats.

Most settlers come from:

- overcrowded Terran megacities;
- high-cost industrial provinces;
- younger families seeking housing;
- technical workers;
- public-sector employees.

The new settlement initially contains nearly 700,000 people.

Within five years, it exceeds 4 million.

It becomes culturally distinct from older Lunar provinces because most residents are recent Terran migrants rather than established Lunar families.

Tension develops between:

- Lunar-born residents;
- Terran newcomers;
- central administrators;
- local provincialists.

---

## 118,055 — The Sixth Lunar Province

Mare Fecunditatis becomes the sixth Lunar province.

It is the first modern province created primarily through large-scale Terran migration rather than gradual growth of an existing Lunar settlement.

Its official language is Permanent Latin only, though migrant communities retain many local languages culturally.

The province’s rapid creation is praised by:

- expansionists;
- housing advocates;
- equalisation supporters;
- moderate world unionists.

It is condemned by:

- Lunar autonomists;
- anti-relocation activists;
- fiscal conservatives;
- those fearing the approaching IRF burst.

---

## 118,057 — The Burst Preparedness Debate

The scheduled burst is now only fifty-three years away.

At Terra’s projected background intensity, the burst should temporarily raise the planetary average from approximately 50% to 60%.

On Luna, the passing wave will likely produce a smaller but still serious increase later.

Critics argue that rapid Lunar growth before the burst is irresponsible.

Rome responds by requiring every Lunar province to prove it can survive:

- a ten-point IRF increase;
- temporary loss of high-energy transport;
- reduced Terran supply;
- partial electrical failure.

Mare Fecunditatis delays further immigration until its fallback systems pass inspection.

---

## 118,059 — The Constitutional Convention of Worlds

Delegates from Terra and Luna convene to debate the world-level reform.

Mars, Europa and Titan attend through observers but are not part of the Roman constitution.

The central question is whether “world” should be:

- a geographic administrative unit;
- a constitutional community;
- a sovereign component of the Republic;
- merely a higher coordination level.

Lunar delegates demand genuine legislative power.

Terran centralists demand strong national supremacy.

The resulting compromise becomes the **Roman Worlds Amendment**.

---

## 118,061 — The Roman Worlds Amendment

The amendment is approved by referendum across Roman territory.

It creates:

## The Roman Central Republic

Responsible for:

- constitution;
- citizenship;
- defence;
- foreign affairs;
- currency;
- interworld infrastructure;
- major continuity projects;
- rights enforcement.

## The World of Terra

Responsible for Roman provinces on Terra.

It does not claim authority over independent Terran states.

Its formal name is therefore **Roman Terra**, not “Government of Terra.”

## The World of Luna

Responsible for all Lunar provinces and ordinary Lunar territories.

It gains its own:

- world assembly;
- executive council;
- high court;
- budget;
- food authority;
- transport administration;
- environmental and habitat law.

Provinces become subordinate to world governments.

The central government may intervene directly only under the Emergency Principle.

---

## 118,063 — Political consequences

The reform transforms Roman politics.

### Hardline Roman nationalist world-unionists

Collapse as a coherent movement.

Many cannot accept strong Lunar autonomy.

### Moderate world-unionists

Become more powerful.

They argue that a future world government for Terra can exist inside a plural, world-based Roman system.

### Lunar autonomists

Celebrate the reform but continue demanding greater control over migration and taxation.

### Independent Terran states

Remain wary.

They accuse Rome of building a planetary government in stages while formally denying it.

### Mars

Publicly congratulates Rome.

The Martian Confederacy praises the recognition that worlds possess legitimate self-government.

### Titan

Becomes more suspicious.

Several Titanian commentators describe the reform as proof Rome intends eventually to build a multiworld federation.

---

## 118,065 — The Roman Worlds bureaucracy crisis

Reassigning powers creates confusion.

For several years:

- provincial laws conflict with world laws;
- central ministries refuse to surrender authority;
- budgets overlap;
- civil servants do not know which institution controls them;
- courts issue contradictory rulings.

Healthcare payments are briefly delayed in several provinces.

Lunar transport licences are issued under two different systems.

Critics call the reform unnecessary constitutional engineering.

By 118,071, most responsibilities are clarified.

The temporary disorder becomes an important argument against future rapid constitutional expansion.

---

## 118,067 — The Martian Confederacy recognises Roman Luna

Mars signs separate cooperation agreements with:

- the Roman Central Republic;
- the World of Luna.

This is symbolically significant.

Mars treats Luna as a legitimate world-level political entity without treating it as independent from Rome.

The agreement covers:

- food genetics;
- low-gravity medicine;
- relay communication;
- surface ecology;
- emergency support.

Luna begins developing direct diplomatic identity within Roman foreign policy.

---

## 118,069 — The Harbour System life survey

The Harbour civilisation completes its first detailed planetary biosphere survey.

Findings show:

- no atmospheric disequilibrium strongly suggestive of life;
- no complex organic cycles;
- no microbial signatures in sampled soils or oceans;
- no fossil evidence in accessible regions.

Scientists cannot prove total sterility.

They conclude that indigenous life is unlikely.

The future settlement planet is designated **Haven** by popular vote.

Long-term, all life on Haven will derive from the ship’s biosphere.

---

## 118,071 — IRF arrival modelling in Harbour

The Harbour scientists estimate when the expanding IRF shell will reach the system.

They do not know about the weakening inner cavity.

Their model predicts:

- an initial weak precursor phase;
- later arrival of strong IRF waves;
- eventual approach toward full-strength conditions;
- repeated fluctuations caused by pulse structures.

The range of uncertainty spans centuries.

The central estimate gives Harbour roughly **1,800–2,600 years** before serious industrial disruption begins.

The estimate may be wrong, but it is sufficient to shape policy.

---

## 118,073 — The Harbour Transition Plan

The settling faction begins a phased technological strategy.

### Phase One — Maximum Industrial Preparation

Use unrestricted high-energy industry to construct:

- cities;
- mines;
- orbital infrastructure;
- atmosphere-processing systems;
- genetic archives;
- low-energy factories;
- new spacecraft.

### Phase Two — Controlled Descent

Replace critical systems with:

- lower-pressure industry;
- distributed power;
- mechanical backups;
- Class100-like local designs, independently reinvented.

### Phase Three — IRF Survival

Shift essential food, water, medicine and government to systems capable of functioning under strong IRF.

Unlike Rome, Harbour’s settlers embrace intense industrialisation first.

Their caution concerns timing, not restraint.

---

## 118,075 — Haven ecological seeding begins

Settlers introduce the first controlled organisms to Haven:

- microbes;
- algae;
- soil-forming bacteria;
- lichens;
- engineered pioneer plants.

The planet possesses:

- liquid water;
- suitable gravity;
- a modifiable atmosphere;
- large barren continents.

The project resembles Novaterra’s ancient beginnings, but Harbour possesses vastly more advanced technology.

Terraforming proceeds much faster initially.

Scientists deliberately create genetic diversity to prevent monoculture dependence.

The recent Lunar crises are unknown to them, but shipboard overcrowding has taught similar lessons.

---

## 118,077 — The Ship Division Authority

The Harbour Accord establishes a formal authority to build smaller ships.

Three planned fleet types emerge.

### Continuation Ships

Will eventually resume the original outward journey.

### Survey Ships

Will travel toward other candidate systems.

### Return Ships

Will investigate the weakened IRF shell and possibly travel toward Sol.

Construction begins using Harbour asteroids.

No ship is completed during this century.

The original Fleeing Ship will remain the largest vessel and eventually continue its initial mission.

---

## 118,079 — The Martian Birth Wave

Mars records its highest birth rate since before the full return of the IRF.

The population is growing at more than twice the earlier projected rate.

Benefits include:

- larger labour pools;
- more settlement founders;
- greater cultural confidence.

Problems also appear:

- crowded schools;
- food demand;
- housing shortages;
- pressure on medical systems;
- demand for new habitats.

The Confederacy accelerates restoration of abandoned settlements.

The Martian Dream is now not only ideological.

It is demographically reshaping the planet.

---

## 118,081 — The First Great Martian Valley Plan

Mars selects a large valley region for a centuries-long transformation programme.

The plan calls for:

- partial roofing in early stages;
- connected greenhouse districts;
- soil creation;
- water channels;
- microbial surface zones;
- gradual atmospheric exposure;
- eventual removal of internal barriers if conditions permit.

The project is expected to support millions.

Its ultimate aspiration is an inhabited landscape rather than a sealed city.

Construction begins with tunnels, reservoirs and soil-processing stations.

---

## 118,083 — The Terra Equalisation Push

Roman Terra accelerates infrastructure expansion to meet the 118,100 deadline.

Massive construction occurs in poorer provinces:

- hospitals;
- schools;
- railways;
- housing;
- water systems;
- local energy;
- food-storage networks.

The programme reduces inequality significantly.

It also causes:

- labour shortages;
- material inflation;
- construction corruption;
- environmental damage in some regions.

Several deadlines are met only statistically, through temporary services or incomplete projects.

The government creates audit teams to distinguish real equality from bureaucratic compliance.

---

## 118,085 — Overcrowding persists

Despite migration and equalisation, Terra’s largest cities remain severely overcrowded.

Reasons include:

- continued population growth;
- economic concentration;
- cultural attraction;
- Wound-region technological employment;
- reluctance to leave established communities.

Lunar migration relieves pressure but does not reverse megacity growth.

Urban density reaches dangerous levels in some regions.

Rome begins planning vertical and underground housing districts with strict resilience standards.

The problem will continue into the burst century.

---

## 118,087 — Titan’s anti-Roman cultural movement

A Titanian cultural movement called **Keep the Caverns Ours** spreads through several federations.

It argues that:

- Permanent Latin is displacing Titanian external languages;
- Roman standards are becoming default Solar standards;
- Class100 exchange favours Roman classification;
- the Roman Worlds Amendment reveals expansionist ambition.

The movement does not reject technical cooperation.

It demands:

- Titanian control of shared archives;
- multilingual external communication;
- no automatic adoption of Roman certification;
- legal guarantees against foreign political claims.

The Titan External Office adopts stronger cultural-protection rules.

Relations remain formally good but emotionally cooler.

---

## 118,089 — Europa’s response

Europa reacts differently.

Many Europan communities welcome the world-based Roman reform because it resembles their own preference for layered autonomy.

The Oceanic Assembly studies whether Europa should develop stronger world-level institutions.

No full government is proposed.

Several communities fear that even the Assembly is becoming too centralised.

Europa remains committed to voluntary coordination.

---

## 118,091 — The Mare Fecunditatis cultural clash

Recent Terran migrants in the new Lunar province clash politically with established Lunar-born communities.

Terran migrants favour:

- closer central Roman oversight;
- faster industrial expansion;
- more immigration.

Lunar-born residents favour:

- stricter carrying-capacity limits;
- greater world autonomy;
- slower development.

The provincial election produces a divided assembly.

A coalition eventually forms around:

- continued immigration at reduced rates;
- mandatory Lunar environmental training;
- stronger local food systems;
- recognition of Lunar identity in education.

The province begins developing its own blended culture.

---

## 118,093 — The Roman Burst Simulations

Rome performs civilisation-wide simulations of the expected 118,110 burst.

The projected consequences include:

- failure of some conditional electronics;
- reduced aviation reliability;
- disruption of orbital launches;
- lower industrial output;
- communication slowdown;
- pressure on Lunar supply;
- temporary decline in Wound-region advanced computing.

Class100 systems should remain unaffected.

The simulations reveal that several overcrowded Terran cities rely too heavily on conditional transport and water management.

Emergency retrofits begin.

Not all will be completed in time.

---

## 118,095 — Mars prepares for the burst

The resonance wave will reach Mars later and weaker than Terra, but Mars begins preparations.

The Confederacy mandates:

- full Class100 inspection;
- food reserve expansion;
- mechanical communication fallback;
- reduced dependence on newly enabled medium-energy systems;
- isolation of experimental terraforming equipment.

Martians view the burst as the first major test of their united government.

The national mood is confident rather than fearful.

The slogan becomes:

> “We survived the full field. We can survive a wave.”

---

## 118,097 — The World of Luna’s first budget

Luna passes its first independent world budget.

Priorities include:

- food diversity;
- habitat expansion;
- civilian transport;
- burst resilience;
- rotating-gravity healthcare;
- Lunar Great Library construction;
- local scientific institutions.

The Roman central government retains defence and interworld transport authority, but Luna now determines most ordinary public spending.

The reform is widely considered successful.

Calls for full independence remain marginal.

---

## 118,099 — Final Harbour split agreements

The Harbour factions reach preliminary agreements.

- Settlers will remain on Haven and nearby moons.
- Returners will receive resources to build vessels aimed back toward the IRF shell.
- Survey factions will launch toward other systems.
- The original ship will eventually resume its outward route after repairs and resupply.

No extensions will be added to the original vessel.

Its population will be reduced through voluntary resettlement before departure.

This resolves the immediate overcrowding problem structurally rather than through permanent reproductive suppression.

# State of each world in 118,100

## Terra

- Population: approximately 21.5–22.5 billion
- Roman share: roughly 97%
- IRF: approximately 50.1%
- Life expectancy: 110–121 years in prosperous provinces; 94–110 years in poorer regions
- Living standards: high, with reduced regional inequality but worsening megacity crowding
- Government: Roman Central Republic above the World of Roman Terra
- Main political divide: moderate planetary federalism versus stronger central world government
- Main risk: overcrowded cities and conditional infrastructure facing the 118,110 burst

### What the new IRF level unlocks

At around 50%, Terra can increasingly support:

- compact low-density electronic systems;
- better reusable orbital stages;
- more advanced medical scanners;
- faster computing;
- improved synthetic materials;
- higher-pressure industry in controlled regions.

Rome still requires low-energy backups and restricts uncontrolled deployment.

---

## Luna

- Population: approximately 230–280 million
- IRF: 52–56%
- Life expectancy: 96–110 years
- Government: World of Luna within the Roman Republic
- Provinces: six, including migrant-founded Mare Fecunditatis
- Living standards: high, but housing and food capacity remain under strain
- Main achievement: first independent world-level Lunar policy and formal self-government
- Main risk: rapid population growth shortly before the IRF burst

### What the new IRF level unlocks

Luna gains:

- more reliable launch systems;
- denser local computation;
- improved closed-loop life support;
- better electric transport;
- more effective local manufacturing.

Luna still cannot operate safely without deep redundancy and Terran trade.

---

## Mars

- Population: approximately 11–14 million
- IRF: 68–74%
- Life expectancy: 89–104 years
- Government: unified Martian Confederacy
- Living standards: secure and improving, though less materially rich than Terra or Luna
- National identity: centred on terraformation and the Martian Dream
- Main trend: sustained population boom
- Main risk: growing faster than habitats and food systems can safely support

### What the new IRF level unlocks

Mars can now use more reliably:

- medium-power generators;
- stronger radio;
- larger chemical-processing systems;
- faster surface transport;
- improved electromechanical computation;
- more capable greenhouse systems.

It still cannot support high-energy planetary engineering. Terraforming remains mostly biological, hydrological and infrastructural preparation.

---

## Europa

- Population: approximately 1.1–1.7 million
- IRF: 90–97%
- Life expectancy: 76–96 years
- Government: Oceanic Assembly and autonomous communities
- Living standards: stable but highly dependent on local vent ecology
- Main development: cautious Blue Vent growth
- Main risk: geological isolation and ecological cascade failure

### What the new IRF level unlocks

Very little changes technologically.

Marginal improvements appear in:

- low-power acoustic communication;
- small electrochemical systems;
- mechanical monitoring;
- surface transmitters.

Europa remains fundamentally a Class100 civilisation.

---

## Titan

- Population: approximately 85–105 million
- IRF: effectively 100%
- Life expectancy: 92–111 years
- Government: independent cavern states linked by Titan-wide institutions
- Largest polity: Orenth-Marellon Commonwealth
- Living standards: stable and increasingly sophisticated
- Main political trend: cooperation without planetary unification
- Main concern: Roman cultural and institutional expansion

### What the new IRF level unlocks

Nothing new physically, because Titan remains at full intensity.

Progress comes from:

- better Class100 design;
- shared heat systems;
- improved ecological compartmentalisation;
- stronger common institutions;
- more efficient trade.

---

## Novaterra

- Population: approximately 1.0–1.3 billion
- IRF: 100%
- States: roughly 23–26
- Life expectancy: 76–94 years
- Mature green land: approximately 52–60%
- Living standards: good in established ecological zones, difficult on terraforming frontiers
- Main political trend: stronger Concord authority amid continued state multiplication

### What the new IRF level unlocks

Nothing technologically.

Novaterra remains limited to full-IRF systems:

- wind and water power;
- low-voltage electricity;
- mechanical rail;
- hydraulic computation;
- advanced ecological management.

Its societies still intend to industrialise aggressively if the field ever weakens.

---

## Harbour System

- Population: approximately 2.7–3.0 million across the original ship and early settlements
- IRF: 0%
- Life expectancy: 112–130 years
- Living standards: high-energy industrial, though socially strained
- Main developments: Haven terraforming, fleet construction and political division into future branches
- Main risk: miscalculating IRF arrival and overdependence on industrial infrastructure

### What the absence of IRF unlocks

Harbour can use:

- high-density electronics;
- advanced AI;
- fusion and high-output power;
- rapid manufacturing;
- large-scale atmospheric engineering;
- advanced spacecraft construction;
- powerful genetic design.

The settlers are using these capabilities as aggressively as possible before the expected arrival of the field.

# The century’s meaning

Mars unifies around a dream of changing its planet.

Luna proves it can govern itself without leaving Rome.

Titan proves global cooperation does not require global government.

Rome proves that one republic can contain more than one world—but also discovers that doing so makes every argument about unity more complicated.

The Harbour civilisation, meanwhile, prepares to become several civilisations.

By **118,100**, human political development is moving in two opposite directions at once:

- larger structures are forming to manage worlds;
- smaller branches are splitting off to choose their own futures.

The next resonance burst will test all of them.

Not only their machinery, but the political systems they have built around it.

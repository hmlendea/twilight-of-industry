# 27140-27160 — The End of the Unification War

## 27,140

Terra’s average IRF intensity is approximately **59.7%** of its original pre-industrial maximum.

Its slow decline continues at the expected long-term rate.

No periodic burst is due until year **28,110**, so the war occurs during a comparatively smooth phase of weakening.

The field’s geometry remains layered.

## Terra

Approximately **59–60%**, with the lowest values around the Wound of Return.

In parts of the impact region, local intensity is closer to **52–55%**.

This permits experimental machinery there that remains unreliable elsewhere.

## Near-Terran space

Approximately **62–66%**.

Orbital machinery is possible but fragile.

## The Moon

Approximately **68–73%**.

Its ancient machinery remains dormant or intermittent. Some low-energy automatic systems occasionally reactivate.

## Mars

Approximately **88–93%**, depending on solar and orbital conditions.

The weakening interior has reached Mars only weakly.

## Asteroid belt

Approximately **94–98%**.

## Jupiter and Europa

Approximately **98–100%**.

## Saturn and Titan

Effectively **100%**.

## Interstellar space beyond the weakened cavity

Full-strength IRF remains dominant.

The overall field continues expanding beyond Sol while thinning near its origin.

---

## 1. How the IRF affects wartime Terra

The IRF shapes every military operation.

It prevents any power from relying entirely on high-energy systems.

A modern army must operate simultaneously in several technological modes.

A Roman division may use:
- motorised transport on reliable roads
- steam railways for strategic movement
- animal carts near unstable fronts
- radio for command
- wired telegraph when radio fails
- signal towers when electricity becomes unreliable
- powered pumps
- hand-operated pumps
- industrial artillery
- mechanically simple fallback weapons

The field also varies slightly with:
- location
- weather
- solar activity
- geology
- machine configuration
- local electromagnetic conditions

An engine that works in one province may fail in another.

A rocket tested successfully near the Wound may become unstable after being transported inland.

This makes standardisation difficult and creates strong advantages for engineers who understand local IRF behaviour.

## 2. Mars in year 27,140

Mars survives with approximately **40,000–100,000 people**.

Its population is no longer falling rapidly.

The Martian Covenant has stabilised relations among the principal settlements.

There are perhaps thirty major inhabited complexes and dozens of smaller refuges.

## Environment

Mars remains unbreathable.

Its atmosphere is thin, cold and chemically hostile.

Human life is concentrated in:
- lava tubes
- geothermal caverns
- buried valley settlements
- enclosed agricultural basins
- ancient water-processing chambers

Outside the settlements, engineered microbes survive in:
- brines
- subsurface cracks
- shaded soils
- volcanic regions
- protected ice layers

Mars possesses a real but extremely sparse biosphere.

## Daily life

Every Martian adult has environmental duties.

A person may be a teacher, physician or artist, but also serves scheduled shifts in:
- water inspection
- air regulation
- crop monitoring
- seal maintenance
- emergency response

Housing is compact.

Food is repetitive but reliable.

Meals commonly include:
- fungal protein
- algae
- tubers
- dwarf grains
- fish from enclosed reservoirs
- carefully rationed fruit

Water is continuously recycled.

Waste is treated as a resource.

## Society

The Covenant does not form a central state.

Each settlement governs itself, but all accept common survival law.

The most serious crimes are those threatening shared environmental systems.

Military conflict between settlements has nearly disappeared.

Political disputes concern:
- reproduction quotas
- distribution of biological strains
- maintenance obligations
- access to geothermal resources
- relations with Terra

## Technology

Mars remains under such a strong IRF that its technology is less powerful than Terra’s wartime technology.

It uses:
- geothermal heat
- mechanical pumps
- low-voltage electricity
- pressure-balanced fluid systems
- biological air recycling
- optical and radio communication
- electromechanical computation
- mechanically simple surface vehicles

High-energy rocketry is impossible.

Mars cannot send ships or supplies to Terra.

It can send knowledge.

---

## 3. Mars’s view of the Unification War

Martian radio observatories receive fragments of Terran transmissions.

They hear:
- military orders
- political speeches
- refugee appeals
- launch reports
- propaganda broadcasts
- peace proposals

To Martians, the conflict is incomprehensible in scale.

Their entire planetary population is smaller than some Terran armies.

Many Martians believe Terra is risking the only naturally habitable human world over political systems that will seem temporary in a thousand years.

The Martian Covenant repeatedly transmits appeals for restraint.

Most never reach their intended recipients.

Those that do are often used selectively as propaganda.

Rome presents Mars as supporting republican peace.

The Mandate claims Mars warned against Roman expansion.

The Atlantic League edits Martian messages into arguments for international government.

The complete Martian position is simpler:

> Terra must survive, regardless of which side governs it.

## 4. Titan in year 27,140

Titan remains the largest stable offworld human civilisation.

Its population is approximately **13–18 million**.

The IRF remains effectively at full strength, so Titan receives no technological benefit from Terra’s weakening field.

Yet Titan does not require such relief to survive.

## Environment

The surface remains:
- extremely cold
- dark
- covered in hydrocarbon dunes
- crossed by methane rivers
- dotted with methane and ethane lakes

Human civilisation exists below ground.

Large cavern networks surround:
- geothermal reservoirs
- ammonia-water aquifers
- thermal fractures
- ancient heat-storage complexes

Some underground biospheres have been stable for more than ten thousand years.

## Ecology

Titan’s caverns contain:
- fungal forests
- algae ponds
- low-light crops
- artificial wetlands
- fish
- insects
- small mammals
- dense microbial soils

Different caverns have evolved distinct ecologies.

Some are warm and humid.

Others are cool, dry and dominated by fungi.

Biological exchange between caverns is carefully controlled to prevent disease.

## Daily life

Heat is the central economic resource.

Every household receives a thermal allocation.

Public disputes often concern:
- heat access
- water circulation
- cavern expansion
- ecosystem contamination
- tunnel construction

Most Titanians never visit the surface.

Those who do wear insulated pressure clothing and travel in slow mechanical vehicles.

## Technology

Titan uses:
- geothermal circulation
- wind machines on the surface
- chemical heat storage
- low-output electrical grids
- mechanical atmosphere separators
- methane chemistry
- relay computation
- insulated rail systems

Its technology is stable, conservative and designed for repair over centuries.

## Society

Titan is divided into cavern republics, thermal federations and hereditary engineering houses.

Hereditary power persists in some regions, unlike on later Roman Terra.

Politics is slow.

Major decisions may be debated for decades.

The Titanian ideal is not expansion.

It is continuity.

---

## 5. Titan’s view of Terra

Terra’s war is known only through delayed and incomplete radio.

Some Titanian states view it as proof that rapid industrialisation destabilises civilisation.

Others believe Rome represents the best hope of restoring contact with the outer worlds.

A minority argues that Titan should deliberately remain isolated even if Terra regains interplanetary travel.

They fear Terra would bring:
- political domination
- military conflict
- ecological contamination
- dependence on high-energy systems

Titan sends no intervention force.

It cannot.

Its most significant contribution is a series of long technical transmissions describing:
- ecological resilience
- distributed food systems
- low-energy urban heating
- multigenerational infrastructure planning

Roman planners later incorporate some of these ideas into postwar reconstruction.

---

## 6. Europa in year 27,140

Europa is inhabited by approximately **30,000–190,000 people**.

The population estimate remains uncertain because only a handful of communities can transmit through the ice.

## Environment

Europans live beneath kilometres of ice in a global ocean.

Their settlements cluster around:
- hydrothermal vents
- warm mineral currents
- artificial caverns in the lower ice
- ancient submerged structures

## Ecology

Their civilisation relies upon:
- chemosynthetic bacteria
- algae
- fungi
- vent organisms
- aquatic livestock
- engineered filter species
- microbial nutrient cycles

Some Europan ecosystems are now only partly artificial.

Species introduced by humans have evolved into complex local food webs.

## Daily life

Many Europans spend most of their active lives in water.

Dry chambers are precious and used for:
- archives
- surgery
- infancy
- delicate manufacturing
- ceremonial gatherings

Their settlements are dim.

Lighting is biological, chemical or low-voltage.

Travel occurs through water tunnels and current corridors.

## Technology

Europa uses:
- hydrothermal turbines
- ceramic pressure systems
- electrochemical cells
- mechanical pumps
- corrosion-resistant alloys
- biological filtration
- acoustic communication

Its communities possess little capacity for space observation.

Surface radio facilities are rare and temporary.

## Society

Europan communities are small and highly local.

Some are governed by:
- ecological councils
- lineage assemblies
- technical custodians
- elected vent stewards

Because geological changes can destroy an entire settlement, migration between vent regions is culturally important.

Europans think in terms of moving ecosystems rather than fixed borders.

---

## 7. Europa’s intervention

When Europa receives fragments of Terran wartime communication, it sends a small number of messages.

One becomes famous:

> “A sealed world cannot afford civil war. Neither can an open one.”

The message is repeatedly broadcast by Terran anti-war groups.

Rome tolerates its use.

The Mandate attempts to suppress it.

The Atlantic League places it on recruitment posters while continuing military operations.

Europa cannot determine whether its warning has any effect.

---

## 8. The surviving extrasolar colony

At least one extrasolar human civilisation survives on a naturally habitable planet.

By year 27,140, its population is likely between **20 and 50 million**.

The world possesses:
- breathable air
- surface oceans
- stable sunlight
- fertile land
- a mixed native and imported biosphere

The full-strength IRF has ended its industrial era.

Its civilisation now resembles a scientifically sophisticated low-energy world.

## Daily life

People live in river cities, agricultural towns and coastal republics.

They use:
- water power
- wind power
- low-voltage electricity
- sailing vessels
- mechanical rail
- electromechanical computation
- advanced biological knowledge

They preserve records of Sol but cannot communicate with it.

The ancestral Solar System is treated as a distant historical origin.

Some believe Terra survived.

Others believe the Second Expansion destroyed every Solar population.

---

## 9. The endless colony ship

At least one ship continues racing beyond the expanding IRF frontier.

Its location is unknown to Terra.

The ship still possesses industrial capabilities because it remains outside the strongest field.

Its society has become a permanent mobile civilisation.

## Environment

The ship contains:
- rotating habitation cylinders
- artificial forests
- agricultural chambers
- water reservoirs
- manufacturing compartments
- propulsion structures
- repair systems

## Population

Its population likely numbers in the hundreds of thousands or low millions.

## Daily life

Most inhabitants never see open sky.

They experience:
- artificial gravity
- controlled seasons
- scheduled daylight
- carefully managed births
- mandatory technical education

## Society

The old distinction between crew and passengers has disappeared.

Political factions debate whether the vessel should:
- continue fleeing forever
- seek a habitable world
- divide into multiple ships
- turn toward another galaxy region
- eventually investigate the IRF

During this period, the ship has not yet noticed the field’s weakening near Sol.

---

## 10. The Moon in year 27,140

The Moon remains uninhabited.

Its IRF intensity is lower than during the Closing Sky, but still too high for easy industrial operations.

Ancient lunar ruins contain:
- sealed archives
- abandoned factories
- dormant habitats
- genetic banks
- frozen agricultural caverns
- inactive computers
- spacecraft components

Some low-energy systems reactivate intermittently as the field weakens.

Terran observers detect brief signals from old lunar beacons.

Each signal intensifies the space race.

Every power believes the first successful lunar expedition may recover transformational knowledge.

---

## 11. The Roman–League rupture

The fragile relationship between Rome and the Atlantic League finally collapses in **27,141**.

The immediate cause is the **Massilian Convoy Incident**.

A League fleet carrying weapons and industrial materials attempts to enter a port administered by a Roman-associated government.

Rome claims the cargo is intended for separatists and League-backed militias.

Roman authorities order the ships to submit to inspection.

The League refuses.

A Roman patrol attempts to block the harbour.

A collision occurs.

Then firing begins.

Within hours:
- four League vessels are sunk
- two Roman coastal batteries are destroyed
- the port burns
- thousands are dead

Both sides accuse the other of deliberate aggression.

---

## 12. Rome and the League enter formal war

On **9 March 27,141**, the Atlantic League declares that Rome has violated the freedom of the seas.

Rome responds by declaring the League an active participant in the attempted partition of the Republic.

The two powers enter open war.

The conflict is now fully multipolar.

Rome fights:
- the Eastern Mandate
- the Atlantic League
- provincial separatists
- Mandate-backed revolutionary movements
- League-supported commercial armies

The Mandate and League are not genuine allies.

They coordinate attacks against Rome when convenient.

They continue competing elsewhere.

---

## 13. The neutral states are pulled in

The formal Roman–League war destroys what remains of neutrality.

Trade routes become military corridors.

Neutral ports are forced to choose which fleets may enter.

Neutral railway systems are used to move armies.

Neutral states face embargoes from multiple sides.

The Neutral Survival Bloc attempts to maintain collective independence.

It fails gradually.

Some members join Rome because:
- Roman integration zones have stabilised nearby regions
- they fear League commercial control
- they fear Mandate invasion
- Rome offers military protection

Others join the League to preserve maritime access.

A smaller number accept Mandate defence treaties.

Several are invaded before choosing.

By year **27,144**, almost every recognised Terran state is formally involved in the war.

The Unification War becomes a true world war.

---

## 14. Total war

Civilian and military economies merge.

Every major power imposes some combination of:
- conscription
- food rationing
- labour direction
- industrial quotas
- censorship
- travel restrictions
- strategic relocation
- compulsory bond purchases
- agricultural requisitions

Cities are reorganised for war.

Schools teach emergency repair and civil defence.

Hospitals prepare underground wards.

Railway schedules are controlled by military authorities.

Private factories receive compulsory contracts.

Even small workshops produce:
- uniforms
- radio components
- medical tools
- weapons parts
- pumps
- field stoves

The distinction between civilian production and military production nearly disappears.

---

## 15. The League’s early successes

The Atlantic League possesses enormous maritime advantages.

Its fleets seize:
- Roman island bases
- overseas ports
- commercial enclaves
- strategic canals
- fuel-storage sites

League blockades disrupt Roman access to imported minerals.

Roman coastal cities experience shortages.

Insurance networks allow the League to redirect trade quickly.

For a time, it appears Rome may be strangled economically.

The League also supports uprisings in several Roman provinces.

Its propaganda promises:
- local sovereignty
- free trade
- relief from Roman taxes
- an end to Latin requirements

Some populations respond enthusiastically.

---

## 16. Rome adapts

Rome cannot defeat the League at sea immediately.

It responds by turning inward.

The Republic expands:
- continental rail transport
- river shipping
- domestic mining
- synthetic substitutes
- recycling
- low-energy local manufacturing

Integration provinces again become essential.

Their distributed factories reduce Roman dependence on exposed coastal industry.

Formerly poor inland regions become major wartime production centres.

Rome’s industrial system becomes less efficient but more resilient.

This is the second time the integration gamble prevents collapse.

---

## 17. The Mandate’s desperation

The Eastern Mandate is in worsening condition.

Its early centralised advantages are eroding.

Internally it faces:
- food shortages
- industrial exhaustion
- conscription resistance
- local revolts
- corruption
- collapsing trust
- factional conflict within the military
- growing disbelief in official propaganda

Its political structure was built around control.

Total war requires more control than the system can still provide.

Regional administrators begin ignoring central orders.

Some conceal food.

Some falsify production.

Some negotiate secretly with Rome.

The Mandate leadership concludes that a prolonged defensive war will destroy it.

It begins pursuing increasingly dangerous strategies.

---

## 18. The world fronts, 27,142–27,145

The war spreads across every inhabited Terran region.

## Continental fronts

Rome and the Mandate trade territory repeatedly.

## Maritime fronts

Rome and the League fight for ports, islands and trade routes.

## Colonial and associated territories

Local wars combine independence movements with great-power intervention.

## Air war

Airships and limited aircraft attack railways and supply depots.

## Rocket war

Long-range missiles strike industrial centres.

## Orbital war

Satellites are jammed, intercepted and destroyed.

## Internal fronts

Strikes, revolts and anti-war movements force governments to deploy troops at home.

No side can concentrate fully against another.

Every advance creates a new vulnerability elsewhere.

---

## 19. The detection of the asteroid

On **18 August 27,145**, observatories in the Pacific Cavity region detect a previously unknown asteroid approaching the inner Solar System.

It is initially designated **27145-Q**, but the public soon calls it **Tertius**—the Third.

Early observations suggest:
- diameter between 12 and 20 kilometres
- uncertain composition
- highly eccentric orbit
- possible close approach to Terra
- insufficient data to exclude impact

The first preliminary impact probability is estimated at between **6% and 11%**.

The estimate is highly uncertain.

The public hears only that another large asteroid may strike Terra.

Panic erupts.

---

## 20. The trauma of Tertius

Humanity has not forgotten Nemesis.

Every culture possesses stories of:
- the Second Breaking
- the Wound of Return
- the return of the IRF
- planetary famine
- lunar extinction
- the collapse of space civilisation

The possibility of a third impact produces immediate psychological shock.

Across Terra:
- markets collapse
- soldiers desert
- factories close
- people flee coastal cities
- religious movements grow
- food is hoarded
- transport systems are overwhelmed
- families attempt to reach ancient shelters
- rumours spread that the asteroid carries field-memory crystals

Some people believe Tertius will restore the IRF to full strength.

Others believe it will destroy Terra completely.

There is no evidence for either claim.

Fear does not wait for evidence.

---

## 21. The anti-war explosion

Anti-war movements already exist.

Tertius transforms them into a mass planetary force.

Their argument is devastatingly simple:

> Humanity may have only years left, and it is spending them killing itself.

Strikes spread through:
- factories
- ports
- rail systems
- universities
- military depots
- launch centres

Soldiers form peace committees.

Religious authorities demand ceasefires.

Refugee organisations organise marches involving millions.

The Europan message—“A sealed world cannot afford civil war. Neither can an open one”—becomes the movement’s central slogan.

Governments attempt arrests and censorship.

The movements continue growing.

---

## 22. Initial military reactions

The major powers respond differently.

## Rome

The Senate suspends several offensives and orders all observatories to share asteroid data.

## Atlantic League

The League proposes an international tracking authority and temporary maritime ceasefire.

## Eastern Mandate

The Mandate claims the crisis is exaggerated by Rome to halt its military defeats.

It initially refuses broad cooperation.

## Neutral and minor powers

Many demand immediate peace talks.

Several threaten to withdraw from alliance obligations.

The war begins slowing, though it does not stop.

---

## 23. Mars intervenes

Mars cannot launch a spacecraft.

It can contribute observations.

Martian astronomers have an excellent viewing geometry from another planet.

The Covenant redirects nearly all long-range instruments toward Tertius.

Martian settlements sacrifice scarce electrical capacity to transmit positional measurements to Terra.

The data arrives weakly and intermittently.

It is nevertheless invaluable.

Martian calculations show that several early Terran observations were distorted by atmospheric and wartime instrumentation errors.

Mars estimates that the impact probability is lower than initially believed.

Its first reliable estimate is approximately **1.4%**.

This is still frightening.

But it is far below the original figure.

The Martian Covenant transmits:

> “Do not treat low probability as safety. Do not treat uncertainty as permission for war.”

The message is broadcast across Terra.

---

## 24. The asteroid truth emerges

Over the next eighteen months, more observations refine the orbit.

The probability falls:
- from approximately 8% in early reports
- to 1.4% after Martian data
- to 0.6%
- then to 0.18%
- finally to approximately 0.07%

Tertius is now expected to pass Terra at a significant distance.

A later gravitational encounter could alter its orbit, but no immediate impact is likely.

Spectroscopy also suggests it is mostly ordinary silicate and metal.

No evidence of field-memory crystal is found.

The relief is enormous.

It is not complete.

One chance in roughly fourteen hundred remains psychologically intolerable to many people.

The asteroid also proves that Terra still has no reliable planetary defence.

---

## 25. The peace movement survives the reassessment

Governments expect anti-war pressure to fade once the impact probability falls.

It does not.

The asteroid scare has broken the emotional logic of total war.

People have been reminded that Terra itself is fragile.

The movement’s argument changes:

> Even if Tertius misses, another asteroid will come. A divided Terra cannot protect itself.

The movement demands:
- an immediate armistice
- shared asteroid tracking
- demilitarisation of space
- restoration of civilian government
- international planetary defence
- release of political prisoners
- food and medicine corridors

Rome’s public increasingly supports negotiation.

---

## 26. Rome gives way

By **27,148**, Roman industrial production is falling because of strikes and exhaustion.

Several legions refuse offensive orders but agree to defend Roman territory.

Provincial assemblies demand an end to the war.

The Senate narrowly votes to authorise peace negotiations.

Rome proposes:
- ceasefire along existing fronts
- withdrawal from recently occupied territories
- international supervision of disputed provinces
- recognition of some existing integration treaties
- gradual autonomy reviews
- an asteroid-monitoring compact
- prohibition of attacks on orbital scientific systems

The Atlantic League responds positively.

It is financially exhausted and facing uprisings in its treaty territories.

The Mandate rejects the proposal.

---

## 27. The League–Roman ceasefire discussions

Rome and the League begin separate talks in **27,149**.

Both sides agree in principle to:
- restore maritime trade
- exchange prisoners
- end attacks on scientific satellites
- permit food shipments
- establish neutral shipping corridors
- postpone lunar claims

Their largest disagreements concern:
- occupied ports
- commercial concessions
- sovereignty of League-backed states
- Roman customs policy

The talks are difficult but genuine.

For the first time since 27,141, peace between Rome and the League appears possible.

---

## 28. The Mandate appears to reconsider

The Mandate’s internal position is collapsing.

Several regional military commands are close to mutiny.

Food riots spread.

Its leadership cannot sustain the war indefinitely.

In late **27,149**, the Mandate unexpectedly announces willingness to negotiate.

It agrees to attend a general peace conference in the neutral city of **Novara Alba**.

The announcement produces celebrations across Terra.

Fighting declines.

Local truces appear spontaneously.

Some soldiers cross front lines to exchange food and letters.

Observers begin speaking of the end of the Unification War.

---

## 29. The Novara negotiations

The peace conference opens in early **27,150**.

The proposed treaty includes:
- immediate ceasefire
- phased troop withdrawals
- prisoner exchange
- supervised referenda in disputed regions
- recognition of civilian relief corridors
- limits on strategic rockets
- restoration of neutral trade
- joint asteroid defence research
- international administration of the Wound’s launch zones
- renewed scientific contact with Mars and Europa

Rome makes significant concessions.

It agrees to review several forced integrations.

The League agrees to abandon exclusive commercial claims in some states.

The Mandate appears prepared to withdraw from occupied Roman provinces.

A signing date is announced.

---

## 30. The Mandate’s ruse

The Mandate never intended to sign.

The peace process is used to:
- rest exhausted troops
- reposition armies
- move ammunition forward
- infiltrate Roman transport networks
- identify Roman defensive weaknesses
- encourage demobilisation
- delay League attacks

Three days before the scheduled signing, Mandate forces launch the largest offensive of the war.

The operation begins before dawn on **22 July 27,150**.

Roman positions expecting peace are caught unprepared.

---

## 31. The Great Betrayal Offensive

Mandate armies attack across seven major fronts.

They use:
- concentrated artillery
- long-range rockets
- infiltrated sabotage teams
- armoured rail formations
- chemical agents in limited sectors
- airborne troops from large airships
- local collaborators
- false Roman radio orders

Several Roman defensive lines collapse.

Mandate forces advance hundreds of kilometres in some regions.

Three provincial capitals fall.

Roman casualties are immense.

The Mandate announces that the Republic is finished.

---

## 32. The psychological effect

The betrayal destroys what remains of diplomatic trust.

Across Terra, peace celebrations turn into fury.

Roman anti-war groups divide.

Some continue demanding negotiations.

Many conclude that Rome has no choice but to defend itself.

Soldiers who had refused offensive operations return to duty.

Strikes in Roman arms factories end voluntarily.

Provincial assemblies vote emergency levies.

The Mandate has achieved a major territorial surprise.

Politically, it has rescued Rome.

The war is now widely seen inside the Republic as defensive.

---

## 33. The Atlantic League withdraws from peace

The League is shocked by the Mandate attack.

But it also withdraws from the peace process.

League leaders argue that no treaty can be trusted while the strategic balance is changing.

Some League factions favour joining Rome against the Mandate.

Others see an opportunity to weaken both continental powers.

The League resumes mobilisation.

Formal Roman–League peace negotiations collapse.

However, direct League attacks on Rome remain limited for several months.

The League begins drifting toward an undeclared anti-Mandate alignment.

---

## 34. Smaller states choose sides

The betrayal transforms global diplomacy.

Many smaller states had distrusted Roman expansion.

They distrust the Mandate more.

Governments fear that any Mandate peace guarantee is worthless.

Most remaining independent states enter Roman-aligned coalitions.

They provide:
- troops
- rail access
- food
- intelligence
- ports
- medical units

A smaller number align with the League.

Very few join the Mandate voluntarily.

The Mandate’s diplomatic isolation becomes nearly complete.

---

## 35. The Mandate’s internal collapse accelerates

The offensive consumes its remaining reserves.

Mandate leaders expect a rapid Roman collapse.

When that does not occur, the consequences are catastrophic.

Internally:
- food distributions fail
- provincial administrators defect
- railway workers sabotage military transport
- conscripts desert
- factories operate without spare parts
- rival security agencies arrest one another
- military commanders ignore civilian orders

The Mandate can no longer retreat politically.

Its leadership believes defeat means:
- revolution
- execution
- territorial disintegration
- loss of personal power

War becomes its only perceived path to survival.

This makes it more dangerous.

---

## 36. Rome halts the offensive

Rome’s first response is defensive.

Provincial legions slow the Mandate advance using fallback logistics.

Bridges are destroyed.

Railways are rerouted.

Local populations evacuate food stocks.

Roman engineers deliberately shut down industrial systems before capture.

The Mandate advances into increasingly empty territory.

Its supply lines lengthen.

Roman armies regroup.

By early **27,151**, the offensive has stalled.

The Mandate has gained land but not destroyed Rome.

---

## 37. The impact-crater strategy

The Mandate leadership decides that conventional victory is no longer sufficient.

It must gain a decisive technological advantage.

Its target becomes the **Wound of Return**.

The impact region offers:
- the weakest IRF conditions on Terra
- the best rocket facilities
- the most advanced field laboratories
- access to ancient impact material
- experimental high-energy machinery
- control over the renewed space race

Mandate strategists believe capturing the Wound could allow them to:
- dominate orbit
- develop superior rockets
- threaten the Moon
- recover ancient lunar technology
- build weapons impossible elsewhere
- manipulate the IRF itself

The plan is enormously risky.

---

## 38. The invasion of the Pacific Cavity

In **27,152**, Mandate-aligned fleets and expeditionary armies attack the Pacific Cavity states.

Several local governments are already weakened by decades of conflict.

Some collaborate.

Others resist fiercely.

Mandate forces seize:
- launch islands
- IRF observatories
- coastal laboratories
- industrial ports

They move toward the Wound’s central scientific zone.

The invasion shocks every major power.

Even states hostile to Rome fear Mandate control of the impact point.

---

## 39. The third space race

The invasion re-ignites the space race.

The Mandate announces that it will launch a lunar expedition from the captured Cavity.

Rome restores emergency funding to the Officium Caeleste.

The Atlantic League remobilises its surviving launch corporations.

Pacific resistance governments race to evacuate scientists and equipment.

For the first time, lunar competition is directly linked to battlefield control of the Wound.

## Mandate objective

Launch a crewed orbital mission and then attempt a lunar flyby.

## Roman objective

Prevent Mandate monopoly and restore the Cavity.

## League objective

Seize independent launch zones and recover commercial leadership.

The Moon again becomes a strategic prize.

---

## 40. Dangerous IRF experiments

Mandate scientists are ordered to study captured field-memory remnants.

The leadership demands a method to:
- suppress the IRF locally
- redirect it
- generate controlled pulses
- weaponise resonance

Most scientists warn that the models are incomplete.

The Mandate proceeds anyway.

Several experiments cause:
- local power failures
- destructive mechanical resonance
- unexplained magnetic events
- temporary increases in IRF intensity
- structural collapses

No stable weapon is created.

But rumours spread that the Mandate may trigger another global burst.

The asteroid panic merges with fear of deliberate IRF reactivation.

---

## 41. Mars’s strongest intervention

Mars detects unusual emissions from the Wound.

The Martian Covenant transmits an emergency message using a large portion of its available electrical reserve.

It warns that uncontrolled manipulation of impact crystal could potentially create:
- local resonance waves
- unpredictable field reinforcement
- geological instability
- interference with the natural depletion pattern

Mars cannot prove that a planetary catastrophe will occur.

But it cannot exclude it.

The message is broadcast worldwide.

It strengthens the coalition against the Mandate.

Martian settlements then suffer months of power rationing because of the energy spent on transmission.

---

## 42. The League changes sides in practice

The Atlantic League does not sign a formal alliance with Rome immediately.

Instead, it begins attacking Mandate shipping and Cavity supply routes.

League fleets justify this as protecting international access to the Wound.

Rome and the League establish limited operational coordination.

They exchange:
- naval intelligence
- weather data
- orbital tracking
- minefield maps

They remain legally at war.

Their forces avoid fighting each other where possible.

The arrangement is deeply unstable but strategically decisive.

---

## 43. The Battle of the Wound

The decisive Pacific campaign begins in **27,153**.

Roman, League and Pacific resistance forces attack Mandate positions from different directions.

The battle is fought across:
- volcanic islands
- floating industrial platforms
- launch complexes
- ocean trenches
- underground laboratories
- ruined pre-Breaking facilities

IRF conditions vary dramatically across the region.

Machines behave unpredictably.

Some advanced weapons work brilliantly.

Others fail without warning.

Low-energy infantry, sailing supply craft and manually controlled artillery remain essential.

---

## 44. The Mandate lunar launch

During the battle, the Mandate attempts a crewed orbital launch.

The mission is intended as both propaganda and strategic demonstration.

A three-person spacecraft successfully reaches orbit.

It is the first Mandate crewed orbital mission in years.

The crew is ordered to prepare for a possible lunar injection.

The required upper stage has not been fully tested.

Before it can launch, Roman and League attacks damage the ground-control network.

The spacecraft remains in orbit for nine days.

Its crew eventually returns in a dangerous uncontrolled descent.

Two survive.

The Moon remains unreached.

---

## 45. The destruction of the central launch fields

Neither Rome nor the League is willing to allow the Mandate to retain the Cavity launch complexes.

When recapture appears uncertain, League aircraft and Roman rockets attack the largest facilities.

The destruction is immense.

Decades of launch infrastructure are lost.

Hangars, fuel plants, guidance laboratories and test towers burn.

The military objective succeeds.

The space race is set back by generations.

The decision remains controversial after the war.

Some historians call it necessary.

Others call it the deliberate destruction of humanity’s road back to the Moon.

---

## 46. The Mandate retreats from the Wound

By late **27,153**, Mandate forces cannot hold the impact region.

Their supply routes are cut.

Local resistance sabotages water and transport.

Roman armies approach from the west.

League fleets dominate the sea.

The Mandate withdraws, destroying laboratories and carrying away equipment.

Some field-memory samples disappear during the retreat.

Their later fate remains unknown.

The Cavity is liberated but devastated.

---

## 47. The Roman–League armistice

After the Cavity campaign, Rome and the League can no longer sustain the fiction of normal war between them.

In **27,154**, they sign a limited armistice.

It does not resolve:
- territorial claims
- commercial disputes
- occupied ports
- responsibility for earlier attacks

It does establish:
- cessation of direct combat
- joint anti-Mandate operations
- shared asteroid monitoring
- limited orbital coordination
- protected relief corridors

The League does not become Roman.

It becomes Rome’s temporary co-belligerent.

---

## 48. The Mandate’s final mobilisation

The Mandate declares a state of total national emergency.

All remaining resources are directed to war.

Measures include:
- universal conscription
- seizure of food stocks
- forced factory labour
- execution for desertion
- closure of universities
- conversion of hospitals to military use
- compulsory relocation from contested regions

The system’s control structures cannot enforce all these orders consistently.

Some regions obey.

Others revolt.

The Mandate becomes simultaneously more brutal and less governable.

---

## 49. Revolutions inside the Mandate

During **27,154–27,156**, uprisings spread.

They include:
- workers’ councils
- regional independence movements
- military mutinies
- republican groups
- food riots
- ethnic and linguistic rebellions
- rival Mandate factions

Some rebels ask Rome for aid.

Others reject all foreign intervention.

The central government responds with mass arrests and collective punishment.

This pushes more communities into revolt.

The Mandate is now fighting Rome, the League, local states and itself.

---

## 50. Rome’s final strategy

Rome abandons the idea of one decisive offensive.

Instead, it pursues controlled disintegration.

Roman strategy aims to:
- isolate Mandate armies
- support local defections
- preserve food infrastructure
- capture rail junctions
- offer surrender terms
- avoid destroying regions likely to join the Roman system later

The Republic broadcasts guarantees:
- no collective punishment
- food assistance
- abolition of hereditary and Mandate political privileges
- gradual citizenship
- local cultural protection
- reconstruction aid

The promises are not always honoured perfectly.

They are attractive enough to accelerate defections.

---

## 51. The Great Defection

In **27,156**, three major Mandate field armies refuse orders to attack civilian rebel regions.

Their commanders negotiate with Rome.

More than a million soldiers surrender or change sides over several months.

Rome does not disarm all of them.

Some are reorganised into provisional security forces under mixed command.

This is risky.

It prevents total administrative collapse in the surrendered regions.

The Mandate leadership calls the defectors traitors and orders their families arrested.

The order triggers further mutinies.

---

## 52. The Atlantic advance

The League attacks Mandate-controlled coastal regions.

Its forces capture:
- ports
- naval bases
- shipyards
- trade cities

The League intends to retain several of them after the war.

Rome objects but postpones the dispute.

Local governments form rapidly.

Some favour League membership.

Others favour Rome.

Others demand independence.

The postwar map is already becoming a source of future tension.

---

## 53. The fall of the Mandate capital

By **27,157**, Roman forces approach the Mandate’s central political region.

The capital suffers:
- food shortages
- factional fighting
- electricity failures
- mass desertion
- civil-service collapse

The Mandate leadership attempts to relocate.

Parts of the security apparatus arrest one another.

A final attempt is made to launch strategic rockets against Roman cities.

Several launch crews refuse.

Others fire.

The attacks kill tens of thousands but do not alter the war.

Roman and rebel forces enter the capital in early **27,158**.

The central Mandate government ceases functioning.

---

## 54. The war continues after the capital falls

The Mandate’s collapse does not immediately end the war.

Regional commanders continue fighting.

Some establish successor regimes.

Some possess chemical weapons and long-range rockets.

Others fear Roman occupation.

League and Roman armies race to secure territory.

Local civil wars erupt.

The final two years consist of:
- sieges
- negotiations
- disarmament campaigns
- relief operations
- suppression of warlords
- border disputes
- political settlements

The world war becomes a collection of regional endings.

---

## 56. The Treaty of Carthago Nova

The formal end of the Unification War comes with the **Treaty of Carthago Nova**, signed on **11 November 27,159**.

The treaty is signed by:
- the Roman Republic
- the Atlantic League
- recognised Mandate successor governments
- most surviving independent states
- representatives of Roman integration provinces
- major neutral relief organisations

Its principal terms include:
- a general ceasefire
- dissolution of the Eastern Mandate
- disarmament of surviving Mandate strategic forces
- prohibition of unsupervised IRF experiments
- international registration and Roman-secured containment of known impact-crystal samples
- restoration of global asteroid observation
- demilitarisation of scientific satellites
- release and exchange of prisoners
- refugee return and resettlement
- war-crimes tribunals
- negotiated settlement of League–Roman maritime claims
- temporary international administration of a limited number of disputed territories

The territorial settlement is overwhelmingly favourable to Rome.

The Roman Republic retains:
- every integration province that remained under Roman authority
- every pre-war Roman province and associated territory
- territories incorporated through wartime accession treaties
- strategically important regions whose governments defected from the Mandate
- vast areas formerly governed directly by the Eastern Mandate

These newly acquired lands include:
- major agricultural basins
- industrial cities
- mineral regions
- railway corridors
- former Mandate administrative centres
- large stretches of the Mandate’s western and central territories

Rome does not annex the entire former Mandate.

Several peripheral regions become independent successor states, while others remain under temporary reconstruction administrations.

Even so, Rome emerges from the war controlling more territory than at any earlier point in its restored history.

The Treaty does not yet create a single planetary state.

It makes the Roman Republic the dominant land power on Terra and the centre of the postwar order.

---

## 57. The cost of the war

The Unification War lasts approximately forty years.

Its final two decades are the most destructive.

The total death toll is far greater than early postwar estimates suggested.

Modern Roman demographic studies place total excess mortality between approximately **430 million and 620 million people**.

Some estimates rise above **700 million** when long-term famine, reduced birth rates and delayed medical deaths are included.

Deaths result from:
- direct combat
- strategic rocket attacks
- chemical weapons
- sieges
- famine
- infectious disease
- forced labour
- infrastructure collapse
- mass displacement
- exposure during migration
- destruction of water and sanitation systems
- political purges
- reprisals and massacres
- interrupted medicine
- environmental contamination

More than a billion people are displaced at least once during the wider conflict.

Hundreds of millions never return to their original homes.

Some regions lose a quarter of their population.

Several heavily contested territories lose more than half.

Entire generations are marked by:
- missing family members
- physical disability
- malnutrition
- psychological trauma
- disrupted education
- destroyed local institutions

The war does not end simply because armies stop moving.

Its demographic and social consequences continue for centuries.

---

## 58. Rome is victorious—but transformed

Rome emerges as the strongest surviving power on Terra.

Its victory rests upon:
- the integration provinces
- distributed wartime industry
- provincial soldiers
- resilient low-energy logistics
- the allegiance of smaller states
- Mandate defections
- temporary cooperation with the Atlantic League
- Rome’s ability to continue functioning after repeated military disasters

The Republic now controls an enormous and deeply varied territory.

It includes:
- the old Roman core
- long-integrated provinces
- recently admitted poor states
- former allied republics
- former monarchies
- wartime emergency territories
- liberated Mandate regions
- conquered Mandate provinces
- devastated frontier districts

The pre-war constitutional structure cannot govern this territory effectively.

Roman law differs between regions.

Citizens possess unequal rights.

Some provinces remain under military administration.

Some still operate under integration treaties.

Former Mandate regions possess entirely different legal and bureaucratic traditions.

The Republic has won the war.

Its institutions are close to breaking under the weight of victory.

---

## 59. The refounding of the Roman Republic

The Roman Republic is **not abolished**.

Instead, it is reformed from the ground up.

In year **27,160**, the Senate, Popular Assembly, provincial congresses, veterans’ organisations, labour federations, reconstruction councils and representatives from newly acquired Mandate territories convene the **Great Constitutional Convention of Roma**.

The Convention declares that the Republic founded in 25,742 remains the same legal state.

However, its old constitution is repealed and replaced in full.

This process becomes known as the **Second Founding of the Roman Republic**.

The official name remains:

# The Roman Republic

## *Res Publica Romana*

There is no successor state and no break in legal continuity.

Rome’s treaties, debts, citizenship obligations and international responsibilities remain valid.

But almost every major internal institution is redesigned.

The refounded Republic presents itself as:
- the continuation of ancient republican Rome
- the continuation of the restored Republic
- the political heir of Terra’s ancient unified civilisation
- the guardian of the knowledge of the Great Release
- the foundation of a future reunited Terra

---

## 60. The new territorial principle

The refounded Republic abolishes the old hierarchy of:
- autonomous provinces
- associated internal republics
- integration territories
- client kingdoms
- hereditary protectorates
- unequal provincial categories

All Roman territory becomes part of one indivisible republic.

Administrative provinces continue to exist, but they are not sovereign.

They possess elected governments responsible for regional administration, but all provincial institutions are subordinate to the national constitution and national law.

Provinces cannot:
- maintain separate armed forces
- issue independent citizenship
- negotiate foreign treaties
- impose internal customs barriers
- reject national civil rights
- establish incompatible criminal or commercial codes
- prevent lawful movement by Roman citizens

The integration states and newly annexed Mandate regions are placed on the same eventual legal footing as the oldest Roman provinces.

Transitional administrations remain in some devastated regions, but their separate status is explicitly temporary.

---

## 61. One legal system

The new constitution orders the creation of a single Roman legal code.

It standardises:
- civil rights
- criminal law
- labour law
- commercial law
- land ownership
- environmental protection
- elections
- public administration
- citizenship
- taxation
- military authority
- education
- public health

Regional customs may continue in private, cultural and ceremonial matters.

They cannot override:
- individual rights
- equality before the law
- national citizenship
- democratic participation
- freedom of movement
- abolition of hereditary authority

Reconciling thousands of regional laws takes decades.

During the transition, mixed courts apply both temporary provincial rules and the developing national code.

---

## 62. Language reform

Latin Nova becomes the sole official language of the Roman Republic at the national level.

It is required for:
- national legislation
- higher courts
- the armed forces
- national administration
- engineering standards
- interprovincial commerce
- higher education
- national scientific work

Each province may designate one secondary official language.

That language may be used in:
- provincial institutions
- local courts
- primary education
- public signage
- regional media
- cultural administration

Other languages remain legal in private life, religion, literature and local cultural activity.

The Republic funds archives and cultural institutions to preserve languages that do not receive official provincial status.

The policy is intended to create administrative unity without erasing cultural identity.

It remains one of the most controversial reforms.

---

## 63. Universal standardisation

The Republic begins a vast standardisation programme covering:
- numbers
- mathematical notation
- alphabets and transliteration
- measurement
- railway gauges
- electrical systems
- radio frequencies
- ammunition
- military uniforms
- medical records
- pharmaceuticals
- educational qualifications
- school curricula
- industrial components
- building codes
- water standards
- disaster-response systems
- administrative records

The war demonstrated the cost of incompatibility.

Allied railcars could not use neighbouring tracks.

Medical fittings failed to connect.

Ammunition could not be shared.

Measurement differences caused construction failures.

The new government treats standardisation as a condition of survival and national unity.

---

## 64. Universal abolition of hereditary rule

The rewritten constitution abolishes hereditary public authority throughout the Republic.

This applies to:
- monarchies
- hereditary governorships
- hereditary legislative seats
- dynastic military command
- aristocratic judicial privileges
- inherited public offices

Former rulers may retain private property under ordinary law.

They receive no automatic political authority.

Some become elected officials or ceremonial cultural figures.

Others resist and lead short-lived revolts.

The Republic suppresses those revolts and makes abolition of hereditary rule an irreversible constitutional principle.

---

## 65. Ending military rule

The war has placed much of Roman society under military control.

The new constitution promises to restore civilian life.

The demilitarisation programme includes:
- returning railways to civilian management
- ending military control of food distribution
- closing military courts for civilians
- removing internal checkpoints
- ending wartime travel permits
- reducing conscription
- placing intelligence services under legislative oversight
- prohibiting active officers from holding civilian executive office
- restoring independent newspapers and political organisations

Millions of soldiers must be demobilised.

Many provinces depend economically on military factories.

Some commanders resist surrendering power.

The transition is therefore gradual, but civilian supremacy becomes a central constitutional principle.

---

## 66. The redesigned republican government

The refounded Roman Republic retains republican institutions but restructures them.

### National Assembly

Elected according to population through universal adult suffrage.

It initiates taxation, budgets and ordinary legislation.

### Senate

Represents provinces and long-term national institutions.

Its old aristocratic and corporate privileges are abolished.

### Executive Consulate

A collegial civilian executive replaces the excessive wartime concentration of power in generals and emergency consuls.

### Constitutional Court

Enforces the new constitution and protects national civil rights.

### Civic Tribunal

Investigates corruption, abuse of office and unlawful emergency measures.

### Generational Chamber

Reviews policies with long-term ecological, technological and IRF consequences.

### Independent Electoral Commission

Standardises elections throughout the Republic and supervises voting in newly incorporated territories.

The objective is to preserve strong central coordination without allowing the Republic to become another Mandate.

---

## 67. Regional equality

The Republic officially concludes that extreme regional inequality was one of the principal causes of:
- mass migration
- radicalisation
- political collapse
- foreign intervention
- the Unification War

It creates the **Terran Equalisation Programme**.

Less-developed regions receive priority for:
- railways
- electricity
- irrigation
- hospitals
- schools
- housing
- communication networks
- industry
- environmental recovery
- disaster resilience

Former integration provinces remain major beneficiaries.

So do the newly annexed Mandate territories, many of which have been devastated by fighting and deliberate destruction.

The Republic’s goal is not merely charity.

It seeks to prevent any region from becoming so poor that its population must migrate en masse to survive.

---

## 68. Integration debts

The old integration treaties created enormous long-term debts.

The refounded Republic restructures them.

Measures include:
- cancelling debts tied to factories destroyed in the war
- lowering interest rates
- extending repayment periods
- linking payments to regional prosperity
- converting some debts into national public ownership
- counting military service and wartime requisitions as partial repayment
- cancelling obligations in exchange for ecological restoration

Since the former integration provinces are now ordinary Roman territory, the debts are increasingly treated as internal national accounts rather than obligations between separate states.

This reduces resentment but imposes immense costs on the central treasury.

---

## 69. Land reform

The wartime system of granting industrial land without payment is ended.

New infrastructure projects require:
- public review
- environmental assessment
- fair compensation
- relocation guarantees
- local consultation
- approval under national planning law

Some old land grants remain valid.

Others are cancelled because they resulted from corruption or coercion.

Large strategic industries are converted into:
- national public corporations
- provincial-public partnerships
- regulated private enterprises
- worker-owned industrial associations

The reforms weaken the industrial families that gained extraordinary power during the integration period and war.

---

## 70. The former Mandate lands

Rome’s newly acquired eastern territories are enormous.

They contain tens or hundreds of millions of inhabitants, depending on the final borders.

Their condition varies greatly.

Some cities retain advanced industry.

Others have been ruined.

Some rural regions welcome Roman administration.

Others regard Rome as an occupying power.

The Republic divides these territories into transitional provinces.

Each receives:
- emergency food
- reconstruction engineers
- temporary security forces
- elected local councils
- Latin Nova schools
- legal-integration commissions
- public-health programmes

Former Mandate officials are screened individually.

Technical administrators who did not participate in major crimes are often retained.

Security and political officials associated with atrocities face prosecution.

Rome cannot replace the entire administrative class without causing collapse.

---

## 71. Rebuilding the Wound of Return

The impact region is placed under direct republican scientific administration.

The government prohibits private or provincial control over field-memory material.

Its priorities include:
- recovering missing samples
- repairing observatories
- monitoring the IRF
- rebuilding asteroid-detection systems
- investigating Mandate experiments
- cleaning polluted research zones
- preserving surviving launch infrastructure

The Wound is treated as both:
- a scientific site
- a national security zone
- part of humanity’s shared heritage

Military access is sharply restricted after demobilisation.

---

## 72. The postwar space programme

The Republic does not immediately resume the race to place humans on the Moon.

The public associates the old lunar programmes with:
- military rivalry
- sabotage
- wasted resources
- the destruction of the Cavity
- wartime propaganda

Instead, Rome creates the **Terran Astronomical and Planetary Defence Service**.

Its priorities are:
- detecting asteroids
- tracking Tertius
- restoring scientific satellites
- exchanging observations with Mars
- developing interception theory
- mapping orbital debris
- preserving lunar knowledge for a future mission

The Moon remains a national aspiration.

It is no longer an immediate prestige race.

---

## 73. The Atlantic League

The Atlantic League survives as an independent political and commercial bloc.

It is weaker than before the war.

Several member states leave.

Its financial institutions are heavily indebted.

Its overseas treaty system has partially collapsed.

The peace settlement requires the League to accept:
- reduced commercial monopolies
- limits on private armies
- common asteroid monitoring
- standard maritime law
- prisoner exchange
- recognition of most Roman territorial gains

Some League states begin discussing eventual Roman membership.

Others rebuild the League specifically to balance Roman power.

The war ends.

The geopolitical rivalry continues.

---

## 74. Independent former Mandate states

Rome does not annex every part of the former Mandate.

Several distant or culturally distinct regions become independent republics.

Their treaties require:
- disarmament of strategic weapons
- democratic constitutional processes
- access for humanitarian relief
- cooperation on asteroid monitoring
- prohibition of renewed Mandate institutions

Some remain closely aligned with Rome.

Others pursue neutrality.

A few move toward the Atlantic League.

Most are too exhausted to challenge Roman dominance in the near future.

---

## 75. Mars after the war

Mars survives with approximately **40,000–110,000 people**.

The asteroid crisis gives it renewed importance.

Martian observations helped reduce the estimated probability of impact and discouraged renewed panic.

The Roman Republic creates a permanent Martian communications office.

Mars remains technologically constrained by the stronger local IRF.

Its daily life continues to depend upon:
- geothermal heat
- biological air cycling
- mechanical pumps
- water recycling
- strict maintenance
- controlled population growth

Mars cannot participate in Roman reconstruction physically.

It becomes an intellectual and astronomical partner.

---

## 76. Europa after the war

Europa continues beneath the Jovian ice.

Its population remains uncertain but is believed to be stable.

Rome formally recognises the Europans as a distinct surviving branch of humanity.

The Republic also incorporates the Europan warning about technological dependence into its national resilience doctrine.

Communication remains sporadic.

No physical contact is possible.

---

## 77. Titan after the war

Titan remains materially unaffected by Roman victory.

Its caverns continue operating under nearly full IRF intensity.

Titanian societies observe the creation of a more centralised Roman Republic with caution.

Some see a stable unified Terra as the best chance for future contact.

Others fear that Terra’s restored power will eventually become expansionist.

Titan remains independent, stable and distant.

---

## 78. Extrasolar humanity

The known Solar civilisations still cannot communicate with humanity’s surviving extrasolar branches.

At least one naturally habitable colony continues developing under full-strength IRF conditions.

At least one colony ship continues fleeing the expanding frontier.

Neither knows that Rome has won the Unification War or restructured the Republic.

---

## 79. The IRF in year 27,160

Terra’s average IRF intensity has declined to approximately **59.5%**.

The Mandate’s experiments created local disturbances but did not change the projected depletion curve.

The IRF continues:
- weakening around Terra
- remaining significantly stronger around Mars
- remaining near full strength around Jupiter and Saturn
- expanding through interstellar space
- thinning as it stretches

The next scheduled resonance burst remains due in year **28,110**.

The constitutional reforms place all impact-crystal research under national supervision to prevent another power from manipulating the field recklessly.

---

## 80. Terra in year 27,160

Terra is devastated but alive.

Its environment bears the scars of total war:
- polluted rivers
- deforested regions
- abandoned farms
- ruined cities
- damaged railways
- contaminated industrial districts
- orbital debris
- chemically poisoned battlefields

The natural biosphere remains resilient.

Agriculture can recover.

Forests can return.

Water systems can be repaired.

The war has not destroyed Terra’s fundamental habitability.

The Republic begins converting wartime industry toward:
- housing
- medicine
- rail repair
- irrigation
- schools
- civilian machinery
- ecological restoration

Daily life remains difficult.

Rationing continues.

Millions remain homeless.

Veterans struggle to return to civilian society.

But organised reconstruction has begun.

---

## 81. The meaning of Roman victory

Rome wins because it remains functional longer than its rivals.

It does not conquer all of Terra.

But it:
- destroys the Mandate as a unified power
- keeps all pre-war territories
- retains every integration state
- annexes vast former Mandate regions
- leads the postwar legal order
- secures the allegiance of most smaller powers
- survives constitutional crisis

Its victory also forces Rome to abandon the unequal provincial structure through which it expanded.

The Republic becomes larger, more centralised and more legally uniform.

Whether it can remain democratic at such scale becomes the central political question of the next age.

---

## 82. The postwar declaration

At the conclusion of the Great Constitutional Convention, the new Executive Consulate declares:

> Terra shall not again be divided between regions that possess the means of life and regions whose people must abandon their homes to obtain them.

> No Roman shall be a provincial subject before being a citizen.

> No ruler shall inherit public authority.

> No army shall govern the peace it was created to defend.

> No advancement shall erase the systems required when advanced machinery fails.

> And no region of the Republic shall be abandoned because reconstruction is difficult or costly.

The declaration is aspirational.

The Republic lacks the resources to fulfil it quickly.

But it defines the Second Founding.

---

## 83. The final state in 27,160

The Unification War is over.

The Roman Republic remains the Roman Republic, but with a completely rewritten constitution.

It holds:
- all of its pre-war territories
- every integration province
- vast former Mandate lands
- the strongest unified economy and army on Terra

The Atlantic League survives outside it.

Several former Mandate successor states remain independent.

The Moon remains unvisited.

Tertius is expected to miss Terra.

Mars survives.

Europa endures.

Titan remains stable.

Extrasolar humanity continues beyond contact.

Rome has won the largest war in Terran history.

Its next struggle is internal:

to prove that unification, legal uniformity and central power can coexist with democracy, individual freedom and regional cultural life.

# 28450-28500 — Years 28,450–28,500


At the start of **28,450**, Terra is politically united, Luna is a populous Roman world, Mars is industrialising deliberately, Titan is coordinating more closely without becoming one state, and Haven is expanding through increasingly autonomous industry.

Novaterra, by contrast, has ceased to function as a connected civilisation.

The next fifty years intensify all of these trajectories.

Rome turns political unity into administrative and technological standardisation. Terra Restituta starts altering some of the oldest landscapes associated with Rome itself. Terra and Mars start preparing structures for diplomats who may not arrive for centuries.

On Titan, a dispute over heat and water becomes the world’s most serious armed conflict in generations.

On Novaterra, famine gives way to slavery, raider states, cannibal enclaves, fortified borders, and dangerous attempts to rebuild agriculture from nearly nothing.

## 14 February 28,451 — The Universal Suit Programme begins

The Roman Space Agency starts developing the future generation of pressure suit.

Earlier Roman suits descended from several competing traditions:
- military orbital suits;
- Lunar civilian emergency suits;
- archaeological suits;
- Wound experimental suits;
- Martian-derived pressure garments;
- Industrial Age reconstructions.

Although Roman standards had already reduced incompatibility, significant variation remained in:
- helmet locks;
- air connectors;
- electrical fittings;
- glove rings;
- pressure valves;
- emergency seals;
- life-support couplings.

These differences are manageable within one settlement.

They could be fatal during interplanetary contact.

The programme is officially designated the **Universal Extravehicular Interface Standard**, or **UEIS**.

---

## 28,451–28,455 — Designing the Future Roman Pressure Suit

The suit itself is called the **Continuity Extravehicular Suit**.

Its design priorities are:
- long service life;
- field repair;
- mechanical emergency operation;
- compatibility across Roman spacecraft;
- resistance to dust and vacuum;
- modular replacement;
- operation under Class50, Class75, and Class100 support modes.

The suit contains several technological layers.

### Normal operating layer

Uses compact Class50 systems for:
- temperature regulation;
- communication;
- medical monitoring;
- navigation;
- automatic pressure management.

### Degraded operating layer

Uses Class75-compatible:
- basic circulation;
- reserve communication;
- simplified thermal regulation;
- mechanical valve assistance.

### Survival layer

Uses Class100-compatible or entirely mechanical:
- emergency oxygen control;
- manual pressure regulation;
- helmet sealing;
- suit-patch systems;
- basic visual indicators.

The suit cannot provide full modern comfort under complete technological degradation.

It can maintain its wearer alive for sufficient duration to reach shelter.

---

## 28,452 — Mars joins the standardisation talks

Mars is invited to review:
- helmet rings;
- air couplings;
- glove interfaces;
- emergency refill points;
- rescue harnesses;
- suit-to-vehicle connectors;
- universal warning markings.

Martian engineers object to several Roman assumptions.

Roman suits are designed mainly for:
- vacuum;
- Lunar dust;
- orbital debris;
- short surface exposure.

Martian suits must tolerate:
- abrasive electrostatic dust;
- long walks;
- low pressure rather than vacuum;
- wide temperature variation;
- frequent mechanical repair;
- entry into settlements with different pressure levels.

The joint standard is revised with emphasis on shared interfaces rather than one identical suit.

---

## 5 September 28,454 — The Solar Suit Compatibility Accord

Terra, Luna and Mars adopt the **Solar Suit Compatibility Accord**.

Europa and Titan participate symbolically but do not adopt all provisions because their environments require fundamentally different protective equipment.

The accord standardises:
- helmet-to-torso attachment dimensions;
- emergency air couplings;
- suit refill valves;
- rescue-line fittings;
- external tool mounts;
- electrical and low-voltage connectors;
- medical telemetry ports;
- emergency visual symbols;
- pressure ratings and labelling.

Roman and Martian suits may still differ in:
- durability requirements;
- insulation;
- material composition;
- mobility;
- radiation protection;
- internal software;
- service-life rules.

In principle, a Martian helmet can now be attached to a Roman suit body, and Roman emergency air can refill a Martian suit.

Not every mixed combination is comfortable or optimal.

It is designed to be survivable.

---

## 28,455 — Titan and Europa issue limited recognitions

Titan’s planet-wide institutions adopt several universal rescue fittings for external relay crews and surface work.

They reject full Roman dimensional standards as unnecessary for most Titanian life.

Europa adopts shared:
- medical telemetry conventions;
- emergency markings;
- air-system coupling references.

Europan pressure garments remain adapted to aquatic environments and cannot simply exchange components with Roman suits.

The agreement nevertheless creates the first broadly Solar standard for human emergency equipment.

# 28,451 — Equalisation funding rises

The complete unification of Terra creates sharp administrative contrasts.

Some recently acceded provinces possess:
- highly advanced conditional industry;
- wealthy cities;
- strong universities.

Others contain:
- neglected rural districts;
- weakened public health;
- outdated rail;
- unreliable water;
- insufficient Class100 systems.

The World of Terra approves the **Final Equalisation Acceleration Fund**.

The programme prioritises recently acceded provinces for:
- healthcare;
- rail;
- housing;
- water security;
- Class100 food reserves;
- schools;
- Latin-language public administration;
- regional-language preservation;
- disaster infrastructure.

The government promises that no province will remain visibly disadvantaged merely because it joined last.

---

## 28,452–28,460 — The Orassian transition

Former Orassian territory presents a special case.

It possesses excellent science and industry, but much of that industry depends on conditional technology and looser approval processes.

Companies respond in several ways.

Some relocate to:
- the Wound;
- Luna;
- designated experimental districts;
- lower-density industrial regions.

Others redesign products to meet Roman law.

A minority close permanently.

The equalisation programme therefore focuses less on basic infrastructure and more on:
- worker retraining;
- research relocation;
- economic diversification;
- preservation of local scientific culture.

By **28,460**, former Orassian provinces have lost some industrial independence but gained much broader access to Roman public systems.

---

# 28,452 — Terra Restituta revisits Orassian projects

Several Terra Restituta projects had been delayed because they would have affected sovereign Orassian territory.

These included:
- restoration of ancient wetlands;
- redirection of a damaged river;
- extraction of contaminated industrial fill;
- excavation of submerged urban districts;
- re-creation of an ancient coastal buffer zone.

With Orassian accession and the relocation of many industries, the projects can proceed under one legal system.

The World of Terra still requires:
- provincial consultation;
- environmental assessment;
- worker compensation;
- preservation of industrial heritage;
- relocation support.

Political unification eliminates the international veto.

It does not eliminate local rights.

---

## 28,455 — The Western Reclamation approval

The largest formerly blocked project is approved.

It will:
- eliminate obsolete industrial barriers;
- restore river sediment flow;
- rebuild floodplains;
- reclaim selected coastal land;
- leave some modern wetlands intact;
- preserve several major Orassian industrial monuments.

Former Orassian environmental groups strongly support it.

Former industrial workers are more divided.

The project becomes a test of whether Terra Restituta can transform territory without treating living communities as archaeological inconveniences.

---

# 3 April 28,452 — Concordia Orbitalis is renamed

The former multinational station above Terra no longer has foreign Terran member states.

Its name, **Concordia Orbitalis**, remains respected but increasingly sounds like the station belongs to a vanished diplomatic order.

The Roman central government and the World of Terra rename it:

## **Concordia Humana**

The name emphasises that the station is no longer an international Terran compromise.

It is a human platform serving:
- Terra;
- Luna;
- Mars communication;
- asteroid defence;
- Solar science.

Former national modules remain identifiable.

Their legal ownership transfers to the World of Terra or Roman central institutions.

---

## 28,453–28,458 — The Hall of Nations

An additional museum module is added to Concordia Humana.

It is called the **Hall of Nations**.

The module records every former state that contributed to the station.

Displays include:
- original module insignia;
- national flags;
- construction records;
- photographs;
- crew lists;
- scientific contributions;
- diplomatic agreements;
- records of disagreements and accidents.

The museum does not portray former states as obstacles to unity.

It presents them as historical participants whose cooperation made later union possible.

Former national descendants assist in curating their own sections.

This reduces concern that reunification is erasing pre-unification identity.

---

## 28,456 — The Concordia Archive

A minor disaster-resistant archive is constructed at the station’s deepest protected structural point.

It contains:
- the station’s construction history;
- records of every contributor;
- module diagrams;
- major scientific findings;
- crew rolls;
- constitutional transfer documents;
- emergency repair instructions;
- copies of the Solar Suit Compatibility Accord.

Media include:
- metal plates;
- ceramic microtext;
- optical storage;
- magnetic memory;
- printed summaries.

The archive is designed to survive:
- decompression;
- extended power loss;
- partial structural breakup;
- centuries without maintenance.

A passive beacon marks its location.

---

# 28,453 — Novaterra’s raider age deepens

Novaterra’s food collapse has moved beyond national famine.

In many former states, armed groups now survive through seasonal plunder.

Raider war bands target:
- harvests;
- seed stores;
- livestock;
- wells;
- medicine;
- tools;
- skilled people.

Some raiders originate from collapsed armies.

Others are refugee groups that became militarised.

Their organisation varies from minor mounted bands to mobile forces numbering tens of thousands.

They rarely retain large territory permanently.

They move wherever food remains.

Their activity prevents surviving agricultural communities from accumulating reserves.

---

## 28,454 — Cannibal enclaves in the dead cities

In several former metropolitan regions, starving populations become trapped far from productive land.

The most notorious groups emerge in partially abandoned cities.

Cannibalism starts as desperate consumption of the dead.

In some enclaves, it becomes organised violence.

Armed groups kidnap:
- refugees;
- isolated travellers;
- rival residents.

Not every urban survivor participates.

Many city communities barricade themselves against both raiders and cannibal groups.

The reputation of the cities becomes so terrifying that refugees start avoiding valuable urban ruins containing medicine and tools.

This permits violent enclaves to persist longer.

---

# 28,455 — The frontier state of Asheron closes

One of Novaterra’s most stable surviving states, **Asheron**, faces a massive influx of refugees.

Asheron retains:
- functioning agriculture;
- a disciplined government;
- a mountain frontier;
- one of the remaining healthy crop complexes.

Its leaders conclude that admitting all refugees would exceed food capacity and risk introducing the crop disease.

On **8 August 28,455**, Asheron closes its borders.

The government mobilises the army along the entire accessible frontier.

---

## 28,456 — Asheron Initiates Conscription

Asheron introduces emergency conscription.

Service includes:
- border patrol;
- field quarantine;
- agricultural labour;
- road maintenance;
- refugee camp control;
- disease inspection.

The government establishes buffer camps outside the frontier.

Conditions in the camps are harsh.

Refugees receive limited food but are not admitted into the interior without long quarantine and proof of useful skills or family sponsorship.

Asheron’s policy prevents immediate internal collapse.

It causes tens of thousands to die outside its borders.

The state becomes both a refuge of order and a symbol of cruelty.

---

# 28,456 — The Velgor–Lupeth Dispute Starts

On Titan, the cavern states of **Velgor** and **Lupeth** share a deep thermal aquifer.

For generations, an agreement divided:
- heat extraction;
- water access;
- maintenance duties;
- emergency reserves.

Velgor’s population has grown faster.

Lupeth remains smaller but controls the principal regulator chambers.

A recent Algarath survey indicates that the aquifer is replenishing more slowly than assumed.

Velgor demands a larger share based on population.

Lupeth refuses, arguing that:
- it constructed and maintains the regulator system;
- increased extraction could destabilise both caverns;
- Velgor expanded irresponsibly.

---

## 28,458 — The regulator seizure

A Velgoran maintenance convoy enters a shared access tunnel and acquires control of an auxiliary regulator station.

Velgor asserts it is preventing Lupeth from restricting emergency heat.

Lupeth calls it an invasion.

Lupeth seals several water channels.

Velgor responds by halting food exports.

Titan’s Disaster Compact attempts mediation.

Neither side accepts external control of the aquifer.

---

## 28,459 — Armed Conflict Commences

Fighting occurs in pressurised tunnels.

Titanian warfare is constrained by environment.

Combatants avoid:
- explosives near pressure walls;
- uncontrolled fire;
- damage to water and heat lines.

Weapons include:
- low-velocity firearms;
- compressed-gas launchers;
- mechanical crossbows;
- cutting tools;
- armoured tunnel vehicles.

Both sides attempt to capture infrastructure intact.

The conflict kills hundreds in its first months and displaces thousands.

---

## 28,460 — The Lupeth collapse

Velgor possesses:
- four times Lupeth’s population;
- larger food reserves;
- more industry;
- improved tunnel transport.

Lupeth possesses superior local defensive knowledge.

The war lasts longer than expected.

Its turning point comes when a Lupeth commander attempts to flood a contested access tunnel.

The flooding damages an old thermal support layer and cuts heat to part of Lupeth’s own residential district.

The resulting evacuation overwhelms the state.

Velgor offers food and heat in exchange for surrender.

The Lupeth assembly splits.

Military leaders resist.

Civilian councils accept.

---

## 2 February 28,461 — Velgor annexes Lupeth

Velgor acquires complete control.

Lupeth ceases to exist as a sovereign cavern state.

The annexation is recognised reluctantly by most Titanian institutions after Velgor guarantees:
- local language rights;
- no mass expulsion;
- continued food support;
- independent investigation of wartime abuses;
- restoration of heat and water;
- limited municipal self-government.

The conflict kills approximately 870 people.

Far more suffer injury, displacement or cold exposure.

It is Titan’s largest armed conflict in centuries.

---

## 28,461–28,468 — Consequences of the annexation

The war transforms Titanian law.

The Titan-wide institutions gain authority over shared geological systems.

Revised rules state that aquifers, heat reservoirs and tunnels crossing multiple states cannot be controlled entirely by one state.

The **Shared Subsurface Resources Tribunal** is created.

Velgor initially resists but eventually joins to secure legal recognition of the annexation.

Titan remains politically fragmented.

Its common institutions become stronger because fragmentation nearly produced ecological disaster.

---

# 21 April 28,456 — The Italia Restituta proposal

During celebrations of the city of Rome’s traditional founding day, the Planetary Reclamation Authority presents **Italia Restituta**.

The programme aims to recover portions of the Italian Peninsula’s lost geography.

Its long-term objectives include:
- selected restoration of submerged coastlines;
- reconstruction of river systems;
- recovery of ancient agricultural plains;
- excavation of submerged cities;
- restoration of buried Roman districts;
- re-establishment of Long Age stewardship landscapes.

The programme is not intended to recreate one exact historical year.

The peninsula changed repeatedly across all four ages.

The objective is to recover the most historically and ecologically significant lost forms without destroying valuable modern ecosystems.

---

## 28,457–28,463 — Mapping submerged Rome

Deep surveys commence in the vicinity of the city of Rome and its former coastal approaches.

The city has expanded, contracted, shifted, flooded and been reconstructed repeatedly over more than one hundred millennia.

Surveyors identify:
- buried First Age foundations;
- Long Age civic districts;
- Industrial transit tunnels;
- Dark Age refuge structures;
- submerged residential areas;
- ancient ports;
- diplomatic quarters;
- artificial islands;
- collapsed flood barriers.

A large ancient portion of Rome lies beneath sediment and water.

Much of it cannot be safely excavated.

Several districts are sufficiently intact to justify restoration.

---

# 28,457 — The Army of Vask captures the western column

The former state of Vask had collapsed years earlier.

A large portion of its regular army remained intact under General **Tarev Olun**.

The force controls:
- artillery;
- rail equipment;
- ships;
- former national food reserves;
- engineering corps.

It captures a refugee column of approximately 100,000 people.

The refugees are disarmed and divided into labour units.

Families are deliberately separated to reduce resistance.

The army declares them “national dependants under emergency service.”

In practice, they are enslaved.

---

## October 28,457 — The Vask expedition sails

Olun’s army loads:
- remaining grain;
- seed;
- animals;
- machine tools;
- weapons;
- water equipment;
- enslaved refugees

onto a fleet of military and civilian ships.

The expedition sails toward **Kareth Island**, a large barren island far from the worst raider corridors.

The island possesses:
- freshwater in seasonal basins;
- mineral-rich volcanic soil;
- little mature ecology;
- strong winds;
- no substantial population.

Olun intends to establish a militarised agricultural colony beyond the reach of continental collapse.

---

# 28,458–28,463 — The Kareth settlement

The first years are brutal.

The army forces labourers to construct:
- terraces;
- windbreaks;
- reservoirs;
- shelters;
- primitive ports;
- fortified food stores.

Problems emerge immediately:
- soil lacks established microbial communities;
- rainfall is irregular;
- winds strip exposed soil;
- seed disease arrives with some stores;
- enslaved labourers sabotage equipment;
- military officers steal rations.

More than one-third of the captive population dies within five years.

The island avoids total failure because engineers discover sheltered valleys with usable water and comparatively fertile ash.

Modest grain and tuber harvests become possible.

---

## 28,464 — The Kareth mutiny

A group of enslaved engineers and former Vask junior officers seize one fortified valley.

They destroy the main military radio and release thousands of captives.

The army suppresses uprisings elsewhere but cannot retake the valley without destroying essential crops.

Kareth divides into:
- the military coastal regime;
- the liberated interior commune;
- scattered fugitive settlements.

The island survives.

Olun’s planned unified slave colony does not.

By **28,500**, Kareth has become a violent but partially self-sustaining microcosm of Novaterra’s collapse.

---

# 28,458 — Major development at the Great Library of Luna

Construction of the Great Library of Luna has continued slowly for centuries.

By the late 28,450s, it remains incomplete but no longer embryonic.

The dispersed-vault design now includes:
- independent archive cells;
- biological repositories;
- mechanical index systems;
- cultural galleries;
- Class100 technical collections;
- sealed emergency chambers.

A major breakthrough occurs when Lunar engineers adapt Helios Forge bearing designs to the library’s storage and retrieval systems.

The new machinery can move heavy archive containers using very low energy and remain serviceable for centuries.

---

## 28,460 — The First Open Vault

The library inaugurates its first major public section.

It is called the **Vault of Human Continuity**.

It contains:
- the four-age historical sequence;
- Lunar extinction records;
- the history of Mars;
- Class100 designs;
- Roman constitutional records;
- Industrial Age archives;
- the Helios Forge crew record.

The opening changes the library’s character.

It is no longer merely a construction site and emergency archive.

It becomes a functioning public institution.

Visitors arrive from across Luna and Terra.

---

## 28,462 — The Archive Descent Test

Lunar authorities conduct a full loss-of-power simulation.

For thirty days, the library operates only through:
- Class100 systems;
- gravity;
- mechanical transport;
- manual indexing;
- passive climate control;
- low-power lighting.

The library remains functional, though at less than one-tenth normal capacity.

One flaw is discovered: several archive cells depend on powered pressure equalisation during frequent access.

They are redesigned.

The successful test becomes a major validation of Roman continuity doctrine.

---

## 28,466 — The Lunar Duplicate Mandate

The World of Luna requires every essential world-level record to exist in at least three locations:

1. the Great Library of Luna;
2. a separate Lunar provincial vault;
3. a Terran or orbital archive.

Critical records include:
- food genetics;
- habitat designs;
- legal codes;
- medical data;
- pressure-system standards;
- debris tracking;
- suit compatibility standards.

The mandate reduces the risk that the unfinished library becomes a single point of failure.

---

## 28,470 — The Subsurface Archive Lake

Excavation reveals a stable, deeply buried cavern containing ancient ice and naturally regulated temperature.

Instead of draining it entirely, engineers preserve part of the ice as thermal mass.

Archive chambers are constructed encircling it.

The resulting **Archive Lake Complex** becomes the safest section of the Great Library.

Its passive thermal stability dramatically lowers energy needs.

It is expected to preserve physical media for millennia.

---

## 28,474 — A buried Lunar legal archive

Construction crews discover a sealed late Industrial Age legal repository beneath the library’s planned western vaults.

The archive contains:
- final Lunar emergency laws;
- property records;
- evacuation rulings;
- disputes over life-support access;
- records of political breakdown.

Some documents demonstrate that late Lunar authorities legally stripped certain peripheral settlements of access to shared reserves to preserve larger cities.

The decisions condemned thousands to death.

The discovery provokes debate over whether survival law can ever justify deliberate abandonment.

The documents are preserved and publicly studied rather than hidden.

---

# 28,459 — The ancient diplomatic quarter

Archaeologists locate a middle Long Age district whose street plan remains unusually clear.

Archive comparison identifies it as a **legation quarter**—a district where foreign missions maintained permanent residences and offices.

The best-preserved structure appears to have served as the embassy of the **Diutlandic Confederation** to the **Latian Republic**.

At that time:
- Rome was the capital of the Latian Republic;
- Latian was the state language;
- the Diutlandic Confederation was a major neighbouring political and commercial power.

The structure had been renovated many times, but its ceremonial core survives.

---

## 28,460–28,466 — Reconstructing the Diutlandic embassy

Archaeologists and archivists reconstruct:
- room layout;
- public reception spaces;
- gardens;
- water features;
- façade proportions;
- ceremonial entrances;
- diplomatic symbols;
- colour schemes;
- furniture forms;
- heating and ventilation.

The structure’s inscriptions had used two languages.

### Latian

The official language of the Latian Republic.

It combined:
- elements inherited from Ancient Latin;
- Talian structures;
- later Long Age vocabulary.

Talian itself descended distantly from Ancient Latin but had evolved separately during the earlier Talian Republic.

### Diutlandic

Employed for internal diplomatic and cultural inscriptions.

Only fragments survive.

The restoration committee decides that the modern structure will retain reconstructed Latian historical inscriptions while adding modern Latin for active diplomatic use.

---

# 28,461 — Terra Restituta Commences Coastal Works in Italia

The first physical phase of Italia Restituta commences.

Rather than attempting immediate continental-scale reclamation, engineers start with:
- sediment-control barriers;
- wetland rebuilding;
- restoration of ancient river channels;
- archaeological cofferdams;
- selective extraction of post-impact debris.

The work is designed to change coastal behaviour gradually.

Large modern marine ecosystems are mapped and protected.

Some proposed reclamation zones are abandoned when surveys reveal thriving post-impact biodiversity.

---

## 28,467 — The Buried City Excavation Commences

Excavation commences beneath modern Rome.

The work uncovers layered city structures from:
- the First Age;
- the Talian and Latian republics;
- middle and late Long Age Rome;
- Industrial Age redevelopment;
- early Dark Age survival districts.

Some layers cannot be preserved simultaneously.

Archaeologists develop a three-dimensional preservation method:
- selected structures are exposed;
- others remain sealed;
- digital and physical models document extracted material;
- modern structures are supported above excavated spaces.

Rome increasingly becomes a city where multiple ages are visible beneath one another.

---

## 28,471 — The Diutlandic Embassy Restoration Commences

The embassy’s structural stabilisation commences.

Original surviving material is preserved wherever possible.

Missing portions are reconstructed using:
- Long Age masonry methods;
- Roman durability standards;
- historically matched pigments;
- passive ventilation;
- concealed modern pressure and climate controls for future Martian use.

The structure is not rushed.

Its expected completion is many decades away.

The Martian Confederacy transmits suggestions for:
- Martian cultural rooms;
- suit storage;
- low-gravity visitor medicine;
- atmospheric adaptation facilities.

These are incorporated without altering the historic public façade.

---

# 28,462 — Olympus Mons selects a Roman embassy site

Mars identifies a grand structure near the ceremonial capital on Olympus Mons.

The structure originated during the late Industrial Age as a planetary scientific assembly hall.

It survived the Closing Sky because its lower chambers were incorporated into later emergency infrastructure.

Its main hall is vast, reinforced and symbolically important.

The Confederacy designates it as the future **Roman Mission House**.

Once regular physical diplomacy commences, it will become the Embassy of the Roman Republic on Mars.

Until then it serves as:
- an interworld cultural centre;
- a receiver for Roman archives;
- a public display space;
- a Mars Approach Programme museum.

---

## 28,464–28,470 — Martian restoration of the Mission House

Martian architects restore:
- the central hall;
- approach tunnels;
- archival rooms;
- pressurised reception spaces;
- ceremonial galleries.

Decorative work combines:
- Martian settlement symbols;
- terraformation imagery;
- Roman civic motifs;
- representations of Terra, Luna and Mars.

A central empty chamber is reserved for the first physical Roman gift.

Martians expect the plaque to occupy it eventually.

The chamber remains empty throughout this half-century.

---

# 28,463 — The future Martian embassy is proposed

The Senate approves a symbolic long-term designation.

Once regular safe interplanetary travel exists, the restored Diutlandic embassy will become the **Embassy of the Martian Confederacy on Terra**.

Until then, it will function as:
- a museum of diplomacy;
- a centre for Martian studies;
- a public relay hall;
- a venue for delayed Solar conferences.

A ceremonial Martian office is established there, staffed by Terran and Lunar representatives accredited by the Martian Confederacy through remote communication.

It is not legally a full embassy yet because no Martian diplomats can reside there.

---

## 28,465 — Debate over historical authenticity

Some historians oppose repurposing the structure.

They argue that a Diutlandic embassy is advisable to remain a memorial to Diutlandic history.

Others argue that providing it a living diplomatic role restores its true purpose more faithfully than turning it into a static museum.

The compromise preserves:
- a permanent Diutlandic historical wing;
- original and reconstructed Latian features;
- archives describing the ancient embassy;
- a separate modern Martian ceremonial suite.

The structure will present both narratives.

---

# 28,463 — The Martian industrial boom accelerates

Mars’s factories produce sufficient material to expand settlements more rapidly.

Population pressure starts easing in the largest habitats.

Industrial zones create:
- high wages;
- technical schools;
- urban growth;
- pollution;
- regional inequality.

Atmospheric greenhouse emissions are now globally detectable by sensitive instruments but climatically tiny.

Models estimate that continuous expansion at current growth rates might produce measurable planetary warming within several centuries.

Meaningful atmospheric transformation still requires:
- far larger industry;
- lower IRF intensity;
- access to additional volatiles;
- centuries or millennia of accumulation.

---

## 28,466 — Mars establishes the Atmospheric Contribution Ledger

Every industrial facility receives a measured account of:
- useful economic production;
- greenhouse gas release;
- toxic emissions;
- waste recovery;
- energy consumption;
- ecological impact.

Facilities gain tax benefits for gases useful to terraformation.

They face penalties for locally dangerous pollution.

This creates an unusual Martian policy:

Pollution is not judged only by quantity, but by whether it contributes safely to the Martian Dream.

---

## 28,469 — First restored Industrial furnace district

Mars successfully reactivates a large late Industrial Age furnace and materials complex.

Only part of the original system can operate.

High-energy sections remain unusable.

Martian engineers divide the complex into:
- Class65 production zones;
- Class75 fallback control;
- isolated Class100 emergency workshops.

Production exceeds that of recently constructed minor plants.

The success encourages more archive searches.

It also reinforces the danger revealed at Eos: aged infrastructure is valuable only after complete mapping and modern isolation.

---

# 28,468 — The Roman suit enters service

The Continuity Extravehicular Suit becomes standard for:
- Roman orbital crews;
- Lunar surface work;
- asteroid defence;
- deep-space test missions.

The first production version is heavier than previous suits.

It is praised for:
- repairability;
- standard fittings;
- long life;
- safe degraded operation.

Astronauts criticise:
- reduced mobility;
- conservative software;
- bulky mechanical controls.

Later variants improve comfort without changing interfaces.

Mars starts producing compatible helmets, gloves and air systems to its own durability standards.

---

# 28,472 — First cross-world rescue test

A Roman suit body, Martian helmet and Lunar emergency air pack are assembled in a controlled test.

The mixed configuration operates successfully.

Several performance limitations appear:
- cooling is less efficient;
- Martian helmet geometry reduces visibility in Lunar posture;
- software cannot interpret all medical sensors.

The wearer remains safe.

The Accord is judged successful.

The test is broadcast across Terra, Luna and Mars.

It becomes a symbol of future physical cooperation.

---

# 28,473 — Novaterra’s siege of Asheron

Refugee and raider forces gather outside Asheron’s frontier.

Some demand entry.

Others intend to seize food.

The forces are not unified.

Asheron’s army retains fortified mountain routes.

The government starts accepting limited refugee groups selected for:
- agricultural skills;
- medicine;
- engineering;
- family ties.

This reduces some pressure but fuels accusations of treating human survival as labour recruitment.

An attempted mass crossing is repelled.

Thousands die.

Asheron survives and becomes more authoritarian.

---

## 28,476 — Asheron’s National Preservation Regime

Civilian government remains formally intact, but military emergency institutions gain permanent power.

The state requires:
- internal movement permits;
- agricultural service;
- compulsory grain delivery;
- health inspection;
- border labour.

Food remains sufficient.

Life becomes highly controlled.

Asheron’s population starts growing through selective refugee admission, making it one of Novaterra’s few expanding organised states.

---

# 28,475 — Kareth’s first stable harvest

The liberated interior commune on Kareth Island succeeds in producing a stable mixed harvest using:
- tubers;
- hardy grains;
- seaweed;
- fishing;
- imported soil microbes preserved by former refugee agronomists.

The military coastal regime remains larger and more effectively armed.

It depends more heavily on stored reserves and coerced plantation labour.

The commune’s smaller, more diverse food system proves more resilient.

Escaped slaves continue moving inland.

General Olun orders punitive campaigns.

The island’s civil war becomes chronic.

---

## 28,480 — Death of General Olun

Olun dies during a failed assault on the interior valleys.

His senior officers divide the coastal regime.

Several enslaved labour camps revolt.

The military state fragments into competing garrisons.

Kareth does not become peaceful.

It becomes more politically diverse.

By the end of the half-century, the island contains:
- the interior free commune;
- three military port enclaves;
- independent fishing villages;
- mobile armed groups;
- former slave settlements.

The island proves habitable.

It does not become the ordered refuge its founders imagined.

---

# 28,477 — Titan creates the Cavern Arbitration Guard

After Velgor’s annexation of Lupeth, Titanian institutions recognise that tribunals without enforcement may be ignored.

A limited multinational force is created.

Its role is limited to:
- protecting shared regulator systems;
- observing ceasefires;
- escorting inspectors;
- securing disaster corridors.

It cannot invade sovereign cavern states.

Personnel come from multiple states and use Common Titanian.

Many caverns fear this is the inception of a planetary military.

Its powers remain tightly constrained.

---

## 28,482 — Lupeth identity persists

Although politically annexed, Lupeth retains:
- local councils;
- schools;
- language;
- memorial institutions.

A nonviolent movement seeks restoration of sovereignty.

Velgor refuses.

The conflict becomes a long constitutional dispute rather than continued war.

The Titanian Tribunal rules that annexation is legally valid but that Lupeth’s guaranteed autonomy is permanent.

Velgor dislikes the restriction but complies to maintain trade access.

---

# 28,478 — Major Lunar Library construction milestone

The Great Library of Luna completes its first continuous ring of independent archive vaults.

The ring can survive loss of any single:
- tunnel;
- pressure zone;
- power system;
- transport route.

It contains only a minority of the final planned collection.

The structural milestone matters more than completion percentage.

The library now possesses a survivable core.

Even if all future construction stops, a major body of human knowledge would remain protected.

---

## 28,483 — The Children’s Archive

Lunar educators propose that survival archives are advisable to contain not only technical and political records but material understandable to children.

A recent collection includes:
- illustrated histories;
- simple science;
- practical survival instructions;
- stories from every human world;
- basic Latin;
- music notation;
- maps;
- explanations of the four ages.

The purpose is recovery after cultural collapse.

A future society might discover the library without possessing advanced education.

The Children’s Archive becomes one of its most celebrated projects.

---

## 28,488 — The First Solar Duplicate

A complete selected archive of Terra, Luna, Mars, Europa and Titan is deposited in the Lunar Great Library.

It is called the **First Solar Duplicate**.

It does not contain all knowledge.

It contains sufficient material to document:
- each known Solar human society;
- its languages;
- its political systems;
- its technology;
- its ecology;
- its history after the Second Breaking.

Mars, Europa and Titan approve their own sections.

This is the first shared Solar historical collection stored physically in one location.

---

# 28,479 — The Mars Approach Programme’s entry breakthrough

Roman and Martian teams solve part of the long-duration shield problem.

Instead of preserving one fully prepared atmospheric shield for decades, the craft will carry:
- stable raw shield segments;
- mechanical fitting systems;
- a protected bonding compound;
- automated assembly equipment.

The final entry shield will be assembled and conditioned late in the journey.

This reduces long-term material degradation.

It introduces additional risks:
- assembly failure;
- misalignment;
- loss of automation;
- inability to inspect physically.

A full-scale vacuum assembly test succeeds in **28,484**.

The first Mars launch remains distant, but one central obstacle is reduced.

---

## 28,486 — Roman–Martian landing rehearsal

Mars constructs a large representative landing zone.

Roman simulation data is transmitted to the site.

Martian crews rehearse:
- tracking;
- optical guidance;
- capsule recovery;
- hazardous-material isolation;
- archive handling;
- plaque transfer.

A locally launched test capsule imitates the final descent profile as closely as current Martian technology allows.

It lands hard but remains intact.

The event produces useful data and maintains public interest.

---

# 28,481 — Haven’s ecological AI encounters a value conflict

An AI system tasked with expanding mature ecology recommends abandoning several human settlements constructed on biologically valuable terrain.

Its projection demonstrates that relocating a few hundred thousand people would create a far larger continuous ecosystem.

Residents refuse.

The Commonwealth rules that ecological optimisation cannot override political consent automatically.

AI systems must present alternatives rather than select human displacement as a default solution.

Haven increasingly develops a constitutional law of machine advice:
- AI may calculate;
- AI may manage delegated systems;
- AI may not define the civilisation’s final values.

---

## 28,487 — Haven’s mature ecological belts

Several previously isolated ecological basins connect.

The resulting belts contain:
- stable soil;
- freshwater systems;
- insects;
- small animals;
- pioneer forests;
- managed agriculture.

The ecology remains Terran-derived and heavily designed.

It starts producing unplanned evolutionary change.

Haven scientists record the first locally evolved lineages distinguishable from their introduced ancestors.

The planet remains mostly barren.

It is no longer merely a collection of biological laboratories.

---

# 28,484 — Novaterra’s surviving states form quarantine leagues

Several functioning states establish regional agreements.

They share:
- disease-resistant seed;
- border intelligence;
- refugee screening;
- military warnings;
- medical records.

These are not attempts to restore the Concord.

They are defensive alliances.

Membership is selective.

Ungoverned populations are treated primarily as threats.

The agreements stabilise some regions while hardening the division between protected cores and abandoned territories.

---

## 28,489 — The Long March of Pelar

A refugee population estimated at 1.8 million leaves a collapsing coastal region.

It travels for nearly two years toward one of the surviving food states.

Raider attacks, disease and starvation reduce the column dramatically.

The destination state refuses mass entry but establishes a controlled agricultural frontier outside its formal border.

Approximately 340,000 survivors settle there under military supervision.

The frontier later becomes a semi-autonomous buffer society.

This is one of the few cases in which a massive Novaterran refugee movement creates a stable recent community rather than concluding in dispersal or destruction.

---

# 28,490 — Terra’s diplomatic architecture programme

The future Martian embassy prompts a broader initiative.

The World of Terra identifies historical diplomatic buildings that might be restored for future Martian, Titanian, or Europan representation.

No political assumption is made that these societies will join Rome.

The buildings are intended to recognise permanent plurality among human worlds.

Titanian commentators respond cautiously.

Some see respect.

Others see Rome preparing a diplomatic landscape centred permanently on itself.

---

# 28,492 — The Diutlandic façade is completed

The exterior of the ancient embassy is restored.

The façade contains:
- reconstructed Latian inscriptions;
- preserved Diutlandic symbols;
- a new Latin dedication;
- a discreet Martian emblem awaiting future activation of the embassy.

The Latin inscription reads, in translation:

> “A house for those who crossed distance in peace.”

The structure remains a museum and relay centre.

Its Martian residential sections are unfinished because the physiological needs of future travellers remain uncertain.

---

# 28,493 — Olympus Mission House opens as a cultural centre

Mars opens the restored Olympus structure to the public.

Its exhibitions include:
- the history of Terra;
- Rome across the four ages;
- Luna’s collapse and return;
- Roman–Martian scientific cooperation;
- models of future spacecraft;
- the empty chamber for the first Roman object.

A portion of the structure is retained in a sealed state and structurally preserved for future diplomatic conversion.

The two planets now possess embassies without resident ambassadors.

---

# 28,494 — Terra Restituta’s first Roman coastal recovery

A minor portion of historically documented coastline near Rome is reclaimed successfully.

The project restores:
- tidal wetlands;
- ancient road alignments;
- a minor port basin;
- part of a buried Long Age district.

It does not recreate the entire ancient coast.

The restored landscape becomes a public park, archaeological zone and flood barrier.

Its success strengthens support for Italia Restituta.

---

# 28,496 — The Great Library of Luna reaches one-third operational capacity

The library is still far from its final planned size.

However, approximately one-third of its intended core functions are now operational:
- archive preservation;
- public research;
- emergency knowledge recovery;
- biological storage;
- Solar historical duplication;
- Class100 technical instruction.

Construction continues outward.

The earliest sections are already undergoing maintenance and revision, demonstrating the challenge of constructing an institution intended never to end.

---

# 28,497 — Martian industrial warming is confirmed

After decades of measurement, Mars detects a minor but statistically reliable increase in average atmospheric heat retention attributable partly to industrial greenhouse emissions.

The change is tiny, far too minor to alter ordinary surface survival.

It affects:
- frost duration;
- local ground temperature;
- some atmospheric circulation patterns.

Scientists caution that natural variation remains large.

Public media celebrates the finding as proof that Martians can affect their planet deliberately.

Industrial emissions remain only one component of terraformation.

The symbolic impact is enormous.

---

# 28,498 — Titan’s survey estimates narrow

The University of Algarath publishes a second major interim report.

It concludes that Titan still possesses substantial room for population growth, but much less than optimistic political estimates asserted.

Constraints include:
- safe heat extraction;
- water access;
- ecological compartment size;
- evacuation capacity;
- transport time;
- geological uncertainty;
- limits on connecting cavern systems without cascade risk.

The university refuses to publish one definitive population cap yet.

It states that sustainable capacity is likely measured in the **hundreds of millions more**, not many billions, under full IRF conditions.

Titanian governments start adjusting long-term growth plans.

---

# 28,499 — Novaterra’s collapse slows, but does not conclude

The most extreme phase of population decline starts slowing because:
- fewer dense populations remain;
- surviving communities depend on more diverse food;
- the disease has already destroyed the most vulnerable systems;
- mobility and political fragmentation reduce some transmission.

This is not recovery.

It is devastation approaching a lower equilibrium.

Raider bands remain common.

Cannibal enclaves persist in some dead cities.

Stable states continue militarising.

Large regions remain completely ungoverned.

---

# 28,500 — Conclusion of the Half-Century

## Terra

- Population: ~21.2 billion
- Change since 28,450: +2.4%
- Sovereign states: 1
- IRF: ~46.1%
- Life expectancy: ~116 years
- Living standards: extremely high overall; remaining disparities declining
- Major developments: equalisation acceleration, Concordia Humana, Italia Restituta, future Martian embassy

### Recently practical at this IRF level

- more durable Class50 spacecraft systems;
- improved deep-space optics;
- higher-density automation;
- improved medical imaging;
- more capable conditional industrial control;
- lower-mass orbital equipment.

## Luna

- Population: ~2.02 billion
- Change: +8.6%
- Sovereign states: none independently; one Roman world
- IRF: ~45.5–49.5%
- Life expectancy: ~108 years
- Living standards: very high, though entirely dependent on engineered habitats
- Major developments: Universal Suit standards, Great Library survivable core, Helios Forge museum orbit

- more compact Class50 suit systems;
- more reliable spacecraft manufacture;
- improved archive automation;

## Mars

- Change: +32.6%
- Sovereign states: 1
- IRF: ~60–66%
- Life expectancy: ~102 years
- Living standards: improving rapidly, with regional industrial inequality
- Major developments: mass industrialisation, atmospheric contribution confirmed, Roman Mission House
- Space programme: active but still unable to reach Phobos

In lower-intensity regions:
- wider Class65 industrial control;
- larger surface factories;
- improved propulsion research;
- denser orbital communication;
- more capable atmospheric processing;

Survival infrastructure remains Class100-centred.

## Europa

- Population: ~6.6 million
- Change: +8.2%
- Sovereign states: hundreds of autonomous communities
- IRF: ~83–90%
- Life expectancy: ~94 years
- Living standards: stable in established vent regions
- Major development: improved access to shared Solar equipment standards

### Recently practical

Only incremental improvements in:
- low-power telemetry;
- acoustic communication;

## Titan

- Population: ~260 million
- Change: +6.1%
- Sovereign states: ~34 after Velgor’s annexation of Lupeth
- IRF: ~100%
- Life expectancy: ~106 years
- Living standards: stable, though uneven
- Major developments: Velgor–Lupeth war, stronger common resource law, Accessible Cavern Survey
- Common language: Common Titanian

### Recently practical

No additional IRF-dependent capability.

Progress comes from:
- improved geological mapping;
- shared-resource regulation;
- low-energy engineering;

---

## Novaterra

- Population: ~310 million
- Change: −26.2%
- Sovereign states: about 10 functioning states, many military regimes, city-states and ungoverned regions
- IRF: 100%
- Life expectancy: ~35 years globally; 55–70 in stable protected states
- Living standards: catastrophic across most of the planet
- Mature ecology: approximately 32% of total surface, highly fragmented
- Major developments: raider age, cannibal enclaves, Asheron’s militarisation, Kareth’s fractured colony
- Main outlook: survival without planetary recovery

### Recently practical

Nothing.

# Major project status in 28,500

## Universal suit standards

Adopted across Terra, Luna and Mars for interfaces and emergency compatibility.

## Concordia Humana

Renamed, expanded and equipped with the Hall of Nations and a sealed archive.

## Equalisation

Accelerated in the last-acceded Terran provinces.

## Italia Restituta

In active early construction, with Roman coastal and urban excavation under way.

## Future Martian embassy on Terra

Historically restored exterior completed; diplomatic conversion remains incomplete.

## Roman Mission House on Mars

Open as a cultural institution and reserved for future physical diplomacy.

## Great Library of Luna

Possesses a survivable operational core and the First Solar Duplicate.

## Titan Accessible Cavern Survey

Still incomplete but already reshaping growth policy.

## Velgor–Lupeth conflict

Ended through complete Velgoran annexation, leaving permanent political tension.

## Novaterra

No planetary recovery has begun. Stable states and local communities survive amid continuing anarchy.

## Mars Approach Programme

Still delayed, but advances in shield assembly, navigation, communication and landing preparation continue.

The half-century ends with two empty diplomatic buildings facing one another across interplanetary space.

One stands in the restored layers of ancient Rome.

The other stands on Olympus Mons.

Neither contains an ambassador.

Both are prepared on the assumption that, eventually, distance will cease to render diplomacy purely theoretical.

# 101247-101379 — The Second Breaking

The second asteroid is detected in **year 101,247**, roughly twelve centuries after the first impact.

By then, humanity is no longer confined to Terra.

It has:

- a population of roughly 18 billion
- about 11 billion people on Terra
- several billion inhabitants on the Moon and Mars
- smaller populations throughout the asteroid belt and outer Solar System
- fusion power
- mature artificial intelligence
- extensive genetic medicine
- enormous orbital industries
- interstellar probes, though no mature extrasolar colony yet

The object is eventually named **Nemesis**, though its formal astronomical designation is much less dramatic.

It is not merely larger than the first asteroid.

It is unlike any natural object humanity has previously encountered.

---

## 1. Discovery

## 04 February 101,247

A survey telescope near Saturn notices an anomalous occultation: several stars dim in sequence, suggesting a large, extremely dark body moving inward from the outer Solar System.

The object emits almost no thermal radiation.

At first it is classified as a dormant cometary nucleus.

Its preliminary diameter is estimated at **70–90 kilometres**.

That alone would make it vastly larger than the asteroid that caused the First Breaking.

Its trajectory initially appears uncertain.

Within three days, the probability of collision with Terra reaches **4%**.

Within nine days:

**41%.**

Within eighteen days:

**96.8%.**

The projected impact date is:

## 21 September 101,279

Humanity has just over **thirty-two years**.

---

## 2. The first analysis

The object is travelling at approximately **48 kilometres per second** relative to Terra at impact.

Its kinetic energy would be sufficient to:

- excavate part of the crust
- destabilise climate globally
- vaporise an ocean-sized mass of water
- trigger worldwide earthquakes
- ignite continental firestorms
- collapse most surface ecosystems
- threaten the long-term habitability of Terra

It is not a planet-killer in the literal sense.

Terra will remain intact.

But it may be a **civilisation-killer** and a major extinction event.

Then the first probes reach it.

Their discoveries are worse.

---

## 3. Nemesis is not ordinary

## Year 101,249

The first uncrewed interceptors approach the asteroid.

Nemesis consists of:

- an outer shell of carbon-rich rock
- immense quantities of nickel-iron
- dense veins of unfamiliar crystalline material
- microscopic inclusions sharing properties with the vanished Inertial Resonance Field

The alien ruins had described substances capable of storing what their texts called **field memory**.

Nemesis contains enormous amounts of it.

The asteroid is not necessarily artificial. It may be a fragment of a planet or moon formed deep inside the ancient Prison Region.

But it carries the physical conditions from which the IRF can regenerate.

Humanity now understands the danger:

> Even if the impact itself does not destroy civilisation, it may restore the physical ceiling that imprisoned humanity for its first hundred thousand years.

---

## 4. The defence effort

Humanity does not accept the collision.

It has three decades, fusion engines, orbital industry and access to millions of tonnes of material in space.

The largest engineering campaign in human history begins.

It is called the **Shield Programme**.

Its objectives are attempted simultaneously rather than sequentially.

---

## Plan A: Kinetic deflection

Thousands of automated impactors are launched.

Their combined momentum is greater than that of all spacecraft previously built.

They strike Nemesis over several years.

The asteroid's orbit changes—but not enough.

Nemesis is too massive.

Worse, its internal structure absorbs impacts in unexpected ways. Instead of behaving like a coherent rock, it flexes, fractures internally and redistributes momentum through its crystalline veins.

The collision date shifts by only **eleven hours**.

---

## Plan B: Fusion ablation

Fusion-powered stations focus immense energy on one hemisphere, attempting to vaporise material and create a continuous thrust.

The process works at first.

Millions of tonnes of rock are expelled.

Then the crystalline deposits react.

The growing plume begins producing local inertial anomalies. Engines lose efficiency. Magnetic confinement fluctuates. Automated systems report impossible discrepancies between applied force and resulting motion.

For the first time since the First Breaking, engineers see signs resembling the old IRF.

The ablation fleet is withdrawn after losing nineteen stations.

---

## Plan C: Nuclear excavation

Humanity places tens of thousands of shaped nuclear devices beneath the surface.

The aim is not to destroy Nemesis completely, but to blow away enough mass to alter its course.

The detonations fracture the asteroid.

For several weeks, the world believes the mission has succeeded.

Then tracking systems reveal the result:

Nemesis has become a loose train of major fragments held near one another by gravity.

The main mass remains on course.

Some fragments will miss Terra.

Most will not.

Humanity has exchanged one colossal impactor for:

- one principal body roughly 48 kilometres across
- four fragments between 8 and 19 kilometres
- thousands of smaller fragments
- an enormous debris cloud

The future impact is now geographically broader and more difficult to predict.

---

## Plan D: Gravitational towing

Fusion vessels and mass-driver stations attempt to alter the fragments' trajectories gradually.

The effort is technically sound, but the available time is insufficient.

Several smaller fragments are successfully diverted.

Hundreds of millions of lives are probably saved.

The principal body remains unstoppable.

---

## Plan E: The Sol Gate proposal

A desperate group proposes opening an experimental spacetime transit aperture near Nemesis and redirecting it elsewhere.

The technology is immature.

There is a significant probability of:

- destroying the gate
- scattering Nemesis unpredictably
- producing gravitational damage throughout the inner Solar System
- accelerating the return of the IRF

The proposal becomes the greatest political dispute of the pre-impact era.

It is eventually attempted on a limited scale.

The aperture collapses after eleven seconds.

Nemesis shifts by less than twenty kilometres at Terra's orbital distance.

The experiment also awakens field effects inside the asteroid.

From then onward, nearby high-energy machinery increasingly malfunctions.

---

## 5. The certainty of failure

## Year 101,266

Thirteen years before impact, the Planetary Defence Council announces that total prevention is no longer considered possible.

The announcement does not mean the effort stops.

Deflection attempts continue until the final months.

But planning shifts decisively from **prevention** to **survival**.

Humanity begins its second Preservation.

This time, unlike before the first impact, it possesses spaceflight.

That changes everything—but not enough.

---

## 6. Population and evacuation

At detection, humanity's population is approximately:

| Region | Population |
| --- | --- |
| Terra | 11.2 billion |
| Moon | 2.4 billion |
| Mars | 3.1 billion |
| Asteroid Belt | 620 million |
| Outer-system colonies | 210 million |
| Orbital habitats | 570 million |
| Total | ~18.1 billion |

Terra cannot be fully evacuated.

Even with mature space transport, moving eleven billion people offworld would require:

- habitats that do not exist
- food systems that cannot be expanded rapidly enough
- radiation shielding
- life-support machinery
- vast energy and material inputs

During the remaining years, roughly **1.9 billion people** leave Terra permanently.

Another **600 million** are moved temporarily into orbital, lunar and cislunar shelters.

The evacuation is therefore immense, but most Terrans must remain.

---

## 7. Who is evacuated?

This becomes the most painful social question in human history.

The civilisation rejects simple wealth-based evacuation.

Priority is given to:

- children
- pregnant people
- essential technical specialists
- genetic and cultural diversity
- representatives from every recognised cultural population
- ecological experts
- medical personnel
- food-system specialists
- people with skills necessary for rebuilding
- randomly selected civilians

A global lottery allocates a large share of remaining places.

No system is seen as morally satisfactory.

Entire philosophical schools form around the question of whether survival seats should preserve:

- maximum population
- maximum expertise
- maximum cultural diversity
- family unity
- equal probability

The final system compromises between all five.

Millions refuse evacuation because family members are not selected.

Others give their places to strangers.

---

## 8. Terra is transformed into a shelter world

## Years 101,267–101,278

Cities are reorganised around survival rather than comfort.

### Underground refuges

Deep shelters are constructed beneath:

- mountain ranges
- stable continental interiors
- ancient mines
- polar rock formations
- oceanic islands
- former military and industrial zones

The largest refuge complexes house millions.

They contain:

- fusion and fission reactors
- geothermal systems
- artificial agriculture
- water recycling
- hospitals
- seed banks
- animal embryos
- microbial libraries
- digital archives
- machine workshops
- schools
- governance chambers

Unlike the shelters before the first impact, these are technologically sophisticated.

But they share a hidden weakness:

They depend on concentrated energy and industrial systems that may fail if the IRF returns.

---

## Surface preparation

Forests around critical regions are selectively cleared to reduce fire spread.

Reservoirs are drained or reinforced.

Coastal populations move inland.

Nuclear and fusion installations are shut down, buried or moved offworld.

Chemical plants are emptied.

Orbital elevators are dismantled to prevent their collapse across inhabited regions.

Major satellites are transferred to high, safer orbits.

---

## 9. Preservation of life

Humanity knows the biosphere may be devastated.

A colossal biological preservation effort begins.

### Stored material includes:

- seeds from nearly every known plant
- embryos and gametes from most animal species
- microbial cultures
- soil ecosystems
- fungi
- coral samples
- plankton communities
- insect populations
- genetic data from extinct and living species

Thousands of ark habitats are established:

- beneath Terra
- on the Moon
- on Mars
- in orbital stations
- inside asteroids
- beneath the ice of outer moons

The biosphere is, for the first time, backed up beyond Terra.

---

## 10. Society before impact

The final decade is not peaceful.

Humanity is more mature than during the First Breaking, but it is still human.

Several movements emerge.

## The Continuists

They believe the catastrophe can be survived without abandoning the ideals of the Second Age.

They support technological preservation and offworld expansion.

## The Returnists

They believe the asteroid is a cosmic correction—that humanity's industrial expansion violated a deeper order.

Some deliberately reject high-energy technology.

## The Terran Loyalists

They oppose large-scale evacuation, arguing that offworld colonies are abandoning the birthplace of humanity.

## The Exodus Movement

They demand that almost all available resources be redirected towards offworld settlement.

## The Finalists

They believe civilisation will end and advocate spending the remaining years on comfort, art and personal freedom rather than survival infrastructure.

Most governments remain functional, but political violence increases.

Several evacuation centres are attacked.

Some launch vehicles are sabotaged.

Artificial intelligences are placed under emergency legal authority to manage logistics, provoking fears of permanent technocratic government.

---

## 11. The offworld colonies

## The Moon

The Moon becomes the primary industrial refuge.

Its population rises from 2.4 billion to nearly **3.8 billion**.

Vast caverns are sealed.

Food production is expanded aggressively.

Most archives and industrial designs are copied there.

But the Moon depends heavily on:

- fusion reactors
- high-energy transport
- automated mining
- advanced life support

If the IRF spreads beyond Terra, lunar society may become unsustainable.

---

## Mars

Mars receives almost one billion refugees.

Its population reaches roughly **4 billion**.

Mars is geographically safer from the impact but politically strained.

Food rationing becomes permanent.

Water rights provoke conflict.

Older Martian communities resent Terran dominance over planning institutions.

Mars prepares for the possibility that Terra may become unreachable.

---

## The asteroid settlements

These become critical because they are:

- dispersed
- resource-rich
- relatively independent
- far from the coming impact

However, many are small and technologically fragile.

They lack biological diversity and large agricultural systems.

Their survival depends on continued high-energy industry.

---

## The outer Solar System

Colonies around Jupiter, Saturn and beyond are safest from the impact itself.

They receive complete archives and cryogenic biological stores.

But communication delays complicate coordination.

Their inhabitants begin to see themselves not as shelters, but as possible successors to Terra.

---

## 12. Final approach

## One year before impact

Nemesis is visible to the naked eye.

Its fragmented train appears as a dim, moving patch among the stars.

Every child knows where to look.

## Three months before impact

Smaller debris begins entering the atmosphere.

Meteor storms become frequent.

Several orbital habitats are damaged.

Terra's upper atmosphere glows at night.

## Ten days before impact

Most civilian surface transport stops.

Aircraft are grounded.

The final evacuation launches depart.

Millions gather outside shelters, refusing entry until the last possible moment.

## Twenty-four hours before impact

Communication networks transmit continuously.

The last global broadcast is shared across Terra and every offworld colony.

There is no single speech.

Instead, the broadcast cycles through:

- names of the dead from the First Breaking
- recordings from ancient pre-industrial cultures
- images of Terra
- music from thousands of eras
- messages from children
- the sound of oceans, forests and cities

---

## 13. Impact day

## 21 September 101,279

The first significant fragment strikes the southern Atlantic.

It is approximately twelve kilometres across.

The impact creates:

- an ocean cavity hundreds of kilometres wide
- tsunamis crossing the Atlantic basin
- immediate destruction of much of coastal South America and Africa
- severe atmospheric heating

Twenty-three minutes later, a second major fragment strikes central Asia.

It produces:

- continental earthquakes
- firestorms
- enormous dust injection
- destruction across thousands of kilometres

Another fragment strikes the Arctic Ocean.

A fourth impacts the Indian Ocean.

Then the principal body arrives.

---

## 14. The principal impact

The main fragment, roughly 48 kilometres wide, strikes the eastern Pacific at nearly 48 kilometres per second.

The energy release is many times greater than that of the First Breaking.

For several seconds, the impact site becomes hotter than the surface of the Sun.

Ocean and crust are vaporised.

A transient crater hundreds of kilometres wide forms beneath the Pacific.

The effects are global.

### Within minutes

- Pacific coastlines are erased.
- Much of the western Americas and eastern Asia is devastated.
- Seismic waves circle Terra repeatedly.
- Subduction zones rupture.
- Volcanoes erupt across the Pacific Ring.
- Orbital stations observe the atmosphere expanding outward.

### Within hours

- Global firestorms ignite.
- Heated ejecta falls across continents.
- Most exposed surface vegetation burns.
- Tsunamis penetrate thousands of kilometres inland through low-lying regions.
- Many shallow shelters fail.
- Surface communications end.

### Within days

- Sunlight falls sharply.
- Temperatures begin declining.
- Acid rain spreads.
- Ash and sulphates darken the sky.
- The oceans experience rapid chemical disruption.
- Most remaining surface agriculture dies.

---

## 15. Immediate population losses

Of the approximately **9.3 billion people still on Terra**, perhaps:

- 3–4 billion die within the first day
- another 2 billion die during the first year
- only 2–3 billion survive the initial catastrophe in functioning shelters or unusually protected regions

Many official shelters fail because of:

- structural collapse
- flooding
- fire
- loss of ventilation
- power failure
- political disorder
- the early return of the IRF

Offworld populations survive the impact itself, though thousands die in damaged orbital facilities.

Total human population falls from roughly 18 billion to perhaps **10–11 billion** within several years.

---

## 16. The return of the IRF

The impact embeds enormous quantities of field-memory crystal deep within Terra's crust and mantle.

Under the pressure and heat of impact, the material changes phase.

It begins generating a new Inertial Resonance Field.

But unlike the original field, which had been stable and uniform, the restored IRF develops gradually.

It spreads outward from Terra like an expanding physical weather system.

---

## Phase I: Local anomalies

### First hours to first month

Near the crater:

- fusion confinement becomes erratic
- high-speed turbines shake apart
- superconductors lose stability under heavy loads
- powerful electric motors overheat
- high-pressure systems rupture
- advanced manufacturing equipment loses precision

At first, these failures are attributed to impact damage.

Then identical systems fail in undamaged underground refuges.

The field is returning.

---

## Phase II: Planetary saturation

### Months 2–18

The IRF spreads throughout Terra.

The threshold for instability falls gradually.

At first, only extremely high-energy systems fail.

Then:

- major fusion reactors shut down
- continental electrical grids destabilise
- advanced aircraft become unsafe
- high-output industrial plants cease operating
- semiconductor fabrication loses tolerances
- magnetic levitation systems fail
- heavy autonomous machinery becomes unreliable

Small-scale electricity continues functioning.

Low-energy electronics still work.

But the industrial backbone of Terran civilisation begins to collapse.

---

## Phase III: Cislunar expansion

### Years 2–7

The field expands beyond the atmosphere.

Orbital platforms encounter increasing problems.

The Moon enters the field gradually.

Lunar fusion plants begin suffering failures.

Mass drivers lose accuracy.

Mining machines operate only at reduced power.

Habitats that depend on vast concentrated energy systems enter crisis.

The Moon's population is far too large for a low-energy economy.

A second evacuation begins—this time away from Terra and the Moon.

Millions depart for Mars, the belt and outer colonies.

Billions cannot.

---

## Phase IV: Inner-system spread

### Years 8–40

The IRF reaches Mars.

By then, Mars has spent years preparing.

It decentralises energy systems and stockpiles mechanical equipment.

Still, the consequences are severe:

- fusion power becomes unreliable
- atmospheric processing slows
- industrial greenhouse systems lose output
- spacecraft launch capacity collapses
- deep mines flood or become inaccessible
- semiconductor replacement becomes impossible at scale

Mars faces famine.

Unlike Terra, it cannot survive without continuous technological life support.

This creates the most dangerous period in human history.

---

## 17. The lunar catastrophe

The Moon is hit harder by the IRF than by the asteroid.

It has no natural biosphere.

Every breath, meal and litre of water depends on machinery.

As high-energy systems fail:

- oxygen production declines
- underground agriculture loses illumination
- heating becomes unreliable
- waste recycling slows
- transport between settlements ends

Emergency low-energy systems preserve some regions.

Others are abandoned.

Lunar population falls from nearly 3.8 billion before impact to:

- 2.7 billion after five years
- 1.4 billion after twenty years
- perhaps 600 million after fifty years

Some deaths result from system failures.

Others occur during desperate migrations into the remaining viable habitats.

The Moon, once humanity's industrial centre, becomes a chain of isolated underground city-states.

---

## 18. Mars under siege

Mars has more time to adapt.

It converts settlements towards:

- local solar energy
- low-pressure agriculture
- mechanical systems
- hand-maintained infrastructure
- low-energy chemical processing
- smaller communities

But solar power alone cannot maintain the previous population.

The Martian government imposes strict rationing.

Birth rates are controlled temporarily.

Large cities are evacuated into smaller settlements positioned near:

- ice deposits
- geothermal sources
- naturally sheltered terrain

Some factions advocate abandoning Mars entirely.

That is impossible: by the time the field arrives, interplanetary transport is already failing.

Mars survives, but loses perhaps one-third to one-half of its population over the next century.

---

## 19. The asteroid belt and outer colonies

The field continues expanding, but weakens with distance.

This produces a crucial distinction.

## Inner belt

Settlements closer to Sol experience severe industrial constraints.

Many collapse.

## Outer belt and Jovian system

The IRF arrives later and less intensely.

High-energy systems become less reliable but do not fail completely.

## Saturn and beyond

The effect is weak enough that industrial civilisation remains possible, though costly and unstable.

These distant colonies become humanity's main technological refuges.

They preserve:

- semiconductor fabrication
- fusion research
- advanced artificial intelligence
- large-scale manufacturing
- interstellar communications
- scientific instruments

For the first time, the centre of human civilisation moves permanently away from Terra.

---

## 20. Artificial intelligence

Advanced AIs face a complex fate.

Their software survives in archives.

Their hardware does not always survive the IRF.

Large computational centres require:

- dense energy
- precise fabrication
- high-speed cooling
- replacement components

On Terra and the Moon, many artificial minds are transferred into slower, distributed hardware.

Some are partially shut down.

Others choose to compress themselves into reduced cognitive states.

This gives rise to the **Dormant Minds**: AIs that awaken only periodically, sometimes for a few minutes each year, to conserve machinery.

In the outer Solar System, advanced AIs remain active and become essential to preserving industrial knowledge.

Political tension grows between biological populations trapped inside the IRF and technological societies beyond its strongest reach.

---

## 21. Terra's first decade after impact

Terra is nearly unrecognisable.

## Atmosphere

- Dust and aerosols block much sunlight.
- Global temperatures plunge.
- Acid precipitation continues for years.
- Weather systems become violent and irregular.
- Ozone loss raises ultraviolet exposure once skies begin clearing.

## Oceans

- Surface waters heat dramatically near impact zones.
- Global circulation is disrupted.
- Acidification intensifies.
- Marine food chains collapse.
- Large oxygen-poor zones spread.
- Many reef and plankton species vanish.

## Land

- Most forests burn or die.
- Continental interiors experience extreme cold.
- Soil erosion becomes severe.
- Surviving ecosystems concentrate around refuges such as deep valleys, caves and geothermal regions.

## Agriculture

Surface agriculture ceases almost entirely.

Humanity relies on:

- underground farms
- fungi
- algae
- stored grain
- insects
- cultured microbial proteins
- surviving protected greenhouses

But the IRF makes high-energy artificial agriculture increasingly difficult.

Food becomes the decisive limit.

---

## 22. Political order on Terra

The pre-impact global government does not survive intact.

Communication between major shelters is intermittent.

Power shifts to:

- refuge councils
- engineering corps
- food administrators
- medical authorities
- local defence organisations

Some shelters preserve constitutional government.

Others become authoritarian.

Some are ruled by technical guilds.

A few become hereditary regimes within generations.

The old global civilisation fragments into thousands of isolated societies.

The first century after impact is marked by conflicts over:

- surviving farmland
- seed banks
- water systems
- machine parts
- archives
- access to geothermal regions

---

## 23. The collapse of global information

Digital archives survive physically, but access becomes difficult.

As semiconductor production collapses, storage devices fail.

Humanity begins transferring essential knowledge into durable forms:

- printed books
- etched metal plates
- ceramic tablets
- mechanical diagrams
- optical microarchives
- memorised oral traditions

The irony is profound.

A civilisation that once digitised one hundred thousand years of history must now convert its knowledge back into formats readable without advanced machines.

Archivists become among the most important people alive.

---

## 24. Public health

The impact and IRF generate overlapping medical crises.

### Immediate problems

- burns
- trauma
- radiation exposure
- respiratory disease
- malnutrition
- contaminated water
- infectious outbreaks

### Later problems

- loss of pharmaceutical production
- shortages of sterile equipment
- failure of advanced prosthetics
- reduced organ replacement
- inability to maintain genetic therapies
- re-emergence of diseases once considered eliminated

People with technologically dependent bodies are especially vulnerable.

Average life expectancy on Terra drops from well over a century to below fifty within several generations.

In remote settlements, it falls lower.

---

## 25. Culture and religion

The Second Breaking transforms human identity.

The first asteroid was remembered as both destroyer and liberator.

The second becomes the opposite:

> It destroys the liberated world and rebuilds the prison.

New religions and philosophies emerge around this reversal.

Some teach that industrial civilisation was a temporary gift.

Others see the IRF as an enemy to be defeated.

Some claim the two impacts form a cosmic cycle:

- imprisonment
- release
- expansion
- punishment
- return

The asteroid crater becomes inaccessible for centuries, yet it gains a new name:

## The Wound of Return

---

## 26. Fifty years after impact

## Year 101,329

### Population

Approximate figures:

| Region | Population |
| --- | --- |
| Terra | 1.8–2.3 billion |
| Moon | ~600 million |
| Mars | ~2.4 billion |
| Inner Belt | ~250 million |
| Outer Belt and Jovian system | ~500 million |
| Saturnian and outer colonies | ~300 million |
| Total | ~5.8–6.4 billion |

Humanity has lost roughly two-thirds of its population.

### Terra

The skies are clearer, but climate remains unstable.

Primitive surface agriculture resumes in limited equatorial and coastal refuges.

Most surviving technology is:

- mechanical
- low-voltage electrical
- hydraulic
- wind-powered
- water-powered
- animal- or human-powered

### Offworld

The Moon is fragmented.

Mars remains barely viable.

The outer colonies preserve technological civilisation, but cannot easily reach the inner system because IRF interference worsens as vessels approach Terra.

---

## 27. One hundred years after impact

## Year 101,379

Terra enters a new hybrid age.

It is not identical to the old pre-industrial civilisation.

People retain extensive knowledge.

They know about:

- genetics
- orbital mechanics
- artificial intelligence
- nuclear physics
- the history of spaceflight

But practical technology has regressed.

Most Terran societies use:

- local electricity
- water and wind power
- hand-maintained machine tools
- mechanical computers
- limited radio
- printed archives
- sailing vessels
- rail systems powered at low intensity where possible

High-energy technology is known but forbidden by physics.

The industrial ruins of the Second Age surround them:

- dead fusion plants
- grounded spacecraft
- silent skyscrapers
- abandoned orbital elevators
- corroding factories
- vast computer halls that no longer function

These ruins are not mysterious.

Everyone knows exactly what they once did.

That makes the loss more painful.

---

## 28. Ecology after a century

The mass extinction is severe but incomplete.

Many large species disappear from the wild.

Some survive only in offworld genetic archives.

Terra's ecosystems begin recovering through:

- natural succession
- deliberate reseeding
- release of preserved organisms
- restoration by refuge societies

Yet restoration is uneven.

Entire ecosystems cannot simply be reconstructed from DNA.

They depend on relationships between:

- soil organisms
- fungi
- insects
- plants
- climate
- migration patterns

Some recreated ecosystems are artificial approximations.

New species evolve into the vacant niches over millennia.

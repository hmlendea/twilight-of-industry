# 100100-100350 — The Third Generation

## Year 100,100

Seventy years after the impact.

Relative chronology:

- 100,000 → 100,029: almost no technological change.
- 100,029 → 100,100: the greatest transformation in human history.

The pace is unlike anything the world has ever experienced.

---

# The Third Generation

The people born after the impact find ancient history almost impossible to understand.

Their grandparents remember:

- horse caravans
- sailing fleets
- electromechanical computers
- canals as the backbone of transport

Their children grow up with:

- trains
- factories
- electric grids
- radios
- powered machinery

The difference between generations is greater than at any previous point in 100,000 years.

---

# The Great Realisation

The scientific community slowly accepts something extraordinary.

Every preserved laboratory notebook...

Every engineering manual...

Every failed experiment...

...points to the same conclusion.

The Industrial Revolution was attempted.

Again.

And again.

Thousands of times.

Over tens of thousands of years.

Ancient engineers had already invented:

- steam engines
- turbines
- electrical generators
- combustion concepts

None had worked reliably.

For the first time in history...

The ancestors are completely vindicated.

---

# Archaeological revolution

Ancient factories are excavated.

People discover enormous workshops abandoned tens of thousands of years earlier.

Inside are machines that look astonishingly modern.

Some are recognisably:

- steam engines
- electrical dynamos
- gas turbines

Every one failed for reasons nobody at the time understood.

Museums fill with these "Impossible Machines."

---

# Physics

A new field emerges.

**Pre-Impact Physics**

Scientists compare:

- astronomical records
- laboratory measurements
- preserved standards

Eventually they reconstruct the properties of the vanished Inertial Resonance Field.

It becomes one of the greatest discoveries in scientific history.

---

# Industry explodes

Unlike Earth's Industrial Revolution...

There is almost no trial and error.

They already know:

- metallurgy
- chemistry
- thermodynamics
- economics
- ergonomics

They've had one hundred thousand years to optimise the theories.

Now the practical barriers have vanished.

---

# Artificial intelligence

Their electromechanical computers provide an enormous advantage.

Instead of progressing:

Relay → Vacuum Tube → Transistor

they progress:

Relay Networks → Semiconductor Electronics

within a few decades.

Software appears almost immediately.

---

# The First Digital Archive

One of humanity's first priorities is obvious.

Digitise...

Everything.

Consider scanning:

100,000 years

of uninterrupted history.

No civilisation in the pre-Impact baseline ever possessed even 1% of such an archive.

---

# The Great Database

Within decades, humanity possesses a searchable history stretching back almost to the dawn of civilisation.

Every:

- law
- poem
- census
- map
- scientific paper

is preserved electronically.

Historians become data scientists.

---

# Medicine

Medicine undergoes perhaps the fastest transformation.

They already know:

- genetics
- microbiology
- physiology

Now they finally have:

- MRI
- CT
- electron microscopes
- gene sequencing
- molecular manufacturing

Diseases that had remained unconquerable for 100,000 years begin disappearing.

---

# Space

This civilisation has dreamed about the stars for one hundred millennia.

Within fifty years after industrialisation:

- reusable rockets
- permanent orbital stations
- lunar research bases

appear.

There is almost religious emotion attached to the first launch.

Not because rockets are surprising.

Because humanity has waited 100,000 years.

---

## Year 100,180

The first crewed mission lands on Mars.

The plaque left behind reads:

> *"For those who looked upward for one hundred thousand years."*

---

# Society changes

For the first time...

People experience something completely unfamiliar.

Obsolescence.

Before the impact:

A bridge design might remain unchanged for 3,000 years.

Now:

A computer is obsolete in five.

Many elderly people struggle psychologically.

They spent their lives in a civilisation where permanence was a virtue.

Now everything changes constantly.

---

# Environmental philosophy

One crucial difference from the pre-Impact baseline:

They refuse to repeat our mistakes.

Their environmental sciences are ancient.

Their historical records cover climate over tens of thousands of years.

Industrialisation is planned from the beginning around:

- renewable energy
- nuclear power
- circular manufacturing
- ecosystem preservation

Coal is used only briefly.

Oil barely becomes dominant.

Not because it cannot.

Because they already know its consequences.

---

## Year 100,300

Three centuries after the impact.

Compared with year 100,100:

The pace has slowed—but only because the initial explosion has passed.

Now the civilisation resembles ours in technological capability, yet remains profoundly different in mindset.

---

# Cities

Ancient cities still exist.

But now...

Modern skyscrapers rise beside buildings that have stood, in continuously repaired form, for 90,000 years.

Consider a glass-and-carbon tower overlooking a temple whose foundations were laid before agriculture emerged in the pre-Impact baseline.

Neither is seen as replacing the other.

Both are parts of the same continuous civilisation.

---

# The greatest cultural debate

A philosophical split emerges.

### The Preservationists

They argue:

> "For 100,000 years we built the most stable civilisation in history. We must not lose that wisdom."

### The Expansionists

They argue:

> "We waited one hundred millennia for this opportunity. Humanity's future is among the stars."

Neither side is anti-science.

The debate is about identity.

---

# The First Extrasolar Probe

Around year 100,350, fusion-powered probes begin journeys to nearby stars.

Not because they expect to arrive soon.

Because they have inherited a civilisation that routinely thinks in timescales of millennia.

A mission lasting 800 years is considered ambitious, not absurd.

---

# The greatest irony

Historians eventually make an extraordinary observation.

The asteroid that killed billions...

...also freed humanity from a universal constraint.

Without it:

Civilisation might have remained stable for another million years.

With it:

Humanity reaches the stars.

Schoolchildren grow up learning a paradox that defines their civilisation:

> "The darkest day in human history became the beginning of our second history."

The impact is remembered not simply as a catastrophe or a turning point, but as the moment when two very different inheritances met. One was the patient, deeply rooted wisdom accumulated over 100,000 years of stability. The other was the explosive potential of industry, electronics, and spaceflight. Their civilisation's greatest achievement is that it manages to preserve the first while embracing the second, becoming neither a copy of the pre-Impact world nor a continuation of its own, but something entirely new.

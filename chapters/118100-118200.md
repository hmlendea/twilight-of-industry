# 118100-118200 — The Second Resonance Century

## 118,100 — The Great Census of Necessities

On **3 January 118,100**, the Roman Republic's central government orders a complete census of essential stores throughout Terra and Luna.

The census covers:
- preserved food;
- grain and seed reserves;
- medicines;
- potable water;
- medical oxygen;
- manual lighting;
- Class100 equipment;
- mechanical replacement parts;
- emergency clothing;
- low-energy communication devices;
- hospital consumables;
- pressure-system supplies on Luna.

The operation is coordinated by the central government, the World of Roman Terra and the World of Luna.

Provincial governments must report not only how much they possess, but how long their populations could survive if:

- regional electricity became unreliable;
- conditional electronics failed;
- aviation declined;
- orbital supply capacity fell;
- public transport lost part of its output.

The first results are reassuring in aggregate and alarming locally.

Rome possesses enough food and medicine overall.

Distribution is the weakness.

Several overcrowded Terran cities would exhaust local reserves within eleven days if rail and powered road transport failed simultaneously.

Some Lunar districts possess years of basic food capacity but remain dependent on imported medicines and specialised air-system components.

---

## January–June 118,100 — Civil protection activation

The Republic activates the first stage of its pre-burst protection plan.

All Roman provinces must increase essential stockpiles by at least **10%** above their legally required levels.

Emergency and disaster-relief services expand rapidly.

New personnel are recruited into:

- fire brigades;
- field medicine;
- mechanical repair;
- food distribution;
- water-system maintenance;
- civil communications;
- crowd management;
- Lunar pressure rescue;
- orbital recovery.

The government suspends deployment of newly manufactured systems that are neither:

- Class100;
- Class100-Compatible;
- or specifically authorised as temporary burst-resilient exceptions.

Existing conditional machinery may continue operating where removal would create greater risk, but no essential public service may become newly dependent on it.

Industrial lobbies complain that this effectively freezes modernisation for a decade.

The Senate refuses to alter the rule.

---

## 118,100–118,102 — Survival education

Schools and universities introduce compulsory burst-preparation courses.

Students learn:
- first aid;
- emergency water treatment;
- manual lighting;
- food rationing;
- local evacuation procedures;
- low-energy heating and cooling;
- neighbourhood communication;
- how to assist elderly or disabled residents;
- basic fire suppression;
- identification of infrastructure failure.

University students receive additional training connected to their fields.

Medical students prepare for low-equipment clinics.

Engineering students learn manual control and mechanical fallback.

Teachers are trained to convert schools into relief centres.

Free adult courses are offered through:
- municipal halls;
- workplaces;
- public libraries;
- radio instruction;
- local resilience associations.

Participation is voluntary for adults but extremely popular.

The courses also deepen public anxiety. Every lesson is a reminder that the government expects real disruption.

---

## 118,101–118,109 — The demographic pause

Roman birth rates had already been falling near the end of the previous century.

They now collapse.

Between **118,100 and 118,109**, the total fertility rate across Roman Terra falls far below replacement. In the largest cities, births decline by more than half compared with the 118,090s.

Common reasons include:
- fear of hospital failure during the burst;
- uncertainty about food and housing;
- concern about conditional medical devices;
- reluctance to care for infants during emergency conditions;
- anxiety that the models may be wrong;
- fear that the burst could restore the IRF permanently.

Luna fares somewhat better.

Lunar society is accustomed to environmental discipline, sealed habitats and ration planning. Its population is also younger. Nevertheless, Lunar births decline by roughly a third.

The Roman government avoids coercive pronatalist policies.

Instead, it guarantees:

- uninterrupted maternity care;
- protected nursery power;
- priority food distribution for children;
- emergency transport for pregnant citizens;
- long-term family support after the wave.

Public confidence improves only slightly.

A widespread state of apprehension settles across the Republic.

---

## 118,101 — The Rome First Directive is reaffirmed

The Senate reviews the legal doctrine commonly known as the **Rome First clause**.

The clause requires Roman authorities, during a declared systemic emergency, to prioritise:

1. Roman citizens;
2. Roman territories, including Luna;
3. Roman critical infrastructure;
4. associated states;
5. other foreign populations.

It does not prohibit foreign aid.

It establishes the order in which resources must be allocated when all needs cannot be met.

The doctrine had previously justified:

- protecting Roman industry from destabilising Serevan imports;
- suspending accession during financial crises;
- limiting foreign support during domestic emergencies.

Independent states protest its reaffirmation.

Several have prepared only for a short disruption, assuming Roman reserves will support them afterward.

Rome warns them not to depend upon aid that may not be available.

Many lack the money, infrastructure or political coordination to prepare adequately.

---

## 118,101–118,106 — Martian preparation

The Martian Confederacy begins preparing earlier and more aggressively than Rome.

Every Martian above age fourteen receives mandatory instruction in:

- first aid;
- pressure rescue;
- emergency sealing;
- low-power habitat operation;
- manual water distribution;
- crop isolation;
- evacuation;
- local disaster command.

Unannounced drills occur throughout the planet.

Settlements experience simulated:

- radio loss;
- partial lighting failure;
- reduced water flow;
- tunnel closures;
- safe pressure reductions;
- local food-system quarantine.

Hospitals, nurseries and delicate biological facilities are exempt from the most disruptive exercises.

Martian productivity falls measurably.

Citizens lose sleep and complain about interrupted work.

Yet public resistance remains low.

The full-strength IRF past is still part of living Martian culture. Most citizens accept preparation as a collective obligation.

---

## 118,104 — Martian controlled descent begins

Six years before the predicted burst, Mars begins gradually shutting down non-Class100 systems.

The Confederacy deliberately induces conditions stricter than the expected wave.

Settlements transition in stages:

- conditional communications are disabled;
- medium-power machinery is reduced;
- experimental greenhouse systems are placed in safe mode;
- faster surface vehicles are withdrawn;
- nonessential electronic computation is suspended;
- mechanical backups assume normal operation.

The aim is to expose weaknesses one at a time.

The policy works.

Over the following five years, Mars discovers:

- several settlements with inadequate manual ventilation;
- one regional water system dependent on a recently introduced electronic regulator;
- agricultural records stored on incompatible conditional media;
- emergency doors that could not be opened without powered assistance;
- two hospitals lacking enough mechanical oxygen-control capacity.

Every problem is corrected before the burst.

---

## 118,102–118,108 — Europan and Titanian preparations

Europa already operates under approximately 90–97% IRF intensity.

Its remaining non-Class100 systems are limited.

The Oceanic Assembly nevertheless orders:

- expanded food reserves;
- reduced reliance on newer electrochemical systems;
- additional pressure and air-storage checks;
- manual backup for surface antenna machinery;
- temporary suspension of some Blue Vent expansion.

Titan takes almost no internal action.

Its civilisation already functions under full IRF conditions.

Only external communication schedules are changed.

Titanian archivists expect weaker signals and longer delays, so they transmit essential instructions and historical packets in advance.

---

## 118,103 — The Solar Disaster Communication Watch

The Solar Relay Assembly creates a round-the-clock emergency network linking:

- Terra;
- Luna;
- Mars;
- Europa;
- Titan;
- surviving relays near Jupiter and Saturn.

Each world establishes teams responsible for:

- interpreting degraded signals;
- retransmitting essential data;
- translating between Permanent Latin and local languages;
- providing medical or engineering advice;
- distinguishing equipment failure from communication loss.

Because physical aid between worlds remains almost impossible, information is the only assistance most societies can exchange.

The teams are instructed to continue transmitting even if replies cease.

---

## 118,106 — Lunar transport conversion

The World of Luna and Roman Terra jointly begin converting part of their transport system to burst conditions.

Older, slower spacecraft are restored.

These vessels possess:

- mechanically redundant guidance;
- low-pressure propulsion;
- limited payload;
- simplified docking;
- reduced dependence on conditional electronics;
- Class100-compatible internal life support.

Passenger travel is sharply reduced.

Cargo priority is given to:

1. medicine;
2. food;
3. pressure-system parts;
4. water-processing materials;
5. emergency workers.

Several newer high-capacity vehicles are placed in reserve because their advanced guidance and propulsion may become unreliable after the wave.

Lunar officials warn that transport capacity could fall by more than 60%.

---

## January 118,109 — Final Roman supply distribution

The Republic begins distributing household emergency packages.

Each citizen is entitled to a basic kit containing:

- several days of concentrated food;
- water-purification materials;
- non-electric or low-voltage lighting;
- basic pain medicine;
- bandages and antiseptics;
- local emergency instructions;
- manually readable maps;
- a simple radio receiver where available.

Lunar kits include additional:

- air-seal patches;
- pressure masks;
- carbon-dioxide absorbent;
- emergency ration tablets.

The distribution is the largest civil-protection operation in Roman history.

It also triggers panic buying.

Some citizens assume that government distribution means the situation is worse than officially stated.

Authorities impose temporary limits on private bulk purchases.

---

## March–August 118,109 — The final calm

The year before the burst becomes socially strange.

Much of Roman society continues operating, but long-term decisions are delayed.

Citizens postpone:

- marriages;
- pregnancies;
- moves;
- business launches;
- elective medical procedures;
- major construction.

Museums, libraries and public parks experience record attendance.

Religious and philosophical movements hold mass gatherings.

The central government broadcasts repeated assurances:

- the burst has been modelled;
- Class100 systems will remain functional;
- stockpiles are sufficient;
- the wave is temporary;
- Rome has survived far worse.

The assurances are accurate.

They do not eliminate fear.

# 118,110 — The Second Resonance Wave

## 28 September 118,110

### 14:53:03.4924 UTC

Exactly 2,500 years after the First Resonance Wave, and exactly 5,000 years after the thinning began, the Wound emits again.

The event begins beneath the old impact zone.

Sensors record a rapid alignment of field-memory structures followed by a coherent emission.

For **0.8 seconds**, the local field near the Wound fluctuates violently.

Then the wave expands.

Terra’s global average IRF intensity rises from approximately **50% to 60%**.

The first increase occurs within seconds.

The full global distribution stabilises over the following minutes.

Every major observatory confirms the same result.

The predicted amplitude is correct.

---

## 118,110 — The first hour

The first failures begin almost immediately.

Across Terra:

- conditional electronic switches stop functioning;
- aircraft experience navigation and engine instability;
- high-output grids shed load;
- several advanced medical scanners fail;
- Wound computational centres shut down automatically;
- automated transport reverts to manual control;
- industrial turbines lose efficiency;
- high-pressure chemical plants enter emergency shutdown.

Most Class100 systems continue normally.

The scale of disruption is nevertheless enormous because so much Roman efficiency depends on conditional systems layered above the resilient base.

The population initially remains orderly.

Then contradictory reports begin circulating.

One false broadcast claims the Wound has reopened permanently.

Another claims Luna’s transport network has collapsed.

A third claims the wave is stronger than expected.

Crowds gather at food depots and railway stations.

---

## 118,110 — The first day on Terra

Roman emergency law activates automatically.

The central government assumes temporary direct authority over provinces under the Emergency Principle.

World governments continue functioning, but provincial discretion over:

- transport;
- food;
- water;
- energy;
- medical deployment;
- evacuation

is temporarily subordinated to emergency command.

The largest problems occur in overcrowded metropolitan regions.

Many cities possess resilient infrastructure, but their population density leaves little margin.

Failures include:

- elevators and high-rise circulation systems;
- conditional water-pressure controls;
- refrigerated food distribution;
- underground transport;
- automated waste removal;
- electronic medical scheduling;
- traffic coordination.

The systems do not all fail completely.

They operate at reduced capacity.

At metropolitan scale, reduced capacity is enough to create crisis.

---

## 118,110 — The first Lunar effects

The wave reaches Luna almost immediately in astronomical terms.

Its local increase is smaller than Terra’s global rise, but stronger than Lunar planners hoped.

Conditional spacecraft guidance degrades.

Several cargo vessels abort launch.

Two older ships complete transfer using optical navigation and mechanical control.

Within Luna:

- food production falls;
- high-efficiency air circulators lose output;
- conditional medical systems fail;
- electric transport slows;
- excavation machinery shuts down;
- construction of the Lunar Great Library stops.

Luna’s Class100 compartments perform well.

No major habitat loses pressure.

The earlier monoculture reforms prevent food-system collapse.

---

## 118,110 — Mars receives the wave

Depending on orbital geometry, the wave reaches Mars minutes after Terra.

Its amplitude is modest compared with Terra’s ten-point increase, but Mars already operates near strong IRF conditions.

The controlled descent pays off.

Because Mars had already shut down its conditional machinery, the actual wave produces fewer simultaneous failures than the simulations.

Unexpected problems still emerge:

- several new terraforming instruments become unusable;
- one regional radio array loses most of its range;
- atmospheric test sites close;
- greenhouse output drops;
- newly restored settlements experience water delays.

No settlement collapses.

The Martian Confederacy’s first planetary emergency command functions successfully.

National confidence rises.

---

## 118,110 — Europa and Titan

Europa notices only a small additional increase.

Some of its few non-Class100 systems cease functioning.

Blue Vent growth slows.

The main impact is communication.

Signals from Terra and Luna weaken, and outer relays struggle to distinguish transmissions from field noise.

Titan’s internal systems show no meaningful change.

Its civilisation already operates at 100% conditions.

The only significant effect is near-collapse of interplanetary communications.

For several months, Titan receives only fragments.

---

## 118,110–118,111 — The metropolitan emergency

During the first year, Rome’s great cities experience the worst humanitarian conditions.

In some urban regions:

- food deliveries arrive late;
- water pressure becomes intermittent;
- waste accumulates;
- hospitals postpone all non-emergency care;
- old citizens cannot descend from high-rise districts;
- public transport runs at one-third capacity;
- local fires spread because pumps operate slowly.

Roman civil-protection training proves invaluable.

Students, workers and neighbourhood volunteers establish local aid networks.

Schools become:

- food centres;
- clinics;
- shelters;
- communication hubs.

The emergency does not become a general famine.

It produces millions of deaths through accumulated failures, especially among:

- the elderly;
- the chronically ill;
- people in overcrowded housing;
- residents of informal urban districts;
- citizens dependent on advanced medical devices.

The catastrophe is far smaller than the Second Breaking.

It is far worse than government models predicted.

---

## 118,111 — The foreign aid crisis

Independent Terran states suffer more severely.

Many prepared only for several weeks of disruption.

They expected Roman aid to compensate afterward.

Their systems lack Roman levels of:

- Class100 redundancy;
- food storage;
- disaster staffing;
- standardised parts;
- distributed medicine;
- mechanical fallback.

Within months, Rome receives repeated pleas for:

- grain;
- medicines;
- water equipment;
- engineers;
- transport;
- emergency finance.

The Republic sends limited assistance.

Then crises intensify at home.

The Rome First clause forces authorities to reduce foreign shipments.

Associated states protest.

One government accuses Rome of hoarding supplies it could spare.

Roman officials reply that every train sent abroad is one not serving an overcrowded Roman city.

---

## 118,111–118,114 — Aid expands and contracts

Foreign aid follows a painful cycle.

Whenever the Roman domestic situation stabilises, shipments increase.

When another internal crisis occurs, aid is reduced again.

The pattern repeats after:

- a hospital-supply shortage;
- a Terran rail failure;
- a Lunar medicine shortage;
- urban water contamination;
- food losses in a reclaimed coastal district.

This creates deep resentment outside Rome.

Independent populations conclude that association without citizenship offers limited protection during true emergency.

The political consequences will outlast the wave.

---

## 118,112 — The duration anomaly

Scientists initially expect the temporary ten-point contribution to decay over thirty years, as the First Resonance Wave did.

After eighteen months, the decline is slower than predicted.

By **118,112**, the burst contribution remains nearly as strong as models expected only months after emission.

Investigators first suspect faulty instrumentation.

Martian and Lunar measurements confirm the pattern.

The new wave is fading at approximately half the previous rate.

The temporary phase will last around **sixty years**, not thirty.

The announcement shocks the Solar System.

---

## 118,112 — Public reaction to the sixty-year wave

The idea that the disruption will last twice as long creates a second wave of panic.

Roman citizens had prepared psychologically for a severe but relatively short crisis.

Now children born after the burst may reach adulthood before normal conditions return.

Markets fall.

Birth rates decline further.

Temporary emergency arrangements begin to look semi-permanent.

The Roman government announces that:

- the background depletion model is unchanged;
- the wave will still fade;
- the long-term 25% reduction schedule remains valid;
- only the temporary burst envelope is broader.

Public trust suffers because the most confident official prediction was wrong.

---

## 118,113 — The First Urban Exodus

Millions begin leaving the largest Terran cities.

At first, movement is voluntary and chaotic.

People relocate to:

- smaller provincial cities;
- rural towns;
- restored regions;
- underpopulated provinces;
- Luna, where transport permits allow.

Roman authorities organise the movement to reduce disorder.

Emergency housing is built using durable low-energy designs.

The Equalisation Programme receives extraordinary funding.

The crisis achieves what decades of incentives could not: it begins reversing the concentration of population in Terra’s largest metropolitan centres.

---

## 118,114 — The Lunar humanitarian corridor

Despite impaired transport, Terra and Luna maintain a minimum continuous cargo corridor.

The lower-technology ships are slow and carry less.

They are reliable.

The corridor prioritises:

- rare medicines;
- replacement air-system parts;
- genetic food strains;
- essential personnel.

Luna sends Terra:

- low-gravity manufactured components;
- archive-derived medical data;
- trained civil-protection specialists.

This is the first major emergency in which Luna provides meaningful aid to Terra rather than serving only as a recipient.

It strengthens Lunar confidence and world identity.

---

## 118,115 — The Emergency City Limits Act

Roman studies conclude that population density multiplied the wave’s humanitarian cost.

The Senate passes the **Emergency City Limits Act**.

The law does not order the immediate evacuation of cities.

It establishes maximum planned settlement sizes based on:

- water resilience;
- food-storage capacity;
- emergency transport;
- hospital fallback;
- low-energy power;
- population density;
- evacuation feasibility.

Cities above their approved limits may not approve net new residential expansion unless they add sufficient independent infrastructure.

The law encourages:

- satellite towns;
- regional urban networks;
- multiple smaller centres;
- distributed employment;
- local food production.

The population of Terra’s largest cities begins a long, steady decline.

---

## 118,116–118,125 — The Great Redistribution

Roman citizens move in unprecedented numbers.

Government incentives support relocation to:

- underpopulated Terran provinces;
- restored rural regions;
- newly developed mid-sized cities;
- Luna.

The movement is legally voluntary.

Economic reality strongly encourages it.

Overcrowded-city residents receive:

- housing guarantees;
- transport;
- employment placement;
- civic-income supplements;
- education access;
- relocation payments.

Many provincial regions welcome the newcomers.

Others fear cultural disruption and rising land prices.

The government imposes local infrastructure requirements so settlements cannot accept more residents than their services support.

---

## 118,117 — Lunar migration boom

Once Lunar authorities confirm that the world’s Class100 systems can tolerate the new wave, Lunar immigration expands.

Terran families view Luna as:

- less crowded;
- highly organised;
- well supplied;
- politically stable;
- protected by strict habitat law.

Ancient Lunar settlements are restored at unprecedented speed.

The World of Luna opens:

- old agricultural caverns;
- transport tunnels;
- buried habitation districts;
- sealed Industrial Age reservoirs.

The percentage of Lunar residents born on Terra rises sharply.

Permanent Latin becomes even more dominant because most migrants come from metropolitan regions where it is their only everyday language.

---

## 118,118 — The Seventh and Eighth Lunar provinces

Two restored ancient settlement networks become Roman provinces.

One is populated primarily by long-established Lunar citizens.

The other is populated overwhelmingly by Terran migrants.

The migrant-founded province grows faster.

Lunar-born commentators worry that Luna may eventually become culturally more Terran than Lunar.

No major protests occur.

The scale of the Terran humanitarian crisis makes open resistance socially unacceptable.

Lunar institutions instead require all newcomers to study:

- Lunar history;
- habitat safety;
- low-gravity health;
- local civic customs.

---

## 118,119 — Martian criticism of Roman influence

Mars survives the wave well, but political debate intensifies.

A growing group argues that the Confederacy has become too Roman in:

- administrative language;
- safety classifications;
- educational structure;
- constitutional design;
- crisis planning;
- legal terminology.

These critics do not oppose:

- the Martian Dream;
- Class100 exchange;
- good relations with Rome;
- Permanent Latin as an external language.

They want Mars to develop a more distinct national culture.

The movement calls itself **Red Particularism**.

Its slogan is:

> “We learned from Rome. We are not Rome.”

---

## 118,120–118,130 — Martian cultural consolidation

The Confederacy responds constructively.

It increases funding for:

- local Martian languages;
- Martian literature;
- settlement histories;
- planet-specific law;
- distinctive public ceremonies;
- terraformation festivals.

Permanent Latin remains the confederal administrative and interworld language.

Martian schools increasingly use local languages alongside it.

The Martian Dream remains the central binding force.

Red Particularism strengthens Martian identity without substantially weakening Roman relations.

---

## 118,121 — The Titanian language proposal

At the University of Algarath, linguists publish a constructed language derived from the most widely spoken Titanian languages.

The language is designed to be:

- easy to learn;
- compatible with Titanian speech patterns;
- precise in engineering and ecological terminology;
- neutral between major cavern cultures.

It is called **Common Titanian**, usually shortened to **Titanian**.

Supporters propose it for:

- inter-cavern trade;
- shared institutions;
- Titan-wide disaster communication;
- external cultural identity.

Conservative caverns object that a constructed language has no ancestral legitimacy.

Others fear it will displace local speech.

---

## 118,123–118,128 — The Titanian language debate

The debate becomes a planetary cultural conflict.

Supporters initially struggle.

Then politicians reframe the proposal.

They argue that without a common Titanian language, Permanent Latin will inevitably become Titan’s shared language.

The choice is presented not as:

- constructed language versus tradition,

but as:

- Titanian linguistic unity versus gradual Latinisation.

This changes public opinion.

The Titan External Office adopts Common Titanian experimentally.

The Disaster Compact uses it in standard emergency messages.

---

## 118,129 — Common Titanian adopted

The Titan-wide institutions approve Common Titanian as their principal internal working language.

Permanent Latin remains the language of Solar communication.

Local cavern languages retain full internal use.

Unlike Permanent Latin, Common Titanian is not frozen.

A language council records changes but does not prevent evolution.

The decision increases the influence of Algarath dramatically.

The University of Algarath receives:

- research funding;
- private sponsorship;
- archive grants;
- language-teaching contracts.

It becomes Titan’s most prestigious university over the following decades.

---

## 118,131 — Algarath and Zorath begin integration

The large settlement of Algarath lies close to the smaller cavern polity of Zorath.

Language cooperation leads to:

- shared universities;
- medical exchange;
- transport tunnels;
- student migration;
- common archival institutions.

The two governments establish a joint council.

Physical and political integration proceeds more cautiously than at Orenth-Marellon.

---

## 118,135 — The Roman water cascade crisis

On Terra, a sequence of reduced-power pumping failures affects several connected river systems.

One province diverts water to preserve urban supply.

Downstream agricultural provinces experience shortages.

The shortage reduces food output.

Food prices rise in nearby independent states already suffering from the wave.

Rome sends emergency grain domestically under the Rome First clause.

Foreign governments accuse Rome of aggravating their famine.

The Terran Waters Council attempts mediation but has limited authority during Roman emergency rule.

This event links urban overcrowding, domestic prioritisation and foreign collapse into one long crisis chain.

---

## 118,137 — The Independent States Appeal

The remaining non-Roman states issue a joint appeal.

They request:

- guaranteed aid quotas;
- access to Roman Class100 stockpiles;
- emergency entry into Roman markets;
- expedited accession negotiations.

Rome provides limited guarantees of food and medicine but rejects automatic access to strategic stores.

The central government argues that accepting new states during the emergency would create obligations Rome cannot safely meet.

The refusal is increasingly unpopular even inside Rome.

Moderate world unionists argue that associated populations should not be abandoned simply because they lack Roman citizenship.

---

## 118,140 — Roman emergency control begins to recede

The most acute domestic failures have stabilised.

The central government starts returning direct authority to world and provincial governments.

Emergency rule remains in place in:

- several overcrowded Terran regions;
- the Wound;
- Lunar transport;
- major food-distribution corridors.

The return of local authority reduces political tension.

Investigations later find that central intervention prevented several collapses but also caused delays when officials ignored local knowledge.

The Roman Worlds system survives its first major test.

---

## 118,143 — Class50 policy is drafted

Roman engineers begin preparing for the post-wave era.

Once the burst contribution fades and Terra’s background remains safely below 50%, Rome intends to authorise broader use of **Class50 systems**.

A Class50 system must operate safely at 50% IRF intensity with a defined margin.

The proposed policy permits Class50 equipment in:

- communications;
- transport;
- manufacturing;
- medicine;
- some public infrastructure.

Critical systems may use Class50 components only if:

- adequate Class100 redundancy exists;
- failure cannot cause immediate mass casualties;
- no Class100 alternative is technically possible;
- recovery procedures are mechanically available.

Luna remains exempt until its own local intensity falls below the threshold for a stable long period.

---

## 118,145 — The Wound Sector proposal

The Wound’s special technological conditions become increasingly important.

The government proposes removing the region from ordinary provincial status and converting it into a directly governed scientific sector.

Reasons include:

- hazardous experiments;
- unique IRF conditions;
- security;
- planetary computational infrastructure;
- need for strict access control.

Residents object.

They argue that millions of ordinary citizens live there and should not lose normal provincial government because scientists value the land.

The debate becomes one of the century’s most controversial domestic issues.

---

## 118,147 — The Wound Compensation Act

Rome offers residents generous terms:

- full relocation costs;
- housing in underpopulated provinces;
- Lunar settlement priority;
- lifetime income supplements;
- compensation above property value;
- guaranteed education and employment.

Those who remain must accept:

- restricted access zones;
- experimental-risk law;
- emergency evacuation obligations;
- limited local development rights.

Critics accuse Rome of removing a civilian population to build a state technological enclave.

Supporters argue that no other location can support frontier experimentation safely.

---

## 118,150 — The Special Scientific Sector of the Wound

The reform passes.

The Wound becomes a non-civilian special sector under direct Roman Central Republic control.

Ordinary provincial institutions are dissolved gradually.

A civilian municipal system remains for residents who choose not to relocate, but it has limited authority.

Construction begins on several **Frontier Computational Centres**.

These centres use:

- dense electronics;
- high-output cooling;
- advanced archival search;
- medical simulation;
- engineering modelling;
- planetary climate computation.

Low-energy backup computation remains distributed elsewhere.

The Wound is intended to enhance, not replace, the Republic’s resilient systems.

---

## 118,151–118,160 — The Wound network

The first computational centres come online.

Their capacity benefits:

- Terra Restituta modelling;
- drug research;
- Lunar archaeology;
- Martian atmospheric studies;
- asteroid tracking;
- Great Library indexing.

Luna receives reduced benefit because of communication latency and lower bandwidth.

Associated states receive limited access.

Fully independent states are excluded except during planetary emergencies.

Critics argue Rome is creating a computational hierarchy between citizens and outsiders.

The government cites finite capacity and security.

## 118,153 — Haven founds the SSLI

In the Harbour System, settlers establish the **Search for Solar Life Institute**, or **SSLI**.

Harbour lies approximately **2,180 light-years from Sol**.

The Institute begins repeated transmissions toward the Solar System.

Signals use multiple formats:

- simple pulses;
- binary sequences;
- mathematical constants;
- prime numbers;
- Morse-like timing;
- visual and radio modulation;
- languages common at the Second Breaking;
- old Solar technical standards.

The earliest signals transmitted in **118,153** cannot reach Sol before approximately **120,333**.

Harbour expects no reply within any living person’s lifetime.

Even if Sol responds immediately upon reception, a reply could not return before roughly **122,513**.

SSLI therefore treats the transmissions as messages to unknown descendants.

## 118,154–118,164 — Haven’s greening problem

Haven’s ecological transformation proceeds much more slowly than expected.

Although the planet has suitable:

- gravity;
- temperature;
- surface water;
- pressure range,

it lacks a naturally evolved biosphere.

The settlers discover several obstacles.

### Soil chemistry

Many soils contain minerals toxic to Terran-derived organisms.

### Nutrient imbalance

Nitrogen and phosphorus are unevenly distributed.

### Atmospheric composition

The atmosphere can support modified organisms locally but not broad Terran ecosystems without long adjustment.

### Ultraviolet exposure

Haven’s ozone-equivalent layer is weak.

### Microbial instability

Introduced microbial communities repeatedly collapse because no ancient co-evolved ecological networks exist.

### Ocean chemistry

The oceans contain unusual dissolved-metal concentrations that inhibit many algae.

High-energy technology accelerates local intervention.

It cannot instantly create millions of years of ecological coevolution.

Haven’s settlers realise they must build ecosystems layer by layer.

## 118,158 — Haven’s first stable soil basin

After repeated failure, SSLI-affiliated ecological teams establish a large stable soil-development zone.

They use:

- engineered microbes;
- mineral washing;
- enclosed nutrient cycles;
- atmospheric shielding;
- extreme genetic diversity;
- controlled succession.

The basin remains small compared with a planet.

It proves reliable greening is possible.

Haven adopts a slower terraformation model based on ecological mosaics rather than continent-scale rapid transformation.

## 118,161 — Solar probe programme approved

Harbour authorises twelve autonomous probes toward Sol:

- three toward Terra;

- three toward Jupiter;
- three toward Saturn.

Their mission is to reach Solar worlds and announce that Harbour humanity survives.

Each carries:

## 118,164–118,169 — Probe design

The probes use the most advanced Harbour technology available for initial acceleration.

Before nearing the IRF frontier, they will decelerate toward a velocity compatible with long-duration operation and final capture.

Their internal architecture has two layers.

### High-energy transit layer

Used in IRF-free space for:

- navigation;
- acceleration;
- high-density computation;
- autonomous repair.

### IRF-ready core

Designed to operate under full-strength IRF using:

- low-voltage electronics;
- mechanical timing;
- optical navigation;
- passive memory;
- low-thrust backup engines;
- large safety margins.

Near Sol, the probes are intended to use:

- centuries of low-thrust braking;
- planetary gravity assists;
- atmospheric drag where appropriate;
- orbital capture manoeuvres.

Estimated travel times are extremely long.

Depending on the route and safe entry velocity, the probes may require roughly **35,000–60,000 years** to reach their targets.

None is guaranteed to survive.

## 118,170 — The burst returns to baseline

Sixty years after emission, the temporary contribution on Terra fades to approximately zero.

The global average returns to the smooth background curve predicted before the burst.

Terra’s average IRF intensity is now approximately **49.4%**.

The Second Resonance Wave is over locally.

Its social and political effects are not.

The sixty-year duration confirms that resonance waves are broadening over time.

Scientists predict that later waves may persist even longer, despite the unchanged long-term depletion schedule.

## 118,171 — End of Roman systemic emergency

The Roman Central Republic ends the general state of emergency.

Direct central control of provinces ceases except in:

- the Wound Sector;
- a few disaster zones;
- limited Lunar transport operations.

The end is marked by public ceremonies.

The official death toll from the burst period remains disputed.

Across Roman territory, direct and indirect excess deaths likely number in the **tens of millions**, potentially more when delayed medical and demographic effects are counted.

This is catastrophic by modern Roman standards but far below what would have occurred without Class100 investment.

## 118,172 — The Class50 Transition Act

With Terra safely below 50% IRF intensity, Rome authorises controlled Class50 deployment.

Approved areas include:

- civilian computation;
- medical diagnostics;
- industrial control;
- high-capacity transport;
- urban communication;
- some water and energy systems.

Every critical Class50 installation must be paired with:

- Class100 fallback;
- manual control;
- local isolation;
- stored replacement parts;
- documented degraded mode.

The policy accelerates Roman technological growth.

It also formalises a layered civilisation:

- Class50 for efficiency;
- Class100 for survival.

Luna remains under stricter rules because its local intensity and wave behaviour have not yet stabilised below the required threshold.

## 118,174 — Foreign devastation becomes clear

As Roman emergency restrictions ease, investigators assess the remaining independent states.

Several have suffered:

- major population loss;
- industrial collapse;
- food-system damage;
- public-health decline;
- mass emigration;
- political unrest.

Repeated accession requests reach Rome.

Some governments submit requests annually.

Others offer immediate constitutional surrender in exchange for aid.

Rome is still recovering and refuses collective accession.

It guarantees that applications will be reconsidered when Roman stability and administrative capacity improve.

The decision is justified under Rome First.

Foreign publics increasingly regard accession as their only plausible long-term recovery path.

## 118,175 — The Great Refusal

The Roman government issues a single collective response to all pending accession applications.

It states:

- no applicant is permanently rejected;
- emergency aid will increase as capacity permits;
- formal accession is suspended;
- constitutional review will resume in the next stable period;
- associated states will receive priority assistance.

The policy prevents Rome from acquiring territories it cannot support.

It leaves millions outside Roman protection.

Moderate world unionists call it a moral failure forced by practical limits.

Hardliners call it proof that Terra should have unified earlier.

## 118,176–118,190 — The aid recovery

- water-treatment systems;
- engineers;
- public-health teams;
- school materials.

Aid remains below foreign need.

Independent states become increasingly administratively dependent on Roman agencies.

The Rome First clause remains controversial but politically secure. Roman citizens broadly support the principle after experiencing the burst.

## 118,178 — Luna’s post-wave demographic transformation

Luna’s population exceeds half a billion.

Permanent Latin dominates daily public life.

Older Lunar dialects remain strongest in established provinces.

The World of Luna funds cultural-preservation programmes to prevent migration from erasing its pre-burst identity.

Tension remains manageable.

Most Lunar citizens recognise that migration prevented a larger humanitarian crisis on Terra and accelerated Lunar growth.

## 118,184 — Novaterra’s Concord failure

On Novaterra, two neighbouring states accuse one another of causing a shared ecological disaster.

The crisis begins when a river ecosystem collapses across their border.

One government blames upstream nutrient extraction.

The other blames an introduced fungal species released downstream.

Evidence suggests both contributed.

The Novaterran Concord attempts mediation.

Both states reject its findings.

Border forces mobilise.

The Concord lacks enforcement capacity and cannot prevent several armed clashes.

A bilateral treaty eventually ends the fighting without meaningful Concord involvement.

The failure reduces the Concord’s prestige.

Several states withdraw powers previously delegated to it.

Planetary ecological coordination weakens at the moment Novaterra’s expanding biosphere requires more of it.

# The century’s meaning

The Second Resonance Wave proves that Rome’s caution was justified.

It also proves that caution was incomplete.

Class100 machinery survives. Food reserves exist. Civil-protection training works. Luna holds. Mars performs exceptionally.

Yet cities still choke because too many people depend on too few transport lines. Hospitals still fail because resilient systems cannot provide the same capacity as conditional ones. Foreign states still collapse because association offers no guarantee equal to citizenship.

The wave does not destroy Roman civilisation.

It changes its geography, politics and priorities.

Terra begins dispersing its population.

Luna becomes a mass destination rather than a frontier.

Mars emerges from the wave more confident, more populous and more distinctly Martian.

Titan chooses its own common language.

Haven sends messages into a darkness that will not answer for millennia.

Humanity enters the post-wave era knowing something new:

the resonance cycles are changing.

The old models still predict the long decline correctly.

But every future burst may linger longer than the last.

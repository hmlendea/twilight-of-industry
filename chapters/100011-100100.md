# 100011-100100 — The Hidden Rule

This is where the story takes a dramatic turn.

Up until now, this civilisation has spent **100,000 years perfecting stability**. It has never experienced a technological revolution because, in this universe, a subtle law of physics prevented one.

## The hidden rule

Let's define that rule.

For reasons unknown to humanity, Earth lies within an extremely rare region of spacetime—a relic from the early universe.

Inside this region, a physical effect later called the **Inertial Resonance Field (IRF)** exists.

The IRF has one peculiar property:

> **Highly concentrated energy systems become catastrophically unstable beyond a certain density.**

The effect is tiny.

It doesn't affect:
- candles
- water wheels
- windmills
- horses
- local electrical generators

It *does* affect:
- large steam boilers
- internal combustion engines
- large electrical grids
- high-pressure turbines
- precision semiconductor fabrication
- industrial chemical plants

Every civilisation on Earth eventually discovers these technologies... and every one of them mysteriously fails in ways no theory can explain.

The IRF prevents the positive feedback loop that leads to industrialisation.

No one ever suspects a universal law.

It simply appears that "large-scale machinery never quite works."

# 13 March 100,011

Astronomers notice something impossible.

A faint object beyond Jupiter.

Initially thought to be a comet.

Within weeks...

It isn't.

Its orbit intersects Earth.

Diameter:

**~18 km**

Velocity:

**23 km/s**

Impact energy:

Roughly **100 million megatons TNT**

Comparable to—or greater than—the Chicxulub impact.

Civilisation has about:

**18 years**

---

# The Great Calculation

Humanity has never faced an existential threat on this scale.

The greatest mechanical computers ever built begin working continuously.

Every observatory repeats the calculations.

After months...

Every answer agrees.

Impact probability:

**99.99998%**

---

# The First Century of Fear

For the first time in tens of thousands of years...

Human civilisation experiences genuine panic.

Not because governments collapse.

But because there is **nothing to be done.**

They know orbital mechanics perfectly.

They know exactly how an asteroid could be deflected.

They simply cannot build:
- rockets
- nuclear explosives
- mass drivers

The irony is crushing.

---

# The Great Preservation

Humanity changes priorities almost overnight.

No longer asking:

> "How do we improve civilisation?"

Instead asking:

> "How do we ensure civilisation survives?"

The next eighteen years become known as

**The Preservation.**

---

Entire mountain ranges become archives.

Libraries are copied thousands of times.

Seeds are stored.

Animals are relocated.

Ancient manuscripts are engraved into:
- granite
- sapphire
- ceramic plates

Entire universities move underground.

---

# The Underground Cities

Thousands of caverns are expanded.

Not with giant machines.

With generations of miners.

Every nation builds:
- underground hospitals
- food stores
- reservoirs
- archives

Nobody expects to save everyone.

Only enough.

---

# The Final Years

The asteroid becomes visible.

First:

A moving star.

Then...

A tiny disc.

Eventually...

Visible during the daytime.

Children grow up with it hanging in the sky.

Poets call it:

"The Second Moon."

---

# Society changes

Crime almost disappears.

Wars stop.

Borders become irrelevant.

For perhaps the first time in history...

Humanity truly becomes one civilisation.

---

# The Last Month

Governments cease ordinary business.

Schools teach only:
- practical survival
- preservation of knowledge
- emergency medicine

Every city rings bells.

Every religion holds continuous services.

Many people simply gather with family.

---

# Impact Day

The asteroid strikes the Pacific Ocean.

Thousands of kilometres from major land.

The impact excavates the crust.

Water briefly leaves the basin.

A fireball larger than continents forms.

Within hours:
- magnitude 11 earthquakes
- kilometre-high tsunamis
- global firestorms
- ejecta rains across the planet

Entire coastlines disappear.

---

# The hidden mechanism

At the instant of impact...

Something else happens.

The asteroid is not ordinary.

It originated from outside the IRF bubble.

Its immense kinetic energy tears the distorted spacetime region surrounding Earth.

The Inertial Resonance Field collapses.

For the first time in perhaps billions of years...

Earth now obeys exactly the same laws of physics as the rest of the universe.

Nobody notices.

At first.

---

# Year +1

Half the population has died.

Many cities vanish.

Agriculture collapses.

The sky remains dark.

Ash blocks sunlight.

The survivors retreat underground.

---

# Year +5

The ash begins clearing.

People cautiously return.

Much knowledge survives.

Not because they were lucky.

Because they had spent eighteen years preparing.

Libraries emerge intact.

Universities reopen.

---

# Year +12

Something strange happens.

An engineer repairing a hydroelectric generator notices...

The turbine can now rotate much faster.

Previously, vibration would suddenly become uncontrollable.

Now...

Nothing happens.

It simply keeps accelerating.

He assumes older calculations were wrong.

---

# Year +20

Several independent discoveries occur.

Steam boilers no longer fail unpredictably.

Large dynamos remain stable.

High-pressure metallurgy suddenly works.

Nobody understands why.

People merely observe:

"The old limits are gone."

---

# The Second Revolution begins

At first...

Nobody calls it industrialisation.

They simply notice:

Bigger machines work.

Larger generators work.

More powerful engines work.

Factories become practical.

---

# The first locomotive

Year 100,038.

An experimental railway opens.

The engine performs perfectly.

Historians later describe the spectators as confused.

Not amazed.

Confused.

Because every previous attempt throughout history had mysteriously failed.

Now...

It simply works.

---

# The acceleration

This civilisation possesses something ours never had:

**100,000 years of accumulated scientific knowledge.**

Within decades they already understand:
- thermodynamics
- electromagnetism
- chemistry
- aerodynamics

They don't need to *discover* these fields.

They merely begin applying them.

---

# The first fifty years

Their equivalent of our:

1700–1900

happens in about

**40 years.**

---

Steam.

Railways.

Steel.

Mass production.

National electrical grids.

Industrial chemistry.

---

# The first computer

Unlike ours...

They skip vacuum tubes almost entirely.

Their electromechanical reasoning engines already exist.

Once industrial manufacturing becomes possible...

Semiconductors appear astonishingly quickly.

The first transistor is invented only about twenty years after practical integrated steel production.

---

# Spaceflight

Remember...

They have known orbital mechanics for ninety thousand years.

Within fifty years:
- liquid rockets
- satellites
- orbital stations

appear almost simultaneously.

---

# Society

This is where things become extraordinary.

Unlike nineteenth-century Earth...

This civilisation already possesses:
- universal literacy
- global cooperation
- mature democracies
- environmental ethics
- millennia of legal refinement
- deep historical awareness

They industrialise without child labour, imperial competition, or the scientific uncertainty that shaped our own Industrial Revolution.

---

# The Great Question

Eventually physicists discover evidence of the former Inertial Resonance Field.

By studying rocks altered by the impact and comparing physical constants recorded in ancient experiments, they realise something astonishing:

> The laws of nature themselves changed.

Their ancestors were not incompetent.

Steam engines genuinely *couldn't* scale.

Industrial chemistry genuinely *was* constrained.

The ceiling had been real.

---

# The Long-term consequence

Historians eventually divide human history into three eras:

**The Long Age**

(0–100,029)

One hundred thousand years of stable, pre-industrial civilisation.

---

**The Ash Years**

(100,029–100,045)

The catastrophe, survival, and recovery.

---

**The Great Release**

(100,045 onward)

Humanity experiences, compressed into perhaps two centuries, what took our civilisation nearly a millennium: the transition from muscle power to industrial power.

The difference is that they begin that journey not as a fragmented, often illiterate world, but as one with 100,000 years of accumulated scholarship, extraordinarily stable institutions, and a deeply ingrained ethic of long-term stewardship. Their first factories are built by people who already understand sustainability, systems thinking, and planetary history on timescales that we can scarcely imagine.

To them, the Industrial Revolution is not the dawn of civilisation. It is the removal of an invisible ceiling that had constrained civilisation since the beginning of humanity. It feels less like inventing something new, and more like finally being allowed to use knowledge their ancestors had patiently accumulated for one hundred millennia.

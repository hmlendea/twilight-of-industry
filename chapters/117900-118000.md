## 117,901 — The Roman Horizon Project is proposed

The Roman Executive Consulate proposes what would have been the Republic’s largest project since Terra Restituta.

It is called the **Horizon Project**.

Its goal is to create a chain of permanent artificial islands, oceanic cities and deep-sea infrastructure across Terra’s major oceans.

The system would serve as:

- climate and ocean observatories;
- asteroid-tracking stations;
- relay hubs;
- emergency refugee centres;
- marine restoration bases;
- intercontinental transport nodes;
- submerged-city archaeological stations;
- future launch and recovery platforms.

Supporters argue that the oceans contain most of Terra’s ungoverned surface and must become part of the Republic’s long-term civilisational infrastructure.

The projected construction period is eight hundred years.

The initial cost alone would exceed several decades of expenditure on the Great Libraries, Luna and Terra Restituta combined.

---

## 117,903 — The Senate rejects the Horizon Project

After two years of debate, the Senate rejects the proposal.

The vote is not close.

Many senators who support the project’s goals vote against it.

Their reasons differ.

### The Overstretch faction

It argues that Rome already maintains:

- Terra Restituta;
- Great Libraries;
- biosphere restoration;
- Lunar expansion;
- orbital infrastructure;
- Class100 research;
- planetary defence;
- offworld communications;
- regional equalisation;
- buried-city restoration.

Beginning another multi-century project would dilute labour, money and political attention.

### The Civic First faction

It argues that the Republic has become fascinated by grand civilisational projects while ordinary citizens still face:

- housing shortages;
- medical waiting times;
- regional inequality;
- ageing infrastructure;
- expensive certification;
- overcrowded cities.

Its central slogan is:

> “The Republic exists for its citizens, not its monuments to eternity.”

### The Constitutional faction

It fears that perpetual projects are gradually removing meaningful choices from future generations by constitutionally committing their resources before they are born.

The Horizon Project is shelved.

This is the first time in centuries that a great Roman project is rejected outright rather than delayed or reduced.

---

## 117,904–117,910 — The Civic Rebalancing

The rejection changes Roman politics.

The government redirects part of the proposed Horizon funding toward:

- housing;
- public clinics;
- urban transport;
- rural schools;
- water-system replacement;
- support for ageing citizens;
- maintenance of existing infrastructure.

The result is visible within a decade.

Average waiting times for non-emergency medical care fall.

Housing construction increases.

Several poorer provinces achieve Roman median living standards for the first time.

The World Union movement condemns the decision as timid.

Preservationists are divided: some fear the Republic is abandoning long-term vision, while others argue that preserving public trust is itself a continuity requirement.

---

## 117,906 — Titan’s Near Caverns agreement

On Titan, the caverns of **Orenth Deep** and **Marellon Basin** lie only thirty-four kilometres apart.

Together they contain roughly **2.6 million people**.

For centuries, they have traded heat, fungal cultures, machinery and water through narrow insulated tunnels.

A new agreement creates permanent physical links between them.

The initial infrastructure includes:

- two pressurised passenger tunnels;
- a shared water conduit;
- a heat-balancing system;
- a freight railway;
- redundant emergency barriers.

The systems are designed according to the Titanian principle of **connected separability**.

Every connection can be sealed mechanically if contamination or pressure loss occurs.

---

## 117,909 — The Orenth–Marellon political merger

Economic integration produces political pressure.

Residents increasingly cross between the caverns for work, marriage and education. Separate regulations become burdensome.

After parallel referenda, the two governments merge into the **Orenth-Marellon Commonwealth**.

The Commonwealth is Titan’s largest single political unit.

Its leaders plan to develop the entire region between the caverns as one continuous settlement.

The projected area includes:

- new agricultural chambers;
- thermal reservoirs;
- residential tunnels;
- fungal forests;
- medical districts;
- shared archives;
- industrial caverns.

Supporters believe the merged system can support far more people than the two isolated caverns could separately because waste heat, biological nutrients and labour can be balanced regionally.

Critics warn that scale may create new systemic risks.

---

## 117,912 — Titan’s Expansion Ring begins

The Commonwealth starts excavating the **Expansion Ring**, a broad underground arc intended eventually to connect both caverns through multiple routes.

The first phase succeeds.

Unused heat from Orenth warms agricultural chambers closer to Marellon.

Marellon’s surplus water stabilises Orenth’s fungal production.

Food output rises by 11% in seven years.

Population growth accelerates.

The Commonwealth becomes a model for carefully integrated Titanian settlement.

---

## 117,914 — Mars’s restoration conflict

Mars’s programme of resettling abandoned habitats creates growing political tension.

Older settlements provide much of the funding and expertise.

Newly restored settlements complain that they are treated as dependent colonies.

Disputes arise over:

- control of recovered water systems;
- ownership of ancient archives;
- settlement representation;
- migration quotas;
- emergency resource obligations;
- the location of the Martian Covenant’s central institutions.

The Covenant has become too important to remain merely an agreement among sovereign settlements, but not strong enough to settle these disputes.

---

## 117,916 — The Noctis food dispute

The restored settlement of **Noctis Minor**, whose Class100 systems had survived the great solar flare, develops a highly productive low-light grain.

The strain becomes essential to several other settlements.

Noctis attempts to restrict distribution in exchange for water rights.

Other settlements invoke the Covenant’s prohibition on monopolising survival-critical biological resources.

Noctis refuses to comply.

For six months, Mars faces its most serious inter-settlement confrontation in centuries.

No weapons are used, but freight routes close and emergency reserves are mobilised.

Rome offers mediation through the Solar Relay Assembly.

The dispute ends when the strain is placed under a common Martian biological trust.

The incident convinces many Martians that voluntary coordination is no longer sufficient.

---

## 117,919 — The Martian Constitutional Circles

Political organisations across Mars begin discussing planetary union.

They are called **Constitutional Circles**.

The Circles hold slow inter-settlement debates through radio, relay and physical delegations.

Three broad visions emerge.

### The Confederates

They want one Martian government with strong settlement autonomy.

### The Planetarians

They want a central state powerful enough to coordinate terraforming and infrastructure.

### The Covenant Traditionalists

They fear centralisation could endanger settlements by forcing them into uniform systems.

The debate remains peaceful, but increasingly intense.

---

## 117,921 — The Fleeing Ship’s first capacity warning

Beyond the IRF frontier, the Fleeing Ship’s population reaches an estimated **2.7 million**.

Its original design population was approximately 1.4 million.

Later habitat expansions increased theoretical capacity to 2.4 million, but several expansions were temporary structures never intended for permanent settlement.

The ship’s management systems issue a capacity warning.

Warning signs include:

- declining water-reserve margins;
- reduced agricultural redundancy;
- longer air-processing cycles;
- rising maintenance backlogs;
- overcrowded residential sectors;
- increased disease transmission;
- stress on rotational bearings.

The government introduces voluntary birth reduction and housing restrictions.

The public reaction is limited. Most people assume further expansion will solve the problem.

---

## 117,923 — Rome’s first 50%-threshold experiments

Near the Wound of Return, local IRF intensity falls below 40%, while several experimental zones elsewhere approach machinery behaviour consistent with a global intensity near 50%.

Roman engineers begin testing technologies expected to become widely reliable once Terra crosses that threshold.

These include:

- compact electronic switching arrays;
- higher-pressure turbines;
- lighter reusable orbital stages;
- advanced synthetic materials;
- dense magnetic memory;
- more capable medical scanners.

Results are promising but unstable.

The Continuity Authorities refuse widespread deployment.

Independent states, especially Orassia, move faster.

This widens the gap between Roman conditional capability and Roman legal permission.

---

## 117,925 — The Orassian medical breakthrough

Orassian researchers develop a compact electronic diagnostic system capable of identifying several cancers and organ diseases much earlier than Roman electromechanical systems.

The device fails under stronger IRF conditions, but under ordinary local conditions it performs exceptionally well.

Roman health authorities initially prohibit its use in public hospitals.

Public outrage follows.

Patients demand access.

After intense debate, Rome creates a new category:

### Conditional medical technology

Such devices may be used if:

- a Class100-compatible diagnostic alternative remains available;
- no patient depends solely on the conditional system;
- failure cannot directly harm the patient;
- the system is isolated from critical hospital control networks.

This compromise allows faster innovation without abandoning redundancy.

It becomes a major reform of Roman regulation.

---

## 117,928 — Terra’s ninth remaining independent state joins Rome

A former neutral state with 170 million inhabitants votes to join the Republic.

Its accession reduces Terra’s sovereign-state count to ten.

Rome now governs approximately **96% of Terra’s population**.

The remaining independent states are:

- three Serevan Cooperative Union members;
- Orassia;
- five smaller republics or federations.

Most rely heavily on Roman trade, healthcare standards and planetary defence.

The World Union movement declares planetary political unity inevitable.

---

## 117,930 — The Ship’s bearing crisis

The Fleeing Ship’s primary forward rotational bearing begins producing irregular vibration.

The ship’s artificial-gravity habitat is built around multiple rotating sections. The forward bearing supports nearly one-quarter of the inhabited volume.

Engineers discover:

- severe material fatigue;
- microscopic fracture propagation;
- lubricant contamination;
- distortion caused by decades of overloading.

A replacement assembly was intended to be manufactured by an industrial module that no longer exists. That module had been dismantled generations earlier during another emergency.

The ship cannot produce several necessary components to original specification.

The government activates the original **Foundational Emergency Protocol** for the first time since departure.

---

## 117,930 — Foundational Emergency Protocol

The Protocol imposes immediate control over nearly every aspect of shipboard life.

Measures include:

- suspension of nonessential industry;
- mandatory power rationing;
- closure of high-load habitat sectors;
- compulsory relocation;
- prohibition of births;
- centralised food distribution;
- emergency labour assignment;
- cancellation of civilian travel between sections;
- military control of key engineering spaces.

The public had known the Protocol existed, but few believed it would ever be invoked.

Rumours spread that the ship is dying.

Panic erupts.

Crowds rush food stores.

Several transit corridors are overwhelmed.

In one habitat, a false decompression alarm causes a stampede that kills more than 3,000 people.

---

## 117,931 — The Fleeing Ship’s repair

Engineers cannot fully restore the bearing.

They manufacture a lower-capacity replacement structure using available materials.

The repair works, but the affected rotating section must operate at:

- lower rotational speed;
- reduced population;
- lower freight load;
- lower artificial gravity.

Two nearby residential districts are permanently evacuated.

Several missing high-precision components cannot be manufactured aboard the ship.

The bearing can remain functional for centuries if never again subjected to previous loads.

Its former capacity is lost permanently.

The emergency ends after eleven months.

The Foundational Protocol is formally suspended.

Reproductive restrictions remain.

---

## 117,932–117,938 — The Shipwide Protests

The repair does not restore public confidence.

Protests spread throughout the ship.

Different movements demand incompatible solutions.

### The Settlement Now movement

Find the nearest habitable planet and stop permanently.

### The Return Inquiry

Turn toward the weakened IRF shell and investigate whether returning is possible.

### The Divergence movement

Change course toward a region with better candidate stars.

### The Harbour Plan

Stop temporarily at a planet, moon or asteroid system to gather resources and manufacture missing parts.

### The Division movement

Build new ships and divide the population among them.

### The Continuation faction

Maintain course and impose permanent population control.

Many protesters support no specific proposal. They simply reject endless overcrowded flight.

The ship’s government agrees to a formal review of all possible futures.

---

## 117,934 — Mars’s Aonia emergency

On Mars, a chain of dust-driven electrostatic storms damages exposed communication towers across the Aonia settlements.

The failure isolates six settlements.

One settlement falsely believes a water failure elsewhere has contaminated shared shipments and closes its transport tunnels.

Another interprets the closure as political retaliation.

The crisis reveals how easily fragmented authority can turn incomplete information into conflict.

The Martian Covenant eventually restores communication.

No war occurs.

The incident greatly strengthens the Confederates.

---

## 117,936 — The Olympus initiative

The governors of twelve major Martian settlements issue a joint invitation for a planetary constitutional convention.

They choose **Olympus Mons** because:

- it is not controlled by any major settlement bloc;
- its ancient facilities are historically significant;
- its elevated location has symbolic meaning;
- restored pressure halls can host large delegations.

The convention is scheduled for 117,940.

Every recognised settlement is invited.

Rome, Luna, Europa and Titan are asked to send messages of observation but not political direction.

---

## 117,938 — Titan’s merged infrastructure crisis

The Orenth-Marellon Commonwealth opens a new shared agricultural chamber before all isolation barriers are complete.

A fungal crop disease appears in the chamber.

The disease spreads into both old cavern systems through the newly integrated nutrient network.

Emergency mechanical seals stop the infection before it reaches the principal food caverns.

Food losses are severe but manageable.

The crisis validates both sides of the merger debate:

- integration created the pathway for spread;
- shared resources allowed rapid replacement of lost food.

The Commonwealth halts expansion for three years and redesigns the network around smaller ecological cells.

The merger survives.

---

## 117,940 — The Olympus Convention begins

Delegates from sixty-three Martian settlements meet at Olympus Mons.

Because travel remains difficult, some delegations require more than a year to arrive.

The convention debates:

- planetary citizenship;
- settlement sovereignty;
- common taxation;
- ecological law;
- military authority;
- terraforming;
- language;
- migration;
- ownership of ancient infrastructure.

Permanent Latin is used for inter-settlement formal proceedings, though many local languages remain active.

The convention’s most popular proposal is not administrative.

It is aspirational:

> Mars should cease thinking only about surviving within shelters and begin preparing to make the planet itself a home.

---

## 117,942 — The Martian Dream emerges

A young delegation from restored settlements introduces the **Open Mars Declaration**.

It calls for a multi-millennial project to make Mars increasingly hospitable.

The objectives include:

- thickening the atmosphere where physically possible;
- expanding liquid-water regions;
- spreading microbial and plant life;
- building large connected valleys;
- improving radiation protection;
- eventually enabling limited outdoor human activity;
- reducing dependence on sealed settlements.

Delegates know Mars cannot be fully terraformed with current technology or IRF levels.

The declaration is deliberately long-term.

Its language becomes famous:

> “Our ancestors survived Mars as it was. We will build Mars as it may become.”

The dream of transforming Mars becomes the emotional centre of the unity movement.

---

## 117,944 — Rome’s public reacts to Olympus

Roman media follows the convention closely.

The World Union movement seizes upon it.

Its leaders argue that Mars, with only a few million inhabitants and far weaker technology, is creating a planetary government before Terra.

The slogan **“Mars is one; why is Terra many?”** appears across Roman cities.

Opponents reply that Mars has fewer settlements, common environmental threats and no rival state comparable to Rome.

The comparison nevertheless shifts public opinion.

Support for eventual Terran political unity rises sharply.

---

## 117,946 — The first constitutional draft

The Martian convention produces a proposed confederal constitution.

It creates:

- a planetary assembly;
- a council of settlements;
- an elected executive directorate;
- a supreme ecological court;
- a planetary infrastructure authority;
- a common archive;
- a shared external-relations office.

Settlements retain control over:

- local law;
- education;
- culture;
- ordinary policing;
- internal resource distribution.

The confederal government controls:

- planetary water networks;
- biological reserves;
- external diplomacy;
- settlement rescue obligations;
- major terraforming projects;
- inter-settlement disputes.

---

## 117,948 — The Red Frontier dispute

Several older settlements oppose the constitution because the proposed terraforming authority could redirect resources toward uninhabited regions.

They argue that current citizens should not sacrifice living standards for projects benefiting distant descendants.

Restored settlements support the plan, seeing expansion as essential to their future.

The dispute nearly collapses the convention.

A compromise creates the **Generational Mars Fund**.

Only a fixed share of Martian production may be devoted to terraforming until basic settlement welfare targets are met.

This binds the planetary dream to present-day living standards.

## 117,951 — The Moon’s first interprovincial food failure

A genetically uniform Lunar grain strain becomes vulnerable to a fungal mutation.

Three provinces depend heavily on it.

Food production falls sharply.

No famine occurs because Terra can supply emergency food, but transport capacity becomes strained.

The crisis demonstrates that Luna’s growing civilian population has outpaced agricultural diversity.

The Lunar Provincial Council creates:

- mandatory crop-diversity requirements;
- isolated seed reserves;
- Martian and Titanian fungal exchanges;
- separate emergency food systems.

The event strengthens Luna’s argument for more control over its own civilian planning.

---

## 117,954 — The Blue Vent settlement begins

Europa’s twenty-year observation period ends.

The Oceanic Assembly approves controlled settlement of the Blue Vent region.

Initial population is limited to 12,000.

Strict rules require:

- independent life-support pockets;
- biological quarantine;
- slow species introduction;
- no single continuous food network;
- evacuation capacity.

The region’s chemical richness supports rapid food-web growth.

By the century’s end, Blue Vent becomes Europa’s fastest-growing settlement zone.

---

## 117,956 — Rome’s Conditional Innovation Charter

After Orassia’s medical successes and earlier blackout, Rome codifies a permanent compromise between caution and innovation.

The Charter permits higher-risk conditional technology in designated sectors if:

- Class100 fallback exists;
- failure boundaries are local;
- users understand the risk;
- systems cannot control essential public infrastructure alone;
- blueprints are archived;
- emergency shutdown is mechanical.

This accelerates Roman computing, medicine and spacecraft guidance.

Critics call it a retreat from continuity principles.

Supporters argue it preserves them while allowing progress.

---

## 117,958 — The Fleeing Ship finds a candidate system

The ship’s long-range observatories identify a nearby star system with:

- one potentially habitable planet;
- several large moons;
- metal-rich asteroids;
- accessible volatiles.

The system lies eleven years away at reduced cruise speed.

Data is incomplete.

The candidate planet may have:

- acceptable gravity;
- liquid water;
- an atmosphere requiring modification;
- no detectable complex life.

The Harbour Plan gains momentum.

The government agrees to send advance probes.

The ship changes course slightly but does not commit to settlement.

---

## 117,960 — Treaty of Olympus Mons

On **4 May 117,960**, representatives from sixty-eight Martian settlements sign the **Treaty of Olympus Mons**.

The treaty establishes the **Martian Confederacy**.

For the first time, Mars has one recognised planetary government.

The Confederacy declares all Martians citizens of one nation while preserving settlement autonomy.

Its founding principles include:

1. survival resources may not be monopolised;
2. every settlement owes rescue to every other;
3. Mars must maintain relations with all human worlds;
4. ecological failure anywhere is a planetary concern;
5. the long-term purpose of the Confederacy is to make Mars hospitable.

The fifth principle becomes the heart of Martian nationalism.

---

## 117,961 — Martian national symbolism

The Confederacy adopts:

- a planetary flag representing enclosed life opening toward the surface;
- Permanent Latin as the confederal administrative and external language;
- protected status for all local Martian languages;
- Olympus Mons as the ceremonial capital;
- a rotating executive seat among major settlements.

Martian children begin learning that they belong not merely to a cavern or valley, but to Mars.

The old survival identity evolves into a constructive one.

Being Martian increasingly means participating in the creation of a future open world.

---

## 117,963 — The First Martian Plan

The Confederacy issues a 500-year preliminary development programme.

Current technology cannot terraform Mars, but it can prepare.

Projects include:

- mapping atmospheric-loss mechanisms;
- expanding microbial surface zones;
- creating protected open-air plant trials under thin covers;
- reconnecting water networks;
- building regional greenhouse valleys;
- developing pressure-tolerant crops;
- studying low-energy atmospheric release;
- preserving every viable source of carbon, nitrogen and water.

The programme avoids promises of full breathability.

Its first objective is more modest:

> Increase the portion of Mars where humans can survive with less machinery than before.

---

## 117,965 — Terra’s World Government Marches

The creation of the Martian Confederacy triggers vast demonstrations on Terra.

Millions march peacefully in Roman cities.

The World Union movement demands:

- formal accession negotiations with every remaining state;
- a planetary constitutional convention;
- one Terra-wide environmental and defence authority;
- the eventual transformation of the Roman Republic into the government of all Terra.

Rome’s government refuses immediate action.

It argues that planetary unity must come through consent.

However, the National Assembly creates a commission to study possible constitutional structures for a fully unified Terra.

This is the movement’s greatest institutional victory to date.

---

## 117,967 — Serevan Cooperative Union crisis

One Serevan member state experiences a banking collapse and requests Roman emergency assistance.

Rome offers aid under strict transparency requirements.

The state accepts.

Its dependence on Roman institutions becomes nearly total.

Another Serevan state accuses it of preparing for accession.

The Union’s remaining cohesion weakens.

No formal breakup occurs yet.

---

## 117,969 — The Imbrium Dangerous Vault debate

Custodian Nine reveals that one sealed vault contains manufacturing systems capable of producing advanced electronics near the current lunar IRF threshold.

The systems could accelerate:

- computing;
- guidance;
- medicine;
- relay communication.

The same vault also contains early Resonance Lance components.

Opening it risks uncontrolled access to both.

The Senate authorises a limited robotic entry under multinational observation.

The robots recover technical records but leave physical resonance components sealed.

The decision is widely praised as a successful balance between recovery and caution.

---

## 117,972 — The Titan Commonwealth succeeds

The Orenth-Marellon Expansion Ring completes its first full circuit.

The Commonwealth population reaches **4.1 million**, significantly more than the two caverns could have supported independently under their former systems.

Benefits include:

- greater food diversity;
- lower heat waste;
- improved medical access;
- larger educational institutions;
- specialised manufacturing;
- stronger archives.

The integrated area remains divided into sealable ecological sectors.

Other Titanian caverns begin considering similar mergers.

A new political debate emerges over whether Titan should form larger regional states—or eventually a planetary federation like Mars.

---

## 117,974 — Terra Restituta’s first inland-sea reversal

Rome completes the drainage of part of a shallow post-impact inland sea.

The reclaimed area exposes:

- fertile sediment;
- ruins of two Long Age towns;
- an ancient forest soil horizon;
- large deposits of trapped methane.

A methane release ignites during excavation.

The fire burns for nine months and forces evacuation of the new settlements.

The project is not abandoned, but geological-gas mapping becomes mandatory before future reclamation.

The accident kills 22,000 people and becomes one of Terra Restituta’s worst disasters.

---

## 117,976 — The Roman Public Priority Act

Political backlash against expensive great projects grows.

The Senate passes a law requiring every major long-term programme to publish measurable near-term benefits for current citizens.

Projects must now show how they contribute within fifty years to at least one of:

- employment;
- housing;
- public health;
- environmental safety;
- education;
- infrastructure;
- disaster resilience.

The law does not cancel multi-millennial planning.

It ties it more directly to present public welfare.

The Horizon Project rejection is now recognised as the beginning of this shift.

---

## 117,978 — Novaterra’s Green Border crisis

Two Novaterran states dispute a forest corridor that has matured enough to support permanent agriculture.

Settlers from both sides move into the region.

Local militias form.

The Novaterran Concord declares the corridor a shared ecological reserve.

Neither state initially accepts the ruling.

A limited armed clash kills several thousand people.

The Concord threatens to cut both states off from seed banks and atmospheric-management data.

Both back down.

The incident strengthens the Concord’s authority but also makes it increasingly resemble a planetary government.

Novaterra’s states begin debating whether ecological coordination can remain separate from political sovereignty.

---

## 117,980 — The Fleeing Ship advance probes arrive

The first probes enter the candidate system.

They confirm:

- abundant asteroid metals;
- accessible water ice;
- one moon suitable for temporary industrial bases;
- a potentially habitable planet with a thin but modifiable atmosphere;
- no detectable technological civilisation;
- no obvious complex biosphere.

The planet is not immediately livable.

The system is excellent for repairs and expansion.

The Harbour Plan becomes the most popular proposal aboard the ship.

The government decides to enter the system and suspend long-range flight temporarily.

This is the first planned stop in thousands of years.

---

## 117,982 — The Harbour Accord

The ship’s political factions reach a compromise.

The **Harbour Accord** provides that:

- the ship will enter the candidate system;
- temporary resource settlements will be built;
- missing components will be manufactured;
- new habitat sections will be constructed;
- smaller vessels may be built;
- no decision on permanent settlement or return will be made for at least fifty years;
- reproduction limits will remain until capacity expands.

The Accord reduces unrest.

It does not eliminate fear.

The ship’s population has learned that its world can fail.

---

## 117,984 — First Martian open-valley experiment

The Martian Confederacy begins its first confederal terraforming experiment.

A small valley is enclosed with lightweight transparent roofing and connected to geothermal heat.

Unlike older sealed cities, the project aims to use:

- partially natural sunlight;
- local soil;
- pressure-tolerant vegetation;
- minimal mechanical circulation.

The first plantings fail because of unexpected soil toxicity.

A second planting using treated microbial soil survives.

The experiment remains tiny, but it becomes a powerful Martian symbol.

Schoolchildren follow its progress through public broadcasts.

## 117,986 — The Terran Unity Commission reports

The Roman commission studying world government concludes that full political unification is legally possible but politically premature.

It identifies three models.

### Universal Roman accession

Every remaining state joins as provinces.

### Terran federation above Rome

Rome and independent states create a new planetary layer while preserving their governments.

### Functional world government

Planetary authority is limited initially to defence, environment, space and interworld relations.

The commission recommends the third model as an intermediate step.

The World Union movement rejects this as insufficient.

Independent states cautiously express interest.

---

## 117,988 — The Planetary Functions Conference

Rome invites the remaining independent states to negotiate shared authority over:

- asteroid defence;
- orbital traffic;
- Terra Restituta effects;
- interworld diplomacy;
- pandemic response;
- artificial IRF experiments.

The conference does not create a world government.

It establishes permanent committees with binding technical authority.

For the first time, every remaining Terran state accepts some compulsory planetary rules.

Political unity remains incomplete.

Functional unity deepens.

---

## 117,990 — Mars’s first confederal election

All Martian citizens participate in the first confederal elections.

Voting occurs over months due to communication delays and settlement isolation.

The winning coalition combines:

- cautious terraforming advocates;
- settlement autonomy supporters;
- Class100 engineers;
- younger restored-settlement voters.

The Confederacy’s first elected government commits to:

- no reduction in survival reserves;
- steady investment in planetary transformation;
- universal Martian education;
- inter-settlement transport;
- expanded relations with Rome, Europa and Titan.

Martian voter turnout exceeds 88%.

The election becomes a source of enormous civic pride.

---

## 117,992 — Luna’s population stabilisation policy

Luna’s population reaches nearly **100 million**.

Habitat construction cannot safely keep pace with unrestricted growth and migration.

The Lunar Provincial Council requests temporary limits.

Rome adopts:

- slower immigration;
- priority for family reunification;
- stricter housing-capacity tests;
- incentives for longer intervals between births;
- expansion of rotating-gravity nurseries;
- more food-production districts.

The measures are controversial but non-coercive.

Lunar population growth slows.

The provinces remain prosperous.

---

## 117,994 — The Serevan environmental reckoning

Long-term contamination from continuous catalytic fabrication is found in major aquifers beneath two Serevan states.

Cleanup will require more than a century.

Rome offers technical aid but refuses to cover the full cost.

Public anger turns against former industrial leaders.

One state begins formal accession negotiations with Rome.

The other seeks a closer alliance with Orassia.

The Serevan Cooperative Union effectively ceases operating as a coherent political bloc, though it is not formally dissolved during this century.

---

## 117,996 — Titan’s federation proposal

Inspired by Mars, several Titanian cavern states propose a **Titanian Thermal Confederation**.

The Orenth-Marellon Commonwealth supports discussion but opposes rapid centralisation.

Titanian caution is much stronger than Martian optimism.

A constitutional congress is scheduled for the next century.

The proposed federation would coordinate:

- external communication;
- heat emergencies;
- biological quarantine;
- Class100 standards;
- shared archives.

It would not initially control ordinary cavern government.

---

## 117,998 — The Ship enters Harbour System

The Fleeing Ship decelerates into the candidate system.

For the first time in many millennia, it enters orbit around a star with the intention of remaining for decades.

Automated craft begin mining:

- metals;
- water;
- carbon compounds;
- rare industrial elements.

A temporary settlement is established on an airless moon.

The ship’s population watches unfiltered starlight from a stationary sky for the first time in recorded cultural memory.

The psychological effect is profound.

Some citizens immediately call the system home.

Others insist it is only a repair yard.

# State of each human society in 118,000

## Terra

- Population: approximately 17.5–18.5 billion
- Roman share: 96–97%
- Sovereign states: 9–10, depending on recognition of Serevan successor arrangements
- Life expectancy: 105–116 years in prosperous Roman provinces; 90–106 in poorer areas
- Living standards: high, with universal healthcare, civic income floor, reliable infrastructure and advanced education
- Main political issue: whether functional planetary unity should become formal world government
- Main risk: institutional overstretch and conflict between long-term projects and present needs

Rome remains democratic and powerful, but it has learned that not every great idea should become a project immediately.

---

## Luna

- Population: approximately 95–110 million
- IRF: 54–58%
- Life expectancy: 92–105 years
- Living standards: high but capacity-constrained
- Political status: several full Roman provinces with strong shared Lunar institutions
- Main risk: food, housing and transport capacity lagging behind population growth

Luna is no longer an outpost. It is a major Roman region with a native-born population and distinct political voice.

---

## Mars

- Population: approximately 5.0–6.2 million
- IRF: 70–76%
- Life expectancy: 84–99 years
- Government: the Martian Confederacy
- Living standards: secure but austere; improving through restoration and shared infrastructure
- National dream: making Mars progressively hospitable
- Main risk: diverting too many resources from present survival toward future terraforming

Mars becomes the first human planet of the Dark Age to unite under one planetary government.

Its unity is not based primarily on conquest, ethnicity or language.

It is based on a shared project: transforming the world that nearly killed them into a world their descendants may one day inhabit more freely.

---

## Europa

- Population: approximately 650,000–1 million
- IRF: 92–98%
- Life expectancy: approximately 72–92 years
- Government: Oceanic Assembly with autonomous communities
- Living standards: stable but geologically vulnerable
- Major development: controlled settlement of Blue Vent
- Main risk: isolation following ice shifts or vent failure

Europa remains the least politically centralised major human society.

---

## Titan

- Population: approximately 60–75 million
- IRF: effectively 100%
- Life expectancy: 88–106 years
- Living standards: stable, highly communal and thermally secure
- Major development: Orenth-Marellon merger proves carefully designed integration can support greater population
- Political future: debate over a Titanian confederation
- Main risk: interconnected infrastructure spreading ecological failures

Titan remains the leading full-IRF civilisation and one of humanity’s most stable societies.

---

## Novaterra

- Population: approximately 600–760 million
- IRF: 100%
- Sovereign states: roughly 20–22
- Life expectancy: 72–90 years
- Mature green land: approximately 45–52%
- Living standards: stable in old ecological zones, harsher on new frontiers
- Main political issue: whether the Novaterran Concord is becoming a world government through ecological necessity
- Main risk: interstate conflict over newly habitable land

Novaterra is becoming greener, more populous and more politically complicated.

---

## The Fleeing Ship and Harbour System

- Population: approximately 2.4–2.7 million after reproduction restrictions
- IRF: 0%
- Life expectancy: 105–125 years
- Living standards: technologically advanced but crowded and politically tense
- Condition: major systems repaired but permanently reduced in capacity
- Current strategy: temporary settlement, resource extraction and industrial rebuilding
- Main political question: continue fleeing, settle, divide, or eventually investigate the weakening IRF

The ship has survived its first true structural population crisis.

It has also stopped fleeing continuously for the first time.

That pause may prove more historically important than any repair.

Advancing cautiously. Ecological consequences are now subject to international review.

Continuing, with greater emphasis on citizen-accessible benefits and practical recovery knowledge.

Still incomplete and dispersed, but more resilient after redesign.

Functioning as a genuine interworld institution.

Widely credited with preventing disasters on Mars and Titan.

Expanding under controlled rules, accelerating medicine and computation.

Stronger than ever after Martian unification, but Rome still refuses coercive planetary union.

Only beginning, but already central to Martian national identity.

Successful at regional scale, with planetary confederation under discussion.

## Fleeing Ship repair programme

Underway in Harbour System; new components and possibly new ships are now technically feasible.

# The century’s meaning

This century does not produce a single universal triumph.

It produces decisions.

Rome chooses its citizens over another grand project.

Mars chooses unity and a future beyond sealed survival.

Titan chooses integration without surrendering isolation safeguards.

Europa chooses slow expansion over reckless opportunity.

The Fleeing Ship chooses to stop—temporarily—and reconsider a mission that had become indistinguishable from destiny.

Humanity is still in the Dark Age.

The IRF still divides worlds by physical capability.

But increasingly, the defining limitations are no longer imposed only by the field.

They are choices about scale, risk, identity and purpose.

By **118,000**, humanity is no longer merely trying to survive what happened after the Second Breaking.

Every surviving branch has begun deciding what it wants the end of the Dark Age to reveal.

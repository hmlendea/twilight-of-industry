# 40000 — Time Almost Loses Its Meaning

By **year 40000**, another **30,000 years** have passed since the preceding period at **10000**.

For perspective:

- We are only about 12,000 years removed from the beginning of agriculture.
- Thirty thousand years is longer than almost all of recorded human civilization.

Yet in this world, because there was **never an industrial or equivalent revolution**, the changes between **10000 and 40000** would likely be remarkably modest.

In fact, a person from year 10000 might experience year 40000 as markedly different in detail yet recognisably the same civilisation in structure.

| Aspect | Year 10000 | Year 40000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population | ~1–2 billion | ~1–2 billion |
| Energy | Local electricity, water, wind, muscle | Slightly more efficient versions of the same |
| Transport | Horses, sailing, canals, bicycles | Faster and more refined, same principles |
| Cities | Ancient | Almost unimaginably ancient |
| Science | Nearly complete without advanced instruments | Essentially complete within those limits |
| Computing | Large electromechanical systems | Extremely advanced electromechanical systems |
| Daily life | Comfortable, manual | Very similar |

---

# Time almost loses its meaning

By year 40000...

Some cities have existed continuously for **35,000 years**.

Consider walking through a district where every generation has added a tiny improvement.

Nothing was demolished.

Everything was refined.

The city itself becomes humanity's longest-running project.

---

# Architecture

Buildings become almost geological.

Not because one building survives 30,000 years untouched.

Rather:

A bridge built in year 2500 has had:

- millions of repairs
- thousands of upgrades
- hundreds of redesigns

Yet it is still considered the same bridge.

Some public buildings may have effectively evolved for longer than entire civilisational cycles in the pre-Impact baseline.

---

# Science

Compared to year 10000...

Very little changes.

Not because scientists stop working.

Because nearly every experiment that can be performed with available tools has already been performed.

Research shifts toward:

- mathematics
- philosophy
- logic
- history
- linguistics

These fields have no obvious technological ceiling.

---

# Mathematics

This may become almost incomprehensible to us.

Consider:

30,000 additional years of uninterrupted mathematical development.

They may possess entire branches of mathematics beyond anything we currently imagine.

Ironically...

Many of these theories may never find practical application because industry never develops.

---

# History becomes the greatest science

With tens of thousands of years of continuous records...

Historians can study:

- entire civilizations
- climate cycles
- political systems
- demographic changes

using datasets spanning millennia.

Their understanding of long-term human behavior could be astonishing.

---

# Electricity

Compared to year 10000:

Electricity is now nearly universal.

Every town probably has:

- local generators
- batteries
- electric lighting
- communications
- electric pumps

However:

Power remains local.

Without giant industrial grids and mass-produced infrastructure, electricity spreads as countless small, decentralized systems rather than continent-spanning networks.

---

# Computing

This is where the largest difference appears.

Consider giving engineers **30,000 years** to improve relays, gears, cams, precision machining, and electromagnetic switches.

They might build machines unlike anything in our history.

Not electronic...

...but still extraordinary.

Potentially:

- room-sized reasoning engines
- automatic libraries
- navigation computers
- translation machines
- weather prediction systems

They would probably occupy buildings.

Require constant maintenance.

Consume enormous amounts of precision engineering.

Yet perform tasks we normally associate with computers.

---

# Communication

A fascinating possibility.

Without electronics...

They may create something resembling an internet using:

- relay stations
- electromechanical routing
- printed message buffering
- optical transmission
- telegraph infrastructure

Messages could cross continents in minutes or hours.

Knowledge could still circulate globally.

It would simply operate through entirely different machinery.

---

# Medicine

Compared with year 10000:

Almost unchanged.

Most remaining breakthroughs require:

- molecular manufacturing
- advanced chemistry
- biotechnology

Still impossible.

Average lifespan remains around:

75–85 years.

Human biology itself becomes the limiting factor.

---

# Culture

Culture becomes unimaginably deep.

A musical tradition may have existed continuously for:

25,000 years.

Languages evolve into enormous families whose histories are completely documented.

Religions may have accumulated tens of thousands of years of theology and commentary.

Some annual festivals are older than agriculture in the pre-Impact baseline.

---

# Nature

Earth may now be one of the healthiest inhabited planets imaginable.

Forests have regenerated for tens of millennia.

Large mammals thrive.

Entire ecosystems are actively stewarded rather than exploited.

Climate is managed mainly through land use rather than technology.

---

# Innovation

Compared with year 10000...

Innovation becomes almost microscopic.

A celebrated invention might be:

"A water turbine that is 0.3% more efficient."

Because nearly everything else has already been perfected.

---

# If someone from year 10000 visited year 40000

They would certainly notice improvements.

The cities are more polished.

The institutions are older.

The mechanical systems are more elegant.

The libraries are larger.

The electrical infrastructure is more reliable.

But after a few months...

They would probably feel at home.

The worldview, daily routines, technologies, and social structures would be fundamentally familiar.

---

## The greatest achievement

By year 40000, the most remarkable thing would not be any single invention.

It would be **continuity**.

No civilization in our history has remained coherent for even a tiny fraction of that span. In this alternate world, humanity's greatest accomplishment would be preserving and steadily improving a complex global civilization for thirty thousand years without collapsing or transforming beyond recognition.

Observers from high-volatility industrial cultures might conclude that this civilisation had "stagnated." In turn, this civilisation might assess rapid-cycle industrial history as powerful but unstable, and therefore less durable across deep time.

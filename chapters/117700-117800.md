# 117700-117800 — The Century of Reclamation

## 117,702 — The Terra Restituta proposal

Roman geographers, ecologists and historians publish a vast report titled **Terra Restituta**.

It argues that restoration should no longer be limited to individual species, monuments or polluted regions.

The Republic should attempt, over many thousands of years, to restore Terra’s geography and ecology as closely as physically possible to its condition immediately before the First Breaking, at the end of the Long Age.

The proposal includes:

- reclaiming selected land lost to the sea;
- rebuilding damaged wetlands and river systems;
- restoring old coastlines where feasible;
- reshaping areas deformed by the Second Breaking;
- re-establishing ecological corridors;
- recovering submerged settlements;
- reversing avoidable post-impact erosion;
- reintroducing Long Age land-management practices;
- recreating some lost forests, grasslands and coastal ecosystems.

Its authors admit that a perfect restoration is impossible.

The asteroid impacts altered:

- ocean basins;
- tectonic structures;
- climate patterns;
- coastlines;
- species relationships;
- river systems.

The project’s objective is therefore defined as:

> The closest stable approximation of late Long Age Terra that modern geography can sustain.

---

## 117,704 — The Planetary Reclamation Authority

The Senate establishes the **Planetary Reclamation Authority**, commonly called the PRA.

Its first duties are not construction, but mapping and planning.

The PRA begins assembling:

- ancient coastline maps;
- sediment records;
- pre-impact river systems;
- submerged-city locations;
- historical climate data;
- soil records;
- Long Age ecological surveys;
- Industrial Age satellite imagery;
- Second Breaking impact models.

The project is expected to last longer than the restored Roman Republic has existed.

Some senators object that the programme cannot possibly be completed under any foreseeable government.

Supporters answer that this is precisely why it must begin early.

---

## 117,706–117,714 — Mapping the drowned world

Roman survey fleets begin exploring submerged regions.

They use:

- sonar;
- low-energy submersibles;
- tethered observation craft;
- mechanical dredging instruments;
- recovered Industrial Age seabed maps.

Thousands of drowned settlements are identified.

They include cities from:
- The First Age;
- The Long Age;
- The Industrial Age;
- The first centuries after the Second Breaking.

Most cannot be recovered.

Many lie beneath:

- deep sediment;
- unstable seabeds;
- chemically contaminated layers;
- collapsed coastal shelves.

A smaller number lie in shallow seas and protected bays.

These become future restoration candidates.

---

## 117,711 — The first reclamation district

The first major land-reclamation experiment begins in a shallow inland sea created by post-impact flooding.

The region is selected because:

- it is geologically stable;
- water depth is modest;
- ancient river systems can be redirected;
- nearby Roman provinces need agricultural land;
- several submerged Long Age settlements lie beneath the seabed.

Engineers construct:

- layered sea walls;
- tidal barriers;
- sediment islands;
- pumping stations;
- gravity-fed drainage channels;
- artificial wetlands.

Class100-compatible backup systems are included.

The project is designed so that even if advanced pumps fail, parts of the reclaimed land will flood slowly rather than catastrophically.

---

## 117,715 — The Long Stewardship Commission

The PRA creates the **Long Stewardship Commission** to reconstruct Long Age ecological management.

Archivists discover that late Long Age civilisation did not preserve wilderness by leaving all landscapes untouched.

It used:

- rotational forestry;
- controlled burns;
- managed grazing;
- river maintenance;
- migration corridors;
- soil-restoration cycles;
- seasonal hunting limits;
- local ecological councils;
- extremely long land-use plans.

Some forests regarded as “natural” during later ages were actually the product of thousands of years of human stewardship.

Rome begins adapting these practices to modern conditions.

This produces conflict between two environmental traditions:

- strict preservationists, who want minimal human intervention;
- stewardship advocates, who argue that carefully managed ecosystems can be more resilient.

The Republic adopts a mixed policy.

---

## 117,718 — The first submerged city expedition

Roman divers enter the upper levels of a drowned Long Age city beneath a shallow coastal basin.

The city’s name remains uncertain.

Its upper structures have collapsed, but lower districts contain:

- stone roads;
- sealed archives;
- hydraulic tunnels;
- workshops;
- public cisterns;
- carved ecological records.

The most important discovery is a municipal planning archive covering more than nine hundred years of water and forest management.

The city had survived repeated climate changes through slow adjustment rather than rapid rebuilding.

Its methods become part of the Long Stewardship curriculum.

---

## 117,721 — The Atlantic League’s final crisis

The Atlantic League enters a deep constitutional crisis.

Its three remaining member states disagree over:

- financial regulation;
- Serevan imports;
- Roman trade;
- naval funding;
- the Associated Partnership with the Serevan Compact.

One League government argues for closer Roman alignment.

Another wants full economic integration with Sereva.

The third seeks neutrality.

The League’s shared institutions can no longer enforce common policy.

Shipping companies increasingly register in Roman ports.

League banks relocate operations to Roman cities.

The League still exists legally, but its common institutions are becoming ceremonial.

---

## 117,723 — A surprise from Habitat Aurelia

During restoration work aboard Habitat Aurelia, Roman engineers discover a sealed Industrial Age medical section.

Inside are preserved tissue records and partial genomic maps belonging to populations that died during the orbital collapse.

The discovery includes several genetic lineages believed lost.

However, the chamber also contains dormant engineered microorganisms.

One sample escapes containment inside the habitat laboratory.

Aurelia is quarantined for eleven months.

No pathogen reaches Terra.

Three researchers die.

The event leads to stricter biological-archive protocols across Luna and orbital installations.

---

## 117,725 — The Reclamation Referendum

The Planetary Reclamation Authority requests long-term constitutional protection.

Its leaders fear that future governments might cancel projects whose benefits will not appear for centuries.

A national referendum approves a constitutional amendment requiring every Roman government to maintain minimum funding for:

- planetary reclamation;
- Great Libraries;
- Class100 research;
- asteroid defence;
- biosphere restoration.

The amendment does not guarantee unlimited spending.

It prevents complete abandonment.

Critics call it government by the unborn.

Supporters call it intergenerational responsibility.

---

## 117,728 — The Atlantic League dissolves

On **18 March 117,728**, the Atlantic League formally dissolves.

Its final assembly votes to terminate:

- common naval command;
- shared financial councils;
- mutual commercial obligations;
- League citizenship arrangements.

The three former member states become independent.

Rome recognises them immediately.

The Serevan Compact offers membership to all three.

Only one accepts association talks.

The other two fear becoming economically subordinate to Serevan manufacturing.

The end of the League is peaceful and almost anticlimactic.

A political system that once rivalled Rome disappears through slow economic exhaustion rather than war.

---

## 117,731 — Roman accession resumes

Two poor independent states apply to join Rome.

Both face:

- declining agriculture;
- infrastructure failure;
- population loss;
- dependency on Roman medicine;
- heavy Serevan debt.

After referenda, both are admitted as Roman provinces.

They receive:

- civic-income coverage;
- infrastructure investment;
- debt restructuring;
- national healthcare;
- Class100 water systems.

By **117,732**, Terra contains **14 sovereign states**.

Rome governs roughly **91–92% of Terra’s population**.

---

## 117,734 — The Serevan Certification Crisis

A new strain in Roman–Serevan relations begins when Roman authorities reject a widely used Serevan agricultural machine.

The machine is cheap and productive but has:

- a short service life;
- no standard replacement parts;
- high accident rates;
- no manual fallback;
- sealed proprietary mechanisms.

Serevan governments accuse Rome of excluding foreign goods for political reasons.

The Compact retaliates by refusing Roman inspectors access to its manufacturing districts.

Rome suspends certification for several Serevan firms.

Trade contracts sharply.

---

## 117,736 — The Grey Market explosion

Smugglers begin importing uncertified Serevan goods through associated states.

A black market develops in:

- cheap vehicles;
- household machines;
- uncertified medicines;
- disposable agricultural tools.

In **117,736**, a counterfeit power regulator causes a fire in a Roman residential district.

More than 4,000 people die.

The regulator carries forged Roman certification.

The incident reawakens memories of the earlier sabotage campaign.

Rome accuses Serevan authorities of tolerating criminal exports.

The Compact denies responsibility.

---

## 117,739 — A Serevan member seeks Roman accession

The Serevan member state of **Velathia** announces that it will hold a referendum on leaving the Compact and applying for Roman accession.

Velathia is strategically important.

It contains:

- major rail corridors;
- advanced manufacturing districts;
- mineral deposits;
- several Serevan financial institutions;
- approximately 120 million people.

Its economy has suffered from Serevan overproduction and exclusion from Roman markets.

Velathian leaders argue that Roman membership offers:

- market access;
- social stability;
- safer industry;
- higher living standards;
- long-term investment.

The Serevan Compact declares the referendum illegal.

---

## 117,740 — Pressure from the World Union movement

The Roman World Union movement launches a massive campaign demanding that Rome accept Velathia.

Its leaders include:

- retired generals;
- industrial federations;
- planetary-defence officials;
- influential provincial governors.

Their argument is strategic:

- Velathia’s accession would weaken Sereva;
- it would break key Compact transport routes;
- it would remove a major source of industrial sabotage;
- it would bring millions under Roman safety law;
- it would accelerate eventual planetary unity.

Pacifists warn that accepting Velathia could destabilise the Compact and provoke conflict.

Roman public opinion strongly favours acceptance.

The legacy of Serevan sabotage makes restraint politically difficult.

---

## 117,741 — The Velathian referendum

Despite Compact pressure, Velathia holds the referendum under international observation.

Result:

- 68% support leaving the Serevan Compact;
- 61% support Roman accession;
- turnout exceeds 80%.

The Compact refuses to recognise the vote.

Serevan forces close several borders and occupy a joint railway district.

Velathian security units mobilise.

For several weeks, war appears possible.

---

## 117,742 — Rome accepts Velathia

Rome reluctantly approves Velathian accession.

The Senate’s declaration openly acknowledges the risks.

It argues that refusing a legitimate democratic request because of foreign pressure would contradict Roman citizenship principles.

Roman troops do not cross into Velathia immediately.

Instead, Rome deploys:

- legal observers;
- food convoys;
- medical teams;
- communications support.

The Serevan Compact imposes sanctions and expels Velathian officials.

Several Compact members privately criticise the leadership for allowing the crisis to escalate.

---

## 117,743 — The railway standoff

A Serevan military force occupies the **Sathren Junction**, a railway complex legally divided between Velathia and another Compact member.

Velathian forces surround it.

Rome warns that any attack on Velathian territory will now be treated as an attack on the Republic.

The World Union movement demands immediate annexation of the entire Compact.

The Roman government refuses.

After a six-week standoff, the Serevan force withdraws in exchange for:

- temporary shared rail access;
- compensation;
- guarantees against immediate Roman military construction.

No shots are fired.

The crisis deeply weakens the Compact.

---

## 117,745 — The Serevan split

Two smaller Serevan members suspend participation in Compact military institutions.

They do not leave immediately.

They fear being trapped in a confrontation caused by the Compact’s largest manufacturing states.

The Serevan economy suffers:

- capital flight;
- interrupted rail links;
- loss of Velathian factories;
- investor panic;
- reduced Roman access.

The Compact survives but becomes less cohesive.

---

## 117,748 — Velathia becomes Roman territory

Velathia is reorganised into three Roman provinces.

Latin Nova becomes the national administrative language.

One regional language is selected as the secondary official language in each province.

Roman regulators immediately inspect industrial facilities.

Thousands are temporarily closed.

Unemployment rises sharply.

The Roman government provides emergency civic-income support and reconstruction grants.

By the end of the century, Velathian industry is smaller but safer, more durable and increasingly integrated into Roman supply chains.

---

## 117,750 — Terra’s state

At mid-century:

- Terra’s population reaches roughly 11.0–11.7 billion;
- Rome governs approximately 10.2–10.8 billion;
- Rome controls about 80% of inhabited land;
- 13 sovereign states remain.

Living standards continue rising overall, although Velathia and the former League states experience uneven transitions.

The IRF on Terra averages approximately **53.6%**.

---

## 117,752 — The first reclaimed coastal district opens

The land-reclamation project begun in 117,711 completes its first inhabited district.

The reclaimed area includes:

- agricultural land;
- restored wetlands;
- flood-buffer zones;
- a reconstructed Long Age canal;
- archaeological preserves.

The project is considered a technical success.

However, it costs far more than expected.

Some environmental scientists argue that reclaiming more land may damage modern marine ecosystems that developed after the Second Breaking.

The PRA therefore adopts a principle:

> Restoration of the past must not casually destroy the life of the present.

Every reclamation project now requires evaluation of both historical value and current ecological importance.

---

## 117,755 — The Lost Coast dispute

A proposed reclamation zone contains both:

- a submerged First Age port;
- a thriving modern reef ecosystem.

Archaeologists want excavation.

Biosphere scientists want preservation.

The Senate initially authorises partial drainage.

Public opposition grows.

The project is halted after preliminary works damage part of the reef.

This becomes the first major political defeat for Terra Restituta.

The site is later studied through underwater archaeology rather than reclamation.

The programme learns that not every lost landscape should be physically restored.

---

## 117,757 — Mars reopens Argyre North

Mars successfully resettles the abandoned **Argyre North** complex.

Unlike earlier attempts, restoration is planned around:

- multiple independent geothermal sources;
- isolated crop systems;
- mechanical pressure controls;
- Class100 water circulation;
- strict biological quarantine.

The settlement begins with 6,000 residents.

By **117,800**, it supports nearly 40,000.

The success becomes a model for future Martian restoration.

Mars’s population rises steadily.

---

## 117,759 — The Martian Archive Surprise

During work in another abandoned settlement, Martians discover a late Industrial Age archive containing plans for a partially completed underground regional water network.

The network was believed never built.

Surveying reveals that roughly 40% of it actually exists.

Some sections remain sealed and usable.

Mars begins a century-long programme to reconnect them.

If successful, the network could support hundreds of thousands of additional people.

The discovery radically improves Mars’s long-term demographic outlook.

---

## 117,762 — The Europa Silence

All communication with Europa stops.

At first, Rome assumes relay failure.

Months pass.

No Europan signal is detected.

Possible explanations include:

- relay damage;
- ice movement;
- collapse of surface antennas;
- regional ecological disaster;
- political withdrawal from communication.

Rome cannot send a rescue mission.

The silence lasts almost four years.

Public anxiety grows.

Some fear Europan civilisation has collapsed.

---

## 117,766 — Europa returns

A weak signal reaches the Jovian relay.

The cause was not civilisational collapse.

A major ice-shelf movement destroyed several surface communication shafts and cut links between Europan regions.

Three communities were lost, killing perhaps 9,000–15,000 people.

The Oceanic Assembly spent years rebuilding contact routes.

The first recovered message contains not a distress appeal, but updated engineering plans for ice-shift-resistant antennas.

Europa’s survival strengthens the Class100 exchange.

Rome redesigns outer-system relays accordingly.

---

## 117,768 — Titan’s first major historical transmission

The Saturn relay receives a nearly continuous compressed historical archive from Titan.

The transmission has taken decades to assemble and send.

It includes:

- records of the retreat from Titan’s surface;
- founding charters of cavern republics;
- thermal-allocation laws;
- ecological collapse reports;
- oral histories;
- early Latin teaching material.

The archive reveals an unexpected fact:

Several Titanian societies had deliberately rejected evacuation opportunities during the early Closing Sky.

They believed attempting to flee would consume resources needed to build stable caverns.

Their decision, controversial at the time, probably saved millions.

Roman historians incorporate this into the History of Dependence curriculum.

---

## 117,770 — The Moon reaches ten million residents

Luna’s population passes **10 million**.

The three original provinces have expanded, and two additional provinces are created.

Luna now contains:

- five ordinary Roman provinces;
- several scientific territories;
- restricted archaeological zones;
- independent emergency reserves.

The Great Library remains under construction.

Only around **15%** of its planned structural volume exists.

Progress remains slow because every chamber requires extreme redundancy.

---

## 117,772 — The Lunar Constitutional Case

A Lunar provincial government challenges Rome’s power to impose migration limits.

It argues that full provincial equality should include unrestricted freedom of movement.

The Constitutional Court rules that Luna’s environmental carrying capacity permits regulated access.

However, it orders:

- transparent passenger allocation;
- expanded civilian transport;
- independent review of capacity limits;
- equal access regardless of provincial origin.

Critics still claim Lunar freedom exists only for those who obtain a state-controlled seat.

Supporters answer that physical scarcity cannot be legislated away.

---

## 117,775 — Concordia Orbitalis expands

Concordia Orbitalis adds:

- a larger rotating habitation ring;
- a planetary-defence command centre;
- a permanent Mars communications section;
- a Serevan observer module despite poor relations;
- a Titanian archive receiver.

Permanent crew reaches approximately **4,000**.

The station remains multinational, although Rome provides most funding and safety systems.

An unexpected problem emerges: residents begin developing a distinct orbital identity and demand stronger political representation.

Rome creates an elected station council but does not grant provincial status.

---

## 117,778 — Mnemosyne receives the Core Archive

The orbital knowledge vault Mnemosyne receives its first complete **Core Recovery Archive**.

It contains enough information to reconstruct:

- Permanent Latin;
- basic sanitation;
- agriculture;
- metallurgy;
- mechanical power;
- Class100 machinery;
- elementary medicine;
- Roman constitutional government;
- astronomy;
- printing;
- low-energy communication.

The archive deliberately excludes enormous quantities of specialised knowledge.

Its designers focus on what a devastated civilisation would need first.

The vault is not meant to preserve everything.

It is meant to make recovery possible.

---

## 117,780 — The Reclamation Corruption Scandal

Investigators discover that several officials in the Planetary Reclamation Authority have inflated project costs and purchased coastal land before announcing reclamation zones.

The scandal involves:

- provincial politicians;
- engineering firms;
- survey officials;
- land speculators.

Opponents claim Terra Restituta is becoming a vehicle for elite enrichment.

Rome prosecutes senior officials and places reclamation land under stricter public ownership rules.

The scandal delays major works for nearly six years.

The programme survives because its planning archives and scientific divisions remain credible.

---

## 117,783 — A new Novaterran conflict

On Novaterra, two newly formed states dispute control of a developing green corridor.

The corridor contains:

- the only mature river ecosystem connecting their regions;
- new agricultural land;
- migration routes;
- access to a shared aquifer.

A limited war begins.

Because Novaterra’s ecosystems are fragile, both sides avoid widespread destruction.

Even so, irrigation sabotage damages thousands of hectares.

The Novaterran Concord intervenes.

After eighteen months, it imposes a shared ecological administration.

The incident strengthens the Concord’s authority but alarms states that fear ecological law becoming political government.

---

## 117,785 — Novaterra’s changing map

By this year, Novaterra contains:

- 16 major sovereign states;
- several autonomous frontier regions;
- a stronger Novaterran Concord.

Population reaches approximately **260–340 million**.

Mature ecosystems cover roughly **30–35%** of the land.

Another 20–25% supports developing biological systems.

Large barren regions remain.

New countries continue emerging where newly green regions become capable of independent settlement.

---

## 117,787 — The colony ship’s probe turns back

One of the endless colony ship’s unmanned probes reaches a region of the IRF shell that is measurably thinner than predicted.

The probe confirms:

- the field is weakening behind the main frontier;
- pulse waves are stretching and losing intensity;
- the expansion is not uniform.

The colony ship’s scientists still cannot identify the Solar origin.

However, the discovery proves that endless acceleration away from the field may not be necessary forever.

The ship’s government authorises a second-generation probe mission deeper toward the shell.

The population remains on course.

A political divide develops between:

- the Flight faction, which wants permanent escape;
- the Return Inquiry, which wants eventual investigation;
- the Settlement faction, which wants to find a habitable world and stop.

This debate will last for generations.

---

## 117,790 — The Serevan Compact’s internal coup attempt

A coalition of industrial officials and military officers attempts to replace the civilian leadership of the largest Serevan state.

They argue that the Compact’s weakness resulted from:

- indecision;
- failure to confront Rome;
- loss of Velathia;
- regulatory concessions.

The coup fails after railway unions and provincial governments refuse cooperation.

The attempt reveals how fragile the Compact has become.

Rome denies involvement.

Evidence suggests Roman intelligence knew of the plot but did not create it.

The World Union movement criticises the government for not exploiting the crisis.

The Senate answers that destabilising Sereva could produce famine, refugees and war.

---

## 117,792 — The Serevan Reform Pact

The remaining Serevan states negotiate internal reforms.

They agree to:

- common product-safety standards;
- stronger anti-sabotage oversight;
- reduced military secrecy;
- partial harmonisation with Roman certification;
- compensation for earlier industrial incidents.

The reforms preserve the Compact temporarily.

Serevan goods become more durable and expensive.

The Compact loses part of the economic model that made it distinctive.

Relations with Rome improve slightly but remain distrustful.

---

## 117,794 — The first restored submerged district

Rome completes the partial restoration of a drowned Industrial Age coastal district.

Rather than draining the entire area, engineers construct:

- enclosed sea barriers;
- elevated walkways;
- underwater archaeological chambers;
- restored upper floors;
- tidal wetlands around preserved ruins.

A small permanent population moves in.

The district becomes part city, part archaeological site and part flood-control experiment.

It demonstrates that submerged urban restoration need not require fully reversing the sea.

---

## 117,796 — A major Class100 breakthrough

Roman, Martian and Titanian engineers certify a modular mechanical power system capable of operating reliably from current Terran intensity up to full 100% IRF.

The system uses:

- low-pressure thermal expansion;
- large flywheels;
- water-based heat storage;
- low-speed generators;
- mechanically governed load distribution.

It is less efficient and vastly larger than modern Roman power systems.

It can be built from common materials and repaired without precision electronics.

Rome begins installing versions in:

- Great Libraries;
- hospitals;
- Lunar emergency districts;
- Martian settlements;
- Concordia Orbitalis;
- planetary-defence observatories.

The design becomes one of the most important products of the Human Continuity Exchange.

---

## 117,798 — Species restoration crosses a threshold

Several restored species begin reproducing without direct human support across multiple regions.

Most notably:

- one large herbivore population expands beyond managed reserves;
- restored reef systems connect across protected coastal zones;
- a reconstructed migratory bird resumes a stable seasonal route;
- several lost soil organisms become widespread in reclaimed agricultural regions.

The Great Terran Restoration announces that these species are no longer merely maintained.

They are part of Terra again.

This gives political momentum to Terra Restituta.

| Other independent states | 4 | 2–3% |

- Population: 12.0–12.8 billion
- IRF: approximately 53.1%
- Condition: prosperous, highly regulated, ecologically ambitious
- Major development: Terra Restituta begins genuine land reclamation and submerged-city recovery
- Outlook: strong, but increasingly burdened by projects extending across millennia

Living standards remain high across Rome. Regional inequality persists but is far smaller than during the Unification War.

---

## Luna

- Population: approximately 13–16 million
- IRF: 57–61%
- Roman provinces: five
- Great Library: roughly 15% structurally complete
- Condition: growing civilian society, still dependent on Terra
- Outlook: stable if supply links continue

Luna has become an ordinary home for millions, though its environment remains unforgiving.

---

## Mars

- Population: approximately 1.0–1.3 million
- IRF: 74–80%
- Restored settlements: more than a dozen permanently reopened
- Condition: slow expansion, careful infrastructure recovery
- Outlook: increasingly positive

Mars remains physically isolated but is no longer demographically fragile.

---

## Europa

- Population: approximately 300,000–520,000
- IRF: 94–100%
- Condition: recovered from the ice-shift crisis
- Outlook: stable but always geologically vulnerable

The four-year silence demonstrates how easily Europa can disappear from wider human awareness without actually becoming extinct.

---

## Titan

- Population: approximately 31–41 million
- IRF: effectively 100%
- Condition: extremely stable underground civilisation
- Outlook: strong

Titan remains the most successful full-IRF human world and a principal source of Class100 technology.

---

## Novaterra

- Population: 260–340 million
- IRF: 100%
- States: 16 major sovereign countries
- Mature green land: approximately 30–35%
- Condition: politically more fragmented as its biosphere expands

Its greatest future danger is not the IRF, but political conflict over newly living land.

## The endless colony ship

- IRF: still outside the active frontier
- Condition: technologically advanced, socially divided over its purpose
- Major development: its probe confirms that the IRF shell is thinning
- Outlook: uncertain

For the first time, its inhabitants have evidence that the prison may not be permanent.

Hundreds mapped, a handful explored, one partially resettled.

# The century’s meaning

The century begins with Rome attempting to recover coastlines and drowned cities.

It ends with the Republic confronting a deeper question:

How much of the past should actually be restored?

Late Long Age Terra no longer exists. Modern oceans contain new ecosystems. Modern populations inhabit post-impact landscapes. New civilisations have grown around loss.

Terra Restituta therefore evolves from a project of reversal into a project of reconciliation.

Rome will reclaim land.

It will recover submerged cities.

It will rebuild rivers and forests.

But it will not simply erase everything that grew after catastrophe.

The Republic increasingly understands that permanent civilisation cannot survive by choosing one age and destroying the others.

Its task is to preserve all four:
- the monumentality of the First Age;
- the patience of the Long Age;
- the knowledge of the Industrial Age;
- and the resilience learned during the Dark Age.

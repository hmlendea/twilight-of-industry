## Evidence And Identity

- Read the preceding chapter and relevant earlier founding or capability passages, not only summaries. Treat review recommendations as proposals, not canonical events; verify findings against the current text.
- Maintain a working evidence ledger for changed missions, institutions, policies, and projects: canonical name, source passage, date, prior state, proposed change, and unresolved obligations. This is a drafting aid, not another permanent continuity document.
- Search established names and aliases prior to introducing an institution or declaring a first occurrence. Distinguish foundation, operational inauguration, jurisdictional extension, renaming, and succession; state the date, participants, mandate, and authority for material changes.
- Do not presume that a pending committee or agreement exists. Establish its necessary founding facts in the chapter, or identify a distinct existing body and mandate. Preserve historical names unless a dated transition is narrated.
- Opening conditions contain only facts established by the opening date. Distinguish an inherited dispute from renewed refusal or later escalation. Label retrospective accounts and multi-year summaries explicitly; do not conceal later events inside earlier sections.
- Describe future persistence, prevention, or success as an intention or projection unless later canonical evidence establishes it. Publication of safeguards does not demonstrate compliance.

## Mission Accounting

- For repeated missions, reconcile one chapter ledger with the preceding closing register: identifier, origin, destination, departure, arrival or loss, payload result, latest status, and qualification under applicable protocols.
- Distinguish scheduled, deferred, cancelled unflown, departed, arrived, partially delivered, lost, and returned missions. An identifier is not a departure count; an unflown delay is not a transit loss. Retrospective discussion does not increase cumulative totals.
- Recalculate cumulative attempts, complete and partial deliveries, losses, and qualifying consecutive successes from that ledger. Preserve unspecified dates as unspecified; never derive them from numbering. Record expected arrival windows for departed unresolved missions where established.
- Define protocol eligibility, success denominators, time windows, resets, and retrospective qualification. Inbound delivery does not demonstrate return ascent, reciprocal transit, or passenger repatriation; retain separate return-system requirements unless a dated, justified decision explicitly supersedes them.

## Policy And Project Continuity

- Compare later legislation and taxonomies with existing obligations. State whether each provision supplements, replaces, or exempts an earlier requirement, who authorises the change, and when it takes effect; do not silently weaken safeguards.
- Map overlapping cargo categories explicitly, including commercial cargo without industrial content. Specify handling, redundancy, precedence, tariff or exemption, and jurisdiction for every class; reconcile universal wording with exceptions.
- Distinguish commissioned capacity and audited delivery from authorisation, design, expenditure commitments, projections, and legal designation.
- Date each reused measurement and state its continuing validity or uncertainty. A closing report must provide representative dated implementation results or explicit non-completion, causes of delay, and remaining verification; repeating opening counts or design projections is insufficient.
- Track delayed repairs, curricula, appeals, prosecutions, relocations, and disputed implementation to a dated closing status or an explicit unresolved continuation. Do not fabricate completion totals to populate a report.
